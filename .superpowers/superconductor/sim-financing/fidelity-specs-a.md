**Answers to the specific question.** `fundBundle` has one arithmetic in real numbers, and most of the floating point is fixed (each tier's `S` summed in input order, `f = R ÷ S` once, a scaled tier's total defined as `R`, the exact `S <= R` test), but not all of it: the order of the final sum is open (FM-2) and the "exactly 1" property is false (FM-1). Because F8 and F9 compare coverages only through the 10⁻⁹ tolerance, neither changes an award in practice. `AVERAGE_DRAWN` (0.55, computed from `DRAW_UP_FRONT` 0.10, both tier 4, R11) is applied exactly where R11 says: to the loan's interest saving and to the guarantee's saving (`:254-255`); not to the `requiredReturn` term, not to `lent`, `guaranteed` or `reserve` (`:233`, `:291`, test `:427`). None of my other three specs uses either constant. (`CONSTRUCTION_LOAN_TO_COST` is also 0.55, a coincidence worth one sentence in the doc so that no one confuses them.)

**Legs.** Delegation: one finding (FM-3); everything else is decided, including error texts, rounding, number formats of the limit statements and tie rules. Gaps: every section of the brief's ten is present; every acceptance criterion has a test. Fidelity: no stale text (no −1.10, "a third", slope 2.8, "about 30%", fixed City fill order, loans before all grants, "upper bound" for loans, `#` ids). Evidence: spot-checked against the digest and the PDFs (see "What I checked").

**Answers to the specific questions.**

- **F9 (c), the award rule.** Specified exactly enough: asks are whole-dollar sums per pool of `projectDollars(granted) + projectDollars(lent) + projectDollars(reserve)` per item; a pool is short when `available < ask`; every short pool leaves in the same round with all its programmes, never returns; the loop ends when none is short or the set is empty; the award test is `S` non-empty and `level(S) ≥ level(B) − 1e-9`; serving is one scan in (applied, keyDay, parcel rank, kind) order with skip-over. No order among pools or programmes enters. The only residual divergence is `fundBundle`'s last-place arithmetic (FM-1, FM-2), which the tolerance absorbs. The four worked examples recompute (0.4106, 123,176.65, 176,850.35, 0.852).
- **F9 (b), the lapse rule.** Exact: re-pricing days are programme `from` and `to + 1`, `off` `from` and `to + 1`, and regime days; three lapse conditions (empty bundle; no size or zero hazard; level below the level just before D, with the tolerance); comparison against the bundle just before D, not the first; a lapse redraws from r′ = D − 1 on the same draws with a fresh `applied` and `keyDay`. The 0.863 example recomputes.
- **The known limit** is tested (`:496`): a spent pool with no due inflow keeps its applicant in line to the horizon; ending the programme lapses it on `to + 1`.
- **F18.** Backed by something actionable: the grouped-bisection algorithm, an eligibility list and an exact `skipAbove`-style bound, a named bench fixture with counts asserted (bench case 6), and a two-step fallback (precomputed `Float32Array` rate tables, then the conductor re-baselines at measured median plus 20%). The cost estimate is inferred, not measured, and says so.

**Legs.** Delegation: none found beyond FE-8 (warning order) and FE-9; "the implementation may use either" (`:239`) is between two equivalent implementations of a defined rule, and is fine. Gaps: FE-2 and FE-3. Fidelity: every clause of F9, F10, F11 and F18 has a behaviour and a test; no stale text. Evidence: the bench values are labelled tier 4; the Exhibit E and F amounts are tier 1.
