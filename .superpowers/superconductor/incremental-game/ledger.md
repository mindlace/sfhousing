- Partner request (verbatim):

  > I want to convert this into an (educational) incremental game, while retaining the ability for people to publish their solutions.
  >
  > The general pattern of an incremental game is that you start "weak" and gradually become more powerful until eventually you win. We're going to mix up that formula a little bit, but the underlying concept of unlock-with-earnings-each-round holds. For the moment I don't imagine a big upgrade tree like lots of incremental games have - just an unlock per "win".
  >
  > Here's the general pattern I imagine:
  > 1. you paint your way to the RHNA target, which unlocks the sim
  > 2. you have nothing you can change, so you run your way to the RHNA 2031 target by 2057 or so
  > 3. you can change permitting rules, and still have to hit the RHNA target
  > 4. you can change zoning rules, and have to beat your previous time to the RHNA target
  > 5. you get specific funding rules, and have to beat your previous time to the RHNA target
  > 6. you can design funding rules, and have to beat your target
  > 7. you get to change the market rules
  > 8. you unlock purchase affordability targets
  > 9. you unlock rental affordability targets
  >
  > Leveling up should be opt-in - even if the player beats the goal, they should still be able to tweak whatever they can at their level, and decide when they want to level up.
  >
  > Currently, nothing 6+ is implemented, and frankly 5 is pretty weak. It is ok if we do 1-5 in this first round, but funding needs to be more realistic - it needs to be finite funding pools.
  >
  > The whole "multiplayer" aspect - there's a whole invite/concurrent user subsystem - should be removed.
  >
  > Aside from the level 1-2 transition, all choices the user makes in a previous round should be active (but still changeable) in the subsequent level.
