Kinds stay closed. Tapping a kind lists its policies in the sheet; tapping one opens its editor full screen. Results sit behind the headline.

What is borrowed

  • A closed kind still shows what is inside. Logic's track stacks and Ableton's group tracks draw a miniature of the child clips on the closed row.
  • The filled-forecast bar sits on the ruler. Premiere's render bar and every streaming player put computed-versus-not on the time ruler: grey for filled, red for played. The rows past it are only dimmed.
  • The world sits in its own band under the ruler. Logic's global tracks (tempo, signature, markers) are set apart from the tracks you edit. Results and market conditions go there, with a lock.
  • Selecting a policy opens its editor beside the timeline. Resolve and Final Cut use a side inspector; Ableton a bottom panel. The timeline never leaves the screen.
  • A change in strength is a step inside the bar. Ableton and Logic draw automation on the clip; a policy that changes is one bar with a visible break, not two policies.
  • Off is dimmed, not removed. Final Cut dims disabled clips in place (the hatched bar under Public money).
  • Open-ended bars fade out to the right; bars with an end date have a square end.
  • Not borrowed: nesting that opens in a separate timeline (Premiere's nested sequences); editors ask for it to open in place.
