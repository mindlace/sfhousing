Incremental loop: runs, collected units and a tree of levers

Levels 2 to 5 become a loop of short runs, a tally and a tree. The player collects units from completing homes and spends them on policy levers. Paint and the simulation become one page, and the site is named SF Housing.

Goal

The game gives a player a reason to go one step further and hands over the forecasting tool one lever at a time. It never changes what the simulation forecasts.

  • A new game opens with the brush. The first goal is room for 82,069 homes. Meeting it starts the first run. The brush does not come back.
  • A run plays the forecast from today to the deadline in about 20 seconds. Every completing project leaves a drop worth its net new homes. The player sweeps the pointer over drops to collect them before they fade.
  • Every completed home counts toward the goal. Only collected homes become units to spend.
  • After a run a tally shows homes by the deadline, units collected, and where the homes not yet built stand.
  • Between runs the page is the tree. A node is a preset, a measure or a step in what the player may set. Buying one puts it in force from today and opens it in the editors. A bought lever can be switched off and on.
  • The goal stays 82,069 homes. The deadline moves in when the player chooses to take the next one.
  • The goal, its bar and what it unlocks are in the header at all times, with side goals behind a tap.
  • Publish sends what is true at that moment: the plan during the opening, the policy as it runs after it.
  • There is no Remix in this round.

The source model is docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-24-incremental-feel-design.html, approved 2026-11-24. Where this document and the source model differ, this document says so in Composer calls.

What the code is today

Read on commit 7bb0efbc. The survey reports are in .superpowers/superconductor/incremental-loop/surveys/.

  • The game layer is five levels. Progress holds a level and banked dates (src/lib/game/progress.ts:13). Allowed locks whole kinds of policy (src/lib/game/allowed.ts:5). Nothing is earned or spent.
  • Every policy in the document already has on: boolean and a pure setOn (src/lib/timeline/policy4.ts:41, src/lib/timeline/spans.ts:212).
  • The levers on offer are 3 permitting presets, a transit rule (4 stop types, up to 3 bands, 1 to 80 stories), 3 protections and 8 money measures, of which 6 add money. A measure is dated from the month it is added (src/lib/sim-page/funding-presets.ts:535).
  • The page receives one record per project, citywide: the CityDelta marker with a label point, the approved, started and completed days, gross homes and flags (src/lib/map/types.ts:70). The map stores it and draws nothing from it. It has no existing homes and no income tiers.
  • Homes by tier are computed in the worker at completion, summed by month and discarded per project (src/lib/sim/world/score/series.ts:345).
  • The funding line is worker state. No count of waiting homes is recorded or sent (src/lib/sim/world/funding/columns.ts:13).
  • The whole window is computed ahead of the playhead and replayed. A change dated from month 1 re-steps the window; a zoning change also builds a view, which took 4.4 to 4.7 s for one metro rule on the live city (docs/dev/policy-timeline.md:150, testimony).
  • Playback has two fixed speeds and no stop day. A seen result follows the furthest day reached, so a drag on the ruler counts the same as playing (src/lib/timeline/TimelineMode.svelte:619).
  • The game store is read in SimRoute, SimPage and TimelineMode, in the header's LevelCell, and on /, /p/[id] and /s/[id].
  • / mounts BrushEditor, which runs the map in generated mode. /sim runs the map in timeline mode, which a map keeps until it unmounts (src/lib/map/SimMap.svelte:407).
  • The map handle has no method to set or lock the view. setPanEnabled(false) leaves scroll, pinch and double-click zoom on (src/lib/map/SimMap.svelte:1107).
  • The name "SF Housing Simulator" is shown in 14 places, among them APP_NAME, PRODUCT_NAME (emails, the passkey prompt) and SES_FROM in wrangler.jsonc.
  • A saved game is v: 1; every parser refuses another version, and the game_save table has a level column.

Composer calls

These are mine. Each follows from a ruling or from what the code allows. Correct any that are wrong.

  1. A run is not the playhead. Run starts it; the ruler is off; drops exist only in a run. Between runs the timeline works as it does today and pays nothing. A goal is counted met when a run reaches its deadline. Reason: today a drag reaches the same result as playing, and P9 needs a run that can be collected from.
  2. A drop is worth net new homes: the project's homes less the homes it replaces. Reason: the goal counts net homes, so collected and completed are then the same kind of number.
  3. Tiers per project wait for P10's round. The engine does not keep them. This round keeps units by kind in the ledger and in prices, with one kind. The source model said a drop would carry tiers now.
  4. Custom timing and custom route shares open at the ending, not as nodes. The source model said both. A purchase must show in the next run, and those steps have no value to set.
  5. Every available measure starts the month it is bought. The source model said a measure starts on its own lawful date; none on offer has one.
  6. One address. / holds the game: the brush editor in the opening, the simulation page after it. /sim redirects to /. The map remounts once at that change and keeps the camera. Reason: the two map modes cannot share one map instance.
  7. The header's unit count opens the game panel: saved games, New game, the sync line, the sound switch.
  8. A deadline once met stays met after a data update, as banked results did (D9).
  9. The open game is the whole tool with no runs, tally or tree. It is what the server renders, what /s/<id> shows and what every e2e spec that seeds no game runs.
  10. Deadlines step in by five years from the window's end to the earliest year the full tree reaches. A build unit measures that year.
  11. Side goals read what the page already has at the deadline's month and the run's own counts.
  12. The tally's "waiting for money" row is its own build unit. The tally ships with stage counts and gains the row when that unit lands.
  13. Drops are drawn on a canvas over the map that takes no pointer events. The map itself gains only a run view.
  14. The run view is flat and north up, fitted to the city. Reason: every drop is then reachable and nothing hides behind a tower.
  15. The name changes in the user-facing places, SES_FROM, the tests that expect it and the documents. The passkey's rpID, the worker, the bucket, the database and the repository keep theirs.
  16. Protections and the two switches that take money away are free. They appear when their branch has its first purchase.
  17. Collect for me is a setting from the start with no penalty, and is also what the last Hands node gives.
  18. Sound is on by default, made in the browser with no audio files, with a switch in the game panel.
  19. During a run the map is the flat basemap and the drops. At the zoom that fits the city, existing buildings do not draw and progress bars are faint or absent. A pop is the drop appearing.
  20. The tree fills the page below the header, as in the wireframe you approved. A bought node carries its own on and off switch.
  21. At / the open game shows the simulation. A test that needs the opening seeds a new visitor.
  22. The ladder has at most five rungs, so each rung opens a ring of the tree.
  23. Homes waiting for money is read at the end of the model month that holds the deadline, up to about 30 days off. The funding line is served once a model month.
  24. A five-level saved game found in a browser is deleted at load, and the game_save table is dropped and made again by generated migrations (P15).
  25. Every number the balance script tunes is in one file, src/lib/game/catalogue/tuning.ts.
  26. A bought node is not shown on the map before the next run. The tree covers the map (call 20). The purchase shows on the node, and in the next run and its tally, which names the change from the run before. The source model said the area the node affects would light on the map.
  27. A first run that fails starts again. The tree is never shown before a run has been settled.
  28. A collected drop does not fly to the unit count. It rings out where it lay and its homes rise there as "+N"; the unit count in the header rolls to the new balance when the run is settled. The run's canvas covers only the map's box, the count sits in the Menu below lg, and a flight for each of hundreds of drops would break the flash limit and the layer's frame budget. The source model said a collected drop flies to the unit count.

You approved the design with calls 1 to 18 on 2026-11-24. Calls 19 to 28 came from writing the specs and were told to you as they were made.

  • The first run starts from the opening card. Every later run starts from the tree.
  • The workshop is the forecasting tool (P1). Its editors list only what is bought.
  • The header is the same in every state: the name, the save state, the goal, the unit count, Publish, the account, About.

A run

  1. Run is pressed. The page waits for the forecast's first results and says so.
  2. The map goes to the run view. The clock plays from today to the deadline in a fixed wall time.
  3. A project whose completed day the clock passes leaves a drop at its label point, worth its net new homes. A project worth nothing leaves none. A project that carries the map's policy mark pops as a diamond in the policy's colour.
  4. A drop is collected when the collecting ring touches it. A drop not collected fades after its linger time.
  5. The ring follows the pointer. The arrow keys move it. With "Collect for me" every drop is collected as it appears.
  6. The goal's bar fills from the forecast as the clock moves.
  7. At the deadline the run is settled: the collected units, a lump if this deadline is met for the first time, and the reward of each side goal first met are added to the balance.

The tree

  • A node has one or more levels. Each level has a price and a grant.
  • A node is shown when its ring is open and one of the nodes it comes after is bought. A ring opens when its deadline comes into play.
  • Buying spends the price, records the level and applies the grant to the policy document, dated from month 1.
  • A grant is a permitting preset, a money measure, a stop type, a further band, a higher story cap, a longer reach, or a Hands step.
  • What is bought sets the limits: which presets and measures run, which stop types, how many bands, how tall, how far.
  • A policy outside the limits does not run and cannot be made in the editors.
  • Switching a bought lever off is the document's own switch. It refunds nothing.
  • Meeting the last deadline lifts the limits on stories, bands, reach, custom timing and route shares. The painted area, own pools, the City funding scale and market terms stay out.

Goals

  • The ladder is a list of deadlines. Rung 0 is the window's end. Each later rung is five or more years earlier.
  • Meeting a rung offers the next one. Taking it is the player's choice (R5).
  • Up to three side goals are shown at a time, each with a reward in units.
  • Meeting the last rung is the ending: the year reached beside the state's own deadline for the goal, the levers bought, public money per added home, and Publish.

Room for affordable units (P10)

  • A balance and a price are amounts by kind of unit. This round has one kind, home.
  • Adding a kind is a change to the kind list, the drop's worth and the catalogue's prices. Saved games stay readable.

G4 Limits

// src/lib/game/limits.ts
import type { StopType } from '$lib/sim/levers/brushes';
import type { Policy, PolicyKind, SimPolicy4 } from '$lib/timeline/policy4';
import type { GameState } from './state';
import type { TreeNode } from './tree';

export interface Hands {
  radiusPx: number;
  lingerMs: number;
  auto: boolean;
}

export interface Limits {
  open: boolean;
  presets: ReadonlySet<string>;
  measures: ReadonlySet<string>;
  stops: ReadonlySet<StopType>;
  bands: number;
  stories: number;
  reachM: number;
  custom: boolean;
  hands: Hands;
}

export const OPEN_LIMITS: Limits;
export const BASE_HANDS: Hands;
export const START_BANDS = 1;
export const START_STORIES = 4;
export const START_REACH_M = 402;
export const FREE_MEASURES: readonly string[];

export function limitsOf(s: GameState, nodes?: readonly TreeNode[]): Limits;
export function within(kind: PolicyKind, p: Policy, l: Limits): boolean;
export function runnable(doc: SimPolicy4, l: Limits): SimPolicy4;
export function isGiven(p: Policy): boolean;
open
The open game: every control, no limit (call 9).
presets, measures
The permitting preset ids and funding preset ids that run.
bands, stories, reachM
The most bands a transit rule may have, its tallest band and its furthest band. All 0 before the first stop type is bought.
custom
Custom timing and custom route shares may be set.
FREE_MEASURES
state-off and federal-credits-off.
  • limitsOf folds the grants of every level bought. The largest stories, reach, bands, radius and linger win.
  • Once a stop type is bought, bands, stories and reachM are at least START_BANDS, START_STORIES and START_REACH_M.
  • BASE_HANDS is a radius of 48 px, a linger of 4,000 ms and no auto. Every Hands step in the catalogue is above it.
  • Once the last rung is met, stories is 80, bands 3, reachM 3219, every stop type is in, custom is true, and presets also holds observed, the preset a custom timing policy carries.
  • within does not read a policy's on.
  • A permitting policy is within the limits when its preset is in presets, and, unless custom, it has no custom edit and no custom route shares.
  • A transit rule is within the limits when its stop types are in stops, it has at most bands bands, no band is taller than stories and none is further than reachM.
  • A protection is within the limits once stops is not empty.
  • A painted area is within the limits only when open (P11).
  • A money policy is within the limits when it is given and its preset is in measures, or its preset is in FREE_MEASURES and measures is not empty. Any other money policy only when open (D4).

Global constraints

  • One future: the same policy document always gives the same forecast. No game state reaches the worker except through the policy document.
  • No file the dataset version hashes is changed: pipeline, src/lib/config.ts, src/lib/{data,generator,timelapse}, src/lib/plan/{geometry,types}.ts, src/lib/sim/finance (pipeline/lib/version.ts:41). The dataset version stays as it is.
  • A policy node acts on a lever the code already has, at a value the code already sources. No node invents a value.
  • The painted area, own pools, the City funding scale and market terms are never offered in a game.
  • Published plans and policies open and play in full, as today.
  • WCAG 2.2 AA: everything works from the keyboard; targets are at least 24 px; motion follows the reduced-motion setting; nothing flashes more than three times a second.
  • Page text is short. A node is a name, a line and a price.
  • The CSP is not widened. Sound is made in the browser; no audio file, font or script is added.
  • A runtime dependency is not added without a credit in src/lib/attribution.ts. None is expected.
  • Code style: 2-space indent, single quotes, no trailing commas, 100 columns.
  • Vitest runs with expect.requireAssertions. Tests are colocated.
  • A schema change goes through npm run db:generate. Migrations are never edited by hand.
  • Signoff fails on server stderr, on timing crutches and on retried flakes. Timing is measured only on a quiet machine.
  • Port 4173 belongs to whichever session started the preview. Never kill it; wait.
  • Commits use Conventional Commits and carry no Claude attribution.
  • Work happens in git worktrees under .claude/worktrees/.

Commitment layering

  • Model commitments come from your request and rulings. Changing one needs you: G1 (units by kind, what a game holds), G2, G3 (what a node is and how it is shown and bought), G4 (what runs), G6 (the ladder, side goals, what a run pays), R1 (what a drop is worth and how it is collected), and rulings P1 to P15.
  • Decomposition choices are mine. A composer fork may change one alone: G5, G7, G8, E1, E2, M1, U1, T1, the file names, the canvas for drops, the cut into subsystems.
  • The composer calls 1 to 28 are decomposition choices unless they restate a ruling. Calls 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 10, 16, 26 and 28 touch what the player earns, sees or may do, or narrow the source model; a change to one of them is told to you after the fact in rulings/.
