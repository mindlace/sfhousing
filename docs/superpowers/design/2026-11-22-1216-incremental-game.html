Incremental game: levels 1 to 5, finite public money, one player

The app becomes a game of reaching the homes goal. Each win unlocks one kind of policy. Public money runs out. Shared sims, invitations and the two side pages go.

Goal

A person starts weak and gets stronger, one unlock per win, and can publish what they made at any point.

  • Levelling up is the player's choice. A win makes it available; it never happens by itself.
  • From level 2 on, every policy the player made stays in force at the next level and stays editable. Nothing painted at level 1 enters the simulation.
  • Today's public money is finite: each source ends when its evidence says it ends, and federal tax credits have a yearly ceiling.
  • One player per game. Invitations, members, roles and shared sims are removed.
  • A player is anonymous and bound to their device until they choose a username, add an email, a password, a passkey or Google. Their saved games are kept on that account.
  • A player can have several saved games, each with its own level, bars, plan and policy. Remixing a published plan or policy makes a new saved game.
  • Current law always reaches the goal inside the window, so every level can be won.
  • Publishing a plan (/p) or a policy (/s) works as it does today.
  • /fzp and /lesscars are removed with everything built only for them.

Levels 6 to 9 (design funding rules, market rules, purchase and rental affordability targets) and the less-cars unlocks (adding transit, removing highways) are named so that nothing built now blocks them. They are not built now.

The game

One level, start to finish

  1. The player arrives at a level with the clock at today and every unlocked kind editable.
  2. They change what they can and play. The forecast runs as it does today.
  3. When the playhead reaches the month the goal is met, or the window's end, the result is seen.
  4. If the seen result wins the level, a card says so and names what levelling up unlocks. It has two buttons: "Level up" and "Keep playing". It also offers a username.
  5. "Keep playing" closes the card. The header's level cell keeps a "Level up" button for as long as the current policy's seen result wins.
  6. "Level up" banks the result, raises the level, returns the clock to today and opens the new kind's row. The policies stay.
  7. A seen result that does not win shows today's message over the map, reworded to name the bar: "Goal reached in Mar 2058. Beat Jun 2057 to level up.", or "Goal missed by 3,313 homes. Reach it before Jun 2057 to level up."

Finite public money

  • Every one of today's sources carries a bound: the day its money stops and where that was read, or the statement that law sets no end and where that was read.
  • Federal tax credits become a pool with a yearly amount, the ceiling: the most the pool receives in a year. Credits not awarded in their year stay in the pool and can be awarded later, as California carries unused bond ceiling forward, so the ceiling bounds what arrives each year, not what is awarded in one year. No pool can always pay: the uncapped flag leaves the model.
  • Every funding preset has an end. The three whose money has none today ("General Fund set-aside", "federal support", "state money doubled") get a stated term.
  • A pool's bar on the timeline shows what is left of all the money the pool receives in the window. It starts full and drains as its money is allocated; a yearly payment never refills it. A pool with no end counts its payments to the window's end.
  • The funding editors take the window's last month from the dataset date, in place of the fixed December 2066.
  • The window's last month is the calendar month that holds the window's last day. The goal's deadline is the last whole month, one before it. On today's live data those are November 2066 and October 2066; on the fixture, October 2066 and September 2066.
  • A closed pool's bar reads empty from the month it ran out until it opens again, because what it still holds is never awarded (F3). Details carries the "ran out" line.
  • A source that has had its last money and cannot pay a project closes (D13). From the next month its share is out of every project's funding, as when a source is switched off, and Details says when it ran out.
  • Yearly lumps, the line and re-pricing are unchanged. The award rule changes only in that a closed source is never part of a bundle.
  • Debt service is still reported and not paid from a pool, and the legal borrowing limits are still statements. Making them bind belongs with level 6.

Accounts and the save

  • Every saved game is in localStorage first. The game works with no network.
  • At the first win the browser sends its saved games. With no session, the server makes an anonymous account and sets its cookie on that response.
  • After that, every change to a saved game is sent, debounced.
  • Each win card shows the account line. While the handle has never been chosen it is a field: "Save as @…". Once chosen it reads "@handle" and links to the account page, where an email, a password, a passkey and Google are added.
  • Signing in on another device brings the account's saved games to that device and sends the device's own to the account. All are kept (D8).
  • When two devices have changed the same saved game, the one that saves second keeps its version as a new saved game named "{name} (this device)" and takes the account's version under the old name.
  • A saved game deleted on one device is deleted on the others the next time they sync, where it is unchanged. A device that changed it before learning of the delete keeps its version as its own saved game, under the same name: a delete never discards a change (D8).
  • Deleting the account deletes its saved games. Published plans and policies stay. Reason: a saved game is its player's own and is never shared; once the account is gone nobody can sign in to open it, so keeping it would keep nothing anyone can reach. What others can open, the published plans and policies and their links, is kept.
  • The header's account entry, /account, /signin, password reset and email verification stay. "Start fresh without an account" on /signin goes: the game makes the anonymous account.

  • changed(id) sends nothing while this browser has no session and the game's firstWinAt is null.

  • One future: no seed control, no band, no average over runs.
  • Published plans and policies, their ids and their pages keep working, including every policy published before this build.
  • A number that is a claim about the world carries its source and tier, in the existing Sourced, Unsourced and Evidence shapes.
  • A runtime dependency that ships in the bundle gets a credit in SOFTWARE. This build adds none.
  • CSP changes are interface changes. This build makes none.
  • Timing is measured only on a quiet machine. A unit that cannot get one defers the measurement and says so.

Commitment layering

  • Model commitments come from your request and rulings. Changing one needs you: G1, G2, G3, S1, F1, F2, F3, and decisions D1 to D14.
  • Decomposition choices are how the work is cut. A composer fork may change one alone: G4, G5, S2, S3, I1, L1, M1, X1, the write sets, the storage key names, the debounce, the save's size limit, the table's columns.
  • During the build, a question that touches a model commitment is ruled by a composer fork from this package and recorded for you to read afterwards.

Out of scope

  • Levels 6 to 9: designing funding rules, market rules, purchase and rental affordability targets.
  • Less-cars unlocks: adding transit, removing highways, freed land.
  • Choosing an upgrade after a win.
  • Goals other than 82,069 homes by the window's end: a larger amount, a nearer deadline, "10% cheaper housing".
  • Making debt service or the legal borrowing limits bind.
  • A free-play switch that opens every control.
  • Leaderboards, or showing other players' results beside yours.
  • Bringing the level 1 plan into the simulation.
  • Removing the older scenario engine. It stays as the step engine's referee.
  • Renaming src/lib/fzp.
  • Re-running a banked policy on new data to move its bar.
  • Making the pipeline's upload or signoff refuse a dataset on which current law misses the goal. The check is a documented step of finishing a dataset branch.
  • Merging two saved games into one.
  • Sharing a saved game with another player.
