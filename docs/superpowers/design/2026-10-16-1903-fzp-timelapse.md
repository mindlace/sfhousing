# Family Zoning Plan timelapse: composition design

Status: **approved with partner rulings** (2026-10-16; OD-T1..T5 ruled, see Rulings). This is where we discuss the design: edit it directly. Partner approval of this doc is the gate before per-subsystem specs are derived.

Source model: [the SF Housing Simulator design](2026-10-10-1749-sf-housing-sim.md). Its seam rows `scenario-loader` (M3), `time-permitting` (M4), `buildout-likelihood` (M2) and `pipeline-overlay` (M5) are the starting points; this package delivers a narrowed slice of each (see Traceability). Research: [literature survey](../research/2026-10-16-fzp-calibration-literature.md) and [dataset probe](../research/2026-10-16-sf-stage-datasets.md).

## Goal

A read-only **Family Zoning Plan mode**. You open it and see San Francisco today, with the adopted Family Zoning Plan (FZP, effective 2026-01-12) as the zoning. Then you travel in time from the dataset's snapshot date (today) to the end of **2050** and watch the city change the way it plausibly will:

- **Every real project already in the pipeline** starts at its real stage, with its real dates.
- **New projects** that the FZP makes possible start on parcels at rates measured from SF's own history.
- Each project appears as a **ghost** as soon as there is any record of it (a pre-application, a Planning application or a permit filing). A **progress bar** shows it moving through the real sequence of stages: entitlement, building permit, construction. It **can die** at any stage, at the rate projects really die there. It **can change size** before construction. Once construction starts the existing building goes. On completion the ghost becomes a solid building.

You cannot paint in this mode. Later, policy levers will change the inputs so people can compare futures.

Partner intent, in their words:

- "watch buildings get added according to some realistic timeframe"
- "ideally rendered from the permit phase onward, like a 'ghost' of a planned building appears with a 'health bar' that is the permitting and building process"
- "to the extent possible I want to realistically simulate how many projects don't make it and in which stage … if 10% of projects die in permit step z, that should be true of our model"
- "allow for at least some buildings to change (to show e.g. downsizing/upsizing before construction)"
- "Eventually I want to tie this to actual records of development, all the way down to models"
- "Eventually I want to let people simulate changing policies to see the difference"

### What the evidence says the answer looks like

These numbers are not design inputs. They set expectations, so nobody mistakes a correct result for a bug.

- **Planning's** estimates for the FZP are 19k–64k units over about 5 years. The **Controller's** are 8.5k–14.6k units by **2045**, about 730 a year. The two differ by roughly 10×.
- The Controller's 2026 feasibility review finds every apartment prototype loses money (−$96k to −$363k per unit). SF completed 405 units in H1 2026.
- The real pipeline holds about 75.9k units (2026-Q2). Of those, 3.3k are under construction and about 58k are entitled but not under construction.

So through 2031 the map will be dominated by **pipeline projects**, with many of them stalling. The new FZP-enabled starts will be modest. If the timelapse shows that honestly, it is working.

## Evidence hierarchy (partner ruling; global)

Every number the simulation uses comes from the highest tier available. It carries that tier and its source, and the UI can show both.

| tier | kind | example here |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | per-record time series | DBI permit dates, Planning approval dates, TCO/CFC dates |
| 1 | published first-party aggregates | Housing Inventory production tables, quarterly pipeline report |
| 2 | researched inferences from observations | a redevelopment hazard fitted to SF parcel history; resize ratios measured across linked records |
| 3 | research models / literature | NAHB construction durations, statutory review clocks used as duration proxies |
| 4 | made up | anything else, labelled as such |

Consequence: calibration is **computed by the data pipeline from DataSF records**, and literature figures are used only where the records can't answer. The dataset probe found tier-0 coverage for almost every stage. There is no tier-0 figure for deaths before any filing, or for the new Local Program's own durations (it has been in force only since 2026-01-12).

## Stage model (model commitment)

The real sequence of an SF housing project, at the granularity public records can observe:

```
(preApplication) ──► filed ──► entitled ──► permitFiled ──► permitIssued ──► construction ──► complete
  │          │             │               │                │
  └─ exit ───┴──── exit ───┴───── exit ────┴───── exit ─────┘
```

| stage entered | observed by (tier 0) |
|---|---|
| `preApplication` (optional) | PPA child record `open_date` (`y673-d69b`, record type PPA) |
| `filed` | Planning PRJ `open_date` (`qvu5-m3a2`); for `permitOnly`, the site/full permit `filed_date` |
| `entitled` | "accepted → approved" end date (`d4jk-jw33`) |
| `permitFiled` | site/full permit `filed_date` (`i98e-djp9`, `primary_address_flag='Y'`) |
| `permitIssued` | site/full permit `issued_date` |
| `construction` | `first_construction_document_date` (site permits), else `issued_date` |
| `complete` | first TCO or CFC (`j67f-aayr`), else permit `completed_date` |
| exit | Planning `project_decision` Disapproved/Withdrawn/Cancelled; permit `status` withdrawn/cancelled/expired/disapproved, dated by `status_date` |

Projects follow one of three **pathways**:
- `discretionary`: Planning Commission or other discretionary entitlement.
- `streamlined`: SB 35, SB 423, AB 2011, the FZP Local Program, or the HSD ministerial path.
- `permitOnly`: no Planning entitlement; it starts at `permitFiled`.

Each pathway has its own transition tables where the data can separate them.

Each transition is a **competing-risks** table. For each month spent in a stage it gives the cumulative chance of advancing and the cumulative chance of each exit reason. The tables are estimated with censoring handled: a project still open today counts as "survived so far", not as dead or done. **The simulated cohort therefore reproduces the observed cumulative incidence of each exit at each stage by construction.** That is the partner's "if 10% die at step z, ours do too." An in-flight project is sampled from the same table conditioned on the time it has already spent in its stage.

Pre-application (PPA) is an **optional first stage**: a real project enters it when a PPA record exists, and a simulated project enters it with the measured share of filings that were preceded by a PPA. PPA records that never become a PRJ are counted as `withdrawn` exits from `preApplication`. Deaths before any record at all remain unobservable and are not modelled. Addenda-level review (`87xy-gk8d`) is not split into sub-stages in this package. The data supports doing that later (see Out of scope).

## Subsystems

- **`scenario-data`** (data pipeline). Ingests the adopted FZP heights from DataSF `h9wh-cg3m` (`base//localProgram-R-4` strings, verified 2026-10-16) and the assessor's secured roll `wv5m-vpq2` (year built, units, stories, land and improvement value; PDDL). It joins both to parcels. For every eligible parcel it writes the FZP envelope, the redevelopment features, and a small yield table (units at each quantised fill level of the envelope, computed with the existing generator). Output is a compact columnar `scenarios/fzp/parcels.bin` under the dataset version. With it the worker can score the whole city without loading every parcel chunk. Height-to-stories rule: `stories = floor(heightFt / 10)`, so 40 ft gives 4 stories, matching Planning's framing (orchestrator call, tier 3). New simulated projects use the **Local Program** height (orchestrator call).
- **`project-records`** (data pipeline). Ingests the tier-0 project datasets (`i98e-djp9`, `d4jk-jw33`, `qvu5-m3a2`, `y673-d69b`, `j67f-aayr`, `xdht-4php`, `6jgi-cpb4`) and links them into one record per project: PRJ ↔ permits via `building_permits`, plus parcel joins. It emits two things:
  - (a) **historical project histories**, as input to calibration only, kept in the pipeline cache and not shipped;
  - (b) **`scenarios/fzp/projects.json`**: every project in flight at the snapshot date, with pathway, stage, dated stage history, units, stories where known, parcels, and source ids.
  It also computes **net units completed since the RHNA period start** up to the snapshot, from completion records (tier 0), cross-checked against the Housing Inventory (tier 1).
- **`stage-calibration`** (data pipeline). Builds `scenarios/fzp/calibration.json` from the histories:
  - competing-risks transition tables per pathway and stage, with censoring;
  - the **initiation model**: a discrete-time hazard that a parcel with no project files one in a given month. It is fitted to SF parcel-years using pre-FZP envelopes and features, then applied with FZP envelopes (tier 2);
  - the **size model**: what fraction of the allowed envelope a project proposes, measured from proposed stories against allowed height (tier 0→2);
  - the **revision model**: the chance, timing and magnitude of a change in unit count, measured by comparing the PRJ proposal, the permit's `proposed_units` and the completed units of linked records (tier 0→2).
  Every parameter carries its provenance (T4). **Fitting window (partner ruling OD-T3):** the longest window available across the majority of stages, with no weighting toward recent years. For each stage, take the earliest year with reliable tier-0 coverage. The common window starts at the latest of those years that still keeps a majority of stages fully covered, and ends at the snapshot. A stage whose coverage starts later uses its own span, and its provenance says so. Initiation is fitted over all available parcel-years with no calendar-year term.
- **`pipeline-massing`** (typology generator). Adds `generateForUnits(parcel, units, maxStories, stories?)`. It inverts the existing generator to mass a real project from its unit count and, where known, its stories. It also supports multi-parcel projects by massing on the union of their parcels. `GeneratedBuilding.source` widens to `'user' | 'pipeline' | 'scenario'`, which design-doc I9 already reserved.
- **`timelapse-sim`** (worker). From the scenario parcels, the in-flight projects, the calibration and a seed, it builds the **schedule** of every project. A schedule is a pure function of `(dataset, seed)` (T5). It also runs N schedule-only realisations for the **uncertainty band**. It generates geometry lazily: only for projects in loaded chunks, and once per revision. It posts a timeline (T6–T7) once per seed. **Scrubbing time never round-trips to the worker.**
- **`timelapse-render`** (map view). Renders timeline geometry so that the state at time `t` is decided on the GPU from per-part time windows and per-project stage times, with `t` passed as a uniform. That state covers hidden, ghost, constructing, complete and dying. It draws the per-project progress bar. It hides each existing building from its project's construction start. `setTime(day)` costs O(1) JavaScript regardless of project count.
- **`fzp-mode`** (route `/fzp`). A read-only page: the map, plus a **time scrubber** (play/pause, speed, date readout, keyboard-operable), a **time-aware scoreboard**, a ghost-state legend, a **project card** on hover or tap, a **"Where these numbers come from"** panel listing each parameter's tier and source, and a **"Show another possible future"** reroll. There is a header link between the editor and this mode.

## Dependency order

1. `scenario-data` ∥ `project-records` ∥ `pipeline-massing` ∥ `timelapse-render` (against hand-made `TimelineUpdate` fixtures).
2. `stage-calibration` (needs `project-records` histories).
3. `timelapse-sim` (needs T3/T4/T11 artifacts; developed against a fixture calibration and the fixture dataset).
4. `fzp-mode`.

A dataset release containing the three `scenarios/fzp/*` files is the integration milestone. `fixture-0` gains mini versions of all three.

## Global constraints

The source design's constraints still apply (free and open, determinism, overshoot meaningful, honesty of numbers, performance, mobile, static versioned data, conventions). In addition:

- **Evidence hierarchy** (above). No number reaches the simulation without a tier and a source. A tier-3 or tier-4 number is never presented as data.
- **Determinism.** The same `datasetVersion` and seed always give the same future, on every machine. The representative seed is chosen deterministically: the run whose final `completedNet` is the median of the N band runs.
- **Honesty of numbers.** The mode is labelled a *simulation*. The scoreboard shows the shown run and the p10–p90 band. Pipeline and new-FZP units are separate lines. Actual completions since the RHNA period start are counted and labelled as actual. Units lost to dead projects are shown.
- **Time scrubbing performance.** At 60 fps on a 2022-era laptop with the whole city's timeline loaded, per-frame JS during scrub or playback is O(1) in project count. A reroll reaches a full new timeline (the shown run, to 2050) within 3 s off the main thread. The p10–p90 band may fill in progressively afterwards; the UI marks it as provisional until it is complete.
- **First paint.** `/fzp` must not require loading every parcel chunk. The whole-city score comes from `parcels.bin`, `projects.json` and `calibration.json`. Geometry streams with the viewport, as on `/`.
- **The editor is unchanged.** `/` and `/p/:id` behave exactly as before. FZP mode is additive.
- **Accessibility.** WCAG 2.2 AA as in `docs/dev/accessibility.md`. The scrubber is a labelled slider: arrow keys step a month, Page Up/Down a year. Reduced motion stops autoplay, but scrubbing still works.

## Out of scope (this package)

- Policy levers (the T9 seam only) and market scenarios.
- Splitting permit review into addenda/station sub-stages (`87xy-gk8d` supports it later).
- Development Agreement phasing schedules for multi-phase projects (Treasure Island, Candlestick, Parkmerced): their pipeline rows run through the ordinary stage tables, and R10 enrichment is deferred.
- Real renderings or 3D models of pipeline projects.
- Affordability breakdowns.
- Sharing a timelapse view.
- Painting in FZP mode.
- SB 79.
- The FZP lawsuits: the plan is treated as in force.

## Rulings (partner, 2026-10-16)

| # | question | ruling |
|---|---|---|
| OD-T1 | When does the ghost appear? | **As soon as we have data**: at the project's first dated record, including a pre-application. That makes `preApplication` an optional first stage (T2). Simulated projects appear at their first simulated stage. |
| OD-T2 | What the ghost and its bar look like | **Option A** from the mockups: a translucent blue massing of the planned building, with a floating stage bar. Bar length = progress through stages; bar colour = the project's current chance of finishing, given its stage and time in stage. Construction turns floors solid from the bottom up. A death turns the ghost red, then it fades. Bars show at street zoom; below that, the ghost's tint encodes the stage (exact zoom and fade timings are for the timelapse-render spec). |
| OD-T3 | Which years of history set the rates? | **The longest window available across the majority of stages.** No subset of 2014–present stands in for the future. The rule is in `stage-calibration`. |
| OD-T4 | Timeline end | **2050-12-31**, because the city's two estimates disagree on whether the shortfall closes by 2031 (Planning/RHNA) or 2045 (Controller). Markers at the RHNA deadline (2031-01-31) and 2045. Both published estimates appear as reference lines (T8 `references`). |
| OD-T5 | Can real pipeline projects die? (tension with R8) | **Yes. All projects are treated the same once in the future.** R8 ("as if built as stated") is narrowed: it does not apply to this mode. |
