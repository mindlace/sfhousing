# `finance-engine`: funded starts, the application queue and the pools

Package: `sim-financing` ([design](../design/2026-11-09-1911-sim-financing.md), approved 2026-11-09; [evidence digest](../research/2026-11-09-financing-evidence.md)). This spec decides the behaviour of rows F9, F10, F11 and F18 and builds against rows F1 to F8 and F12. It runs in step 3 of the design's dependency order, after `finance-model` and Release 1 (`market-baseline` with `finance-data`), and before `finance-score` and `finance-levers`.

**Amended on 2026-11-10 (reappraisal 2).** Rebased on the paired-draw fix (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-10-paired-draw-fix-design.md`): section 11 lists the changes.

Terms used throughout, defined once:

- **Lot**: one T11 row of `SimInput.parcels`. A **candidate** is a lot the existing engine can initiate on (`Precompute.candidates`, `src/lib/sim/scenario/input.ts:85-89`).
- **Attempt k**: the k-th project on a lot, k = 0, 1, 2, …, each on its own draw stream `streamOf(sh, entity, k)` (`src/lib/sim/scenario/rng.ts:48-50`). Today a lot is at risk from day `r` (its risk start: `asOf`, or its holders' last exit), and after a simulated exit on day x it is at risk again from x with k + 1 (`src/lib/sim/scenario/engine.ts:1026-1044`).
- **Month T of an attempt**: the T-th month after `r`, which starts on day `start(T) = r + 1 + floor((T − 1) × MONTH_DAYS)`. This is the existing grid of `initiationDay` (`engine.ts:891-896`) and `lastInitiationMonth` (`input.ts:147-154`).
- **Ordinary start**: the start the engine makes today, on the INIT and INIT_DAY draws, at the lot's hazard under `regimes` and `market`. It is kind `market` and carries no money.
- **Funded latent start**: for each project kind that some programme reaches, a second possible start on the same lot and attempt (F9 (a)). It turns into an **application** on its day.
- **Bundle**: the programmes a project is offered (F9 (b)); **certain**: `isCertain` of its coverage (`finance-model`, coverage ≥ 1 − 1e-9).
- **Coverage level**: `level(c) = isCertain(c) ? 1 : c`. This is the row's "coverage, counted up to 1" (F9 (b), (c)), with `isCertain`'s tolerance, so a level is 1 exactly when the bundle is certain. Two levels are compared with the same tolerance, as F8 now requires ("Every comparison of two coverages in F9 (b) and (c) allows the same tolerance"): x is **below** y when `x < y − 1e-9`. The 1e-9 is `finance-model`'s `CERTAIN_TOLERANCE` (F8: "a floating-point tolerance, not a model number"); no other tolerance is introduced.
- **Off**: a pool is off on day d when some entry of `finance.off` names it with `from ≤ d` and (`to` null or `d ≤ to`) (F9, F7). Overlapping entries of one pool are allowed and mean their union.
- **Awarded set**: the programmes an award actually uses, after the programmes of short pools are left out (F9 (c), section 4.6).
- **The line**: the applications waiting for money, across all lots and both kinds.
- **Pool**: F5. **Capped pool**: a pool with `uncapped: false`.

## 1. Scope

The design's subsystem row:

| Slug | Scope |
| --- | --- |
| `finance-engine` | The engine's funded starts: one latent start per reached project kind, the bundle, the application queue, pool commitments, releases and repayments, the ledger events, the arrival rule for inflows, the project card's funding fields, and the same results on the net fast path. Performance budgets. |

**In:**

1. `SimInput.finance` (F9) read by the engine: validation, the per-attempt funded latent starts, the bundle (with pools switched off by `finance.off`), the line and its order, pool accounting, the award rule that leaves out the programmes of short pools (F9 (c)), the funded projects' stages (certain projects never exit), re-pricing in place and lapse (including lapse when the coverage level falls), the reservation rule for applications, and the next attempt after a funded project exits.
2. `Schedule.kind` and `Schedule.funding` (F10), including `displaced`, all from the awarded set's pricing.
3. `Sink.ledger` events (F10), each with its `programme`: what is emitted, when, and in which order within a day.
4. `ProjectCard.kind` and `ProjectCard.funding` (F10), filled by `projectCard` in `src/lib/sim/scenario/card.ts`. Step 0 declares every F9 and F10 field this spec fills (`SimInput.finance?`, `Schedule.kind?`, `Schedule.funding?`, `Sink.ledger?`, `ProjectCard.kind?`, `ProjectCard.funding?`, in `src/lib/sim/scenario/types.ts`; design "Dependency order", item 0); this subsystem adds their behaviour, not their declarations.
5. Draw purposes `INIT_AFFORDABLE` and `INIT_DAY_AFFORDABLE` (F11).
6. `inflowArrivals` (F5), the one implementation, in `src/lib/sim/scenario/funding.ts`; and `PIPELINE_FUNDED` (F9 (l)).
7. `sampleAdvance` in `src/lib/timelapse/model.ts`, a named form of the paired-draw fix's `sampleTime` (section 4.18).
8. `buildSimInput` setting `finance` from the calibration (F9's last sentences), and the body of `marketLevel(calibration)` (`src/lib/sim/scenario/market.ts`, shipped by `market-baseline` returning `'all'`) changed to F3's rule, so the engine and the page's statement of the market switch to the private-starts level together (F3).
9. The equality of `simulateNet` with the detailed run when `finance` is present.
10. The performance budgets of F18: the algorithm, the bench cases, the fallback.
11. Naming the fixture goldens that change because the default now runs the baseline funding, and the Release 2 steps; the regeneration, the dataset build and upload and the signoff happen once, at Release 2, on the release branch, not on this subsystem's branch.

**Out:**

- The market shift itself (`market-baseline`, F2 and F3). This spec uses the piecewise hazard F3 defines and adds programme and pool days to its segments; it does not respecify it.
- Grant-equivalents, coverage, `fundBundle`, `isCertain`, `projectDollars`, `MarketTerms`, `ResolvedTerms` (`finance-model`, F4 and F8).
- What the baseline holds and its ids (`finance-data`, F12).
- `FinanceLevers`, `baselineScale`, building the `off` spans from scales of 0, the twin's input, clipping arrivals before `asOf` on the levers path (`finance-levers`, F7, F15 to F17).
- Collecting the ledger in `simulateDetailed` and every result series (`finance-score`, F13 and F14).
- Every piece of UI (`funding-ui`), including the card line's text.

**Hands to:**

- `finance-score`: funded schedules (`kind`, `funding`), ledger events with `programme` in day order with the protocol of section 4.7, and the assumption `PIPELINE_FUNDED` (section 4.13).
- `finance-levers`: the engine's validation messages (section 4.2), so a compile bug surfaces with a path; the single `inflowArrivals` function (section 4.3, F5).
- `funding-ui`: `ProjectCard.kind` and `ProjectCard.funding` for the card line (OD-F5, section 4.12).

## 3. Global constraints

- **Evidence hierarchy.** Every number the simulation uses carries a `Provenance` with its tier and source, and the interface can show both. A value that cannot be sourced is `Unsourced` with a reason; what depends on it is unavailable, or runs on a value labelled "custom, not sourced" that the user typed. No tier 3 or 4 number is presented as data.
- **Money never decides alone.** A funded project needs a lot the user's zoning makes hostable and free, takes its size from the same draw, and passes through the same stage tables as every other project. Only a fully funded project is exempt from drop-out (R6).
- **Deterministic and paired.** A run is a pure function of (dataset, levers, seed). The plan and the twin share draws: no draw purpose changes from what the paired-draw fix leaves (ruling R17; F11), and a lot's draws do not depend on the order lots are processed in. That fix draws a stage step's kind and its time apart, so a change to timing alone never changes how a project leaves a stage. This package keeps that property for every stage step: nothing in it draws a kind and a time from one number, and a fully funded project's time in a stage comes from the stage's time draw (F9 (i)). Through money, a timer can still change which applications are awarded and when, because releases and repayments fall on exit and completion days (F9 (g), (h)). That is an effect of the money, not of the sampler.
- **No public money means no change.** With `finance` absent, the engine's output is identical, schedule for schedule, to its output before `finance-engine` under the same `market`. A test asserts it. The site's default is not that case: it runs the baseline funding.
- **The engine is in the dataset version.** `src/lib/sim/scenario`, `src/lib/sim/levers`, `src/lib/scoreboard`, `src/lib/timelapse` and `pipeline/` are hashed into the dataset version. The finance model shapes published data too, because the `/lesscars` build step runs the baseline funding: `src/lib/sim/finance/` (the functions and constants of F4 and F8) is added to the hashed paths in `pipeline/lib/version.ts` by `market-baseline`, the first subsystem whose pipeline code imports it. Any change in a hashed path needs a dataset build and upload before signoff ([pipeline.md](../../dev/pipeline.md), "Finishing a branch that changes the dataset"). The page's code stays outside them, in `src/lib/sim-page/`. `PATHS` in `pipeline/lib/version.ts` is the full list of hashed paths. A path added to it is also added to the dataset rule in `chreode.config.ts` (what signoff runs) and to the path filters of `.github/workflows/pipeline.yml`.
- **Goldens change only at the two releases.** The pipeline fixture goldens, the lesscars fixture goldens and `fixture-0` are regenerated by their documented commands for Release 1 (`market-baseline` with `finance-data`) and for Release 2 (`finance-engine`, when the default starts running the baseline funding), each reviewed as an engine or data change. Between the two, and outside this package, the paired-draw fix regenerates them once, together with `golden-seed1.json` and `model-golden.json` (ruling R17). The fixture build emits a fixture market model and fixture baseline funding, labelled tier 4, so the page and its e2e tests have both. `golden-seed1.json` and `model-golden.json` (the mini city and the small calibration, which have no market model and no funding) do not change in this package: Release 2 leaves them as the paired-draw fix wrote them. They are the lock on "no public money means no change".
- **Open data only.** The dataset stays ODbL and PDDL. New sources go in the lock and the attribution list.
- **Worker-safe and client-side.** The simulation stays in the worker; nothing here adds a server computation or a binding. Shared policies stay in the `plan` table's `body`: no migration.
- **Stored documents keep loading.** A schema-2 policy, saved locally or published, opens with default funding. It is never treated as corrupt.
- **Constant dollars.** Every dollar figure is in the dataset year's dollars except bond repayment, which is nominal and labelled.
- **House rules.** Svelte 5 runes, Tailwind 4, colocated Vitest with `expect.requireAssertions`, Playwright e2e, WCAG 2.2 AA, compact results with detail on demand, status apart from controls, Conventional Commits, work in worktrees, signoff as the only merge gate.

## 4. Behaviour

### 4.1 Where it runs

`runEngine` (`engine.ts:1065-1071`) keeps its three passes. The simulated pass branches once: with `input.finance` absent it runs today's `simulatedPass` (`engine.ts:994-1048`) unchanged, so a run without finance is the run before this subsystem under the same `market`, schedule for schedule (global constraint "No public money means no change"). With `finance` present it runs `fundedPass`, which has three phases:

1. **Eager phase**, lot by lot, in any order: each candidate's attempts are walked as today while its ordinary start comes before every funded application of the attempt. Those attempts produce exactly today's schedules and are handed to the sink at once. The first attempt whose earliest funded application is on or before its ordinary start "parks" the lot: its state goes to the day loop.
2. **Day loop**, in day order from `asOf` to the horizon: inflows, project money events, re-pricing, new applications, service of the line, ordinary starts of parked lots, and the next attempts of parked lots after an exit.
3. **Close**: reservation reports for parked reserved lots, and `reservationsOff` as today (`engine.ts:1047`, `1051-1059`).

Pipeline rows and multi-phase masters run exactly as today (rule (l)); their passes come first and fill `Holds`, as today.

Missing data is not an engine error; `buildSimInput` handles it (section 4.14).

### 4.3 Time conventions

- **A programme is active on day d** when `from ≤ d` and (`to` is null or `d ≤ to`): `to` is inclusive, as an inflow's `to` is ("up to `to`", F5). A programme whose `from` is before `asOf` is active from the first simulated day.
- **A programme is offered on day d** when it is active on d and its pool is not off on d (F9 (b)). Only offered programmes enter a bundle. Switching a pool off never splits or renames a programme (F7): its programmes simply are not offered while it is off.
- **A month's bundle** is the bundle of its first day, `start(T)`. Every rule that varies by month (view, market step, offered programmes) uses the month's first day, as regimes do today (`regimeMonths`, `input.ts:162-164`) and as F3 now states for market steps.
- **Segments.** For one attempt, the months from `r` split into segments at every day in: the regime days, the market step days (F3), every programme's `from` and `to + 1`, and every `off` entry's `from` and `to + 1`. A day b becomes the month bound `lastInitiationMonth(r, b − 1)` (the number of months that start before b), exactly as `regimeMonths` does. Within a segment the view, the market step and the offered programme set are constant.
- **Arrivals.** `inflowArrivals(inflow, asOf, horizon)`: a `once` inflow arrives on its `day`; a `yearly` inflow arrives on `from` and on the same UTC month and day of each later year (29 February becomes 28 February in a common year), up to `to` inclusive. Only arrivals with `asOf ≤ day ≤ horizon` are received. An arrival on `asOf` is received on `asOf` (the baseline's first inflow is on `asOf`, `finance-data` section "First inflow on `asOf`"). An arrival before `asOf` never arrives, which is what `finance-levers` does on its path (its section 4.6), so the base input built by `buildSimInput` and a compiled plan agree. One implementation serves both subsystems (F5).
- **An arrival on a day its pool is off is not received** (no `in` event, `received` unchanged). F7 says of a pool at scale 0 that "nothing arrives"; `finance-levers` already scales those arrivals to 0 dollars (F17), so on a compiled plan this rule changes nothing, but it makes `off` mean the same thing whatever builds the input (a test input, the twin, a later caller).
- **The award day** is the day the line serves the application; the project's first stage is entered that day.
- **Money days**: commit on the award day; spend on `entered.construction`; repay or release at completion on the completion day; release or loss on the exit day. Nothing after the horizon is emitted; a project open at the horizon keeps its commitments, money out and reserves.

### 4.4 The latents of one attempt

For lot i, attempt k, risk start r, existing units e (0 once the lot has been demolished in the run, as today), stream s = `streamOf(sh, entity, k)`:

- `uO = drawOn(s, P.INIT, 0)`, `vO = drawOn(s, P.INIT_DAY, 0)`: the ordinary draws, unchanged.
- `uA = drawOn(s, P.INIT_AFFORDABLE, 0)`, `vA = drawOn(s, P.INIT_DAY_AFFORDABLE, 0)`: the affordable kind's draws (F11). `INIT_AFFORDABLE = 7` and `INIT_DAY_AFFORDABLE = 10` are the two values the paired-draw fix reserves for this subsystem in `P` (`rng.ts`; `P` uses 1 to 6, 8, 9, 11 and 12, and the retired 17 to 23 stay unused), so no existing draw moves.
- `uS = drawOn(s, P.SIZE, 0)`: the size draw, made first (F9 (a)), the same draw the ordinary start uses (`engine.ts:930`).

**Size per view.** In view v (0 = `input.parcels`, j = `regimes[j − 1]`), the size level is the first f with `uS < cum_v[f]`, where `cum_v` is the view's size row against e (`sizeCum`, or `sizeCumAgainst` after demolition, `engine.ts:926-933`); none when the row is all 0. The units are `yield_v[i·F + f]`, the stories `fillStories(FILL_LEVELS[f], envelopeStories_v[i])`, the prototype `prototypeFor(stories, units)`. This is the size the ordinary start would draw in that view.

**Bundle per segment and kind.** In segment j with view v, for kind κ: the programmes of `finance.programmes`, in that order, with `reach = κ`, offered on the segment's first month day (active, and the pool not off), and `minHomes ≤ units_v`. If v has no size, or the lot has hazard 0 in v (not a candidate of that view), the bundle is empty. A non-empty bundle is priced once with `fundBundle(items, κ, prototype, terms)`, memoised per (kind, programme set, prototype id) per input (`finance-model` section 4.5, "Cost and memoising").

**Why the memo by set is sound.** Under F8's tier rule (pro rata within a tier) the exact result of `fundBundle` depends only on the set of items; in floating point it can also depend, by an ulp, on the order in which a tier's shares are summed. The engine always passes items in `finance.programmes` order (here, in section 4.6's leave-out rounds, and when re-pricing), so a set of programmes determines its order and the memo key may be the set, bit for bit: a bitset over programme indices (at most 24 added plus the baseline's programmes, F7 and F12, so two 32-bit words suffice for any input today; the key is a string of the indices if more are ever allowed). For the `affordable` kind the prototype does not change the price (`uncoveredPerHome` and `costPerHome` are the `AFFORDABLE` constants, and so is `ownValuePerHome`, F4 and F8), so the affordable memo ignores the prototype. The memo is per input, so the plan and the twin keep separate ones.

**Rates per segment.** With `z` the lot's log-odds in view v (`Precompute.logit`) and `m_j` the market step's log-odds:

- Ordinary: `log(1 − logistic(z + m_j))`: F3's rate, computed by `market-baseline`'s code.
- Kind `market`: `x = z + m_j + (bundle non-empty ? oddsShift(g, 'market', p, slope) : 0)`.
- Kind `affordable`: `x = z + m_j − slope × (uncoveredPerHome('affordable', p) − uncoveredPerHome('market', p)) ÷ costPerHome('affordable', p) + (bundle non-empty ? oddsShift(g, 'affordable', p, slope) : 0)`, the unshifted affordable log-odds of F9 (a) plus the bundle's shift.
- Funded rate: `−Infinity` when the bundle is certain (hazard 1), else `log(1 − logistic(x))`; 0 (no start) where the view has no size or the lot's hazard is 0.

`logistic(x) = 1 ÷ (1 + exp(−x))` with `math.ts` `exp`, as `initiationHazard` (`model.ts:394-398`).

**Latent months and days.** `T_κ = piecewiseMonth(log(1 − u), bounds, rates_κ)` (`input.ts:174-194`), with `u = uO` for `market` (F9 (a): the market kind's funded start uses the ordinary start's draws) and `u = uA` for `affordable`. `piecewiseMonth` already handles a certain segment exactly: with rate `−Infinity` it returns the segment's first month (`ceil(finite ÷ −Infinity)` is −0, so `max(1, −0)` is 1). The day is:

- in a certain month: `start(T)`, the month's first day. A certain start's timing is set by money, not by a draw; placing it on the first day keeps every certain application of a cohort on one day, so rule (d)'s second key, not a uniform, orders them (the OD-F9b site rule).
- otherwise: `r + 1 + floor((T − 1 + v) × MONTH_DAYS)` with `v = vO` (market) or `vA` (affordable), as `initiationDay`. So a market funded start in the same month as the ordinary start falls on the same day.

The attempt has an **application of kind κ** on that day when T ≠ 0, the day is on or before the horizon, and the month's bundle is non-empty. A latent start that falls in a month with an empty bundle makes no application: money that is not on offer that month moves nothing. Because the market kind's funded log-survival rate is at or below the ordinary one in every segment (its shift is ≥ 0, and 0 where the bundle is empty) and it uses the same draw, its month is never later than the ordinary start's (F9 (a)).

**The ordinary start** of the attempt is today's: `T_O` from `uO` at the ordinary rates (with today's `skipAbove` shortcut on attempt 0 of an unheld lot), its day from `vO`, cut by the horizon and by a kept reservation as today.

**The line key** of an application (rule (d)), compared in this order:

1. `applied`: its application day (section 4.11 keeps it across re-pricing).
2. `keyDay`: the first day of "the latent start month the lot's own draw for that kind gives at the lot's unshifted hazard": `start(T_ref)` with `T_ref = piecewiseMonth(log(1 − u), bounds, rates_ref)`, where `u` is the kind's draw (`uO` for market, `uA` for affordable) and `rates_ref` are the kind's rates with no bundle shift and no certainty (for market: the ordinary rates; for affordable: the unshifted affordable rates), not cut by the horizon. `T_ref = 0` (never) and any day after 2^28 compare equal, after every other day (a numerical bound, tier 4: day 2^28 is about 735,000 years out). The first day of the month, not the month count, is used because lots at risk from different days count months from different `r` (F9 (d)). A new spell after a lapse (section 4.11) computes its `keyDay` afresh from its own risk start, as it does its `applied`.
3. The parcel id, by `parcelRankOf`.
4. The kind, `market` before `affordable` (F1's order), for a lot with two applications on equal keys.

For the market kind, `keyDay` is the ordinary start's month: market applicants are served in the order they would have started without money. For the affordable kind it is the lot's site odds on its own draw, the rule OD-F9b rules: a funded project's site is drawn the way a private project's is, on a separate draw.

### 4.5 The eager phase and parking

For each candidate, in candidate order (the order does not matter; section 4.16):

1. Holds and reservations exactly as today (`engine.ts:1003-1021`): a blocked lot is skipped; a held lot is at risk from its last exit, demolished if a holder built.
2. For attempt k: compute the ordinary day `O` (or none) and each reached kind's application day `A_κ` (or none).
3. If no application day is on or before `O` (none, or all later, with "no O" meaning after every day), run the attempt as today: `simulatedProject` on `O`, hand the schedule to the sink, update the reservation and demolition state, and on an exit go to attempt k + 1 from the exit day. This produces exactly today's schedule for that attempt.
4. Otherwise the lot **parks**: its state (k, r, e, demolished, reservation day, the attempt's draws, `O`, each `A_κ`, the sizes per view) is handed to the day loop, and nothing more is done for it here.

Cost note: on attempt 0 of an unheld lot whose first INIT draw is above `skipAbove`, `O` is none without further work, as today. Its funded latents are evaluated only if the lot can be in some bundle (section 4.17, the eligibility list); otherwise the lot is passed over on the one draw, as today.

### 4.6 The line

The line holds applications. Each has: its lot, its kind, its key (section 4.4), its current view, size and prototype, its current bundle B (the programmes offered to it when it was last made or re-made, in `finance.programmes` order), B's price `fundBundle(B)` and coverage level `level(B)`, and whether it is certain (`level(B) = 1`).

**Asks.** For a set S of programmes, priced per home by `fundBundle(S, κ, p, terms)`, and `units` the application's size: for each item, `grant = projectDollars(item.granted, units)`, `loan = projectDollars(item.lent, units)`, `reserve = projectDollars(item.reserve, units)`; S **asks** each pool for the sum, over S's items on that pool, of `grant + loan + reserve`. These are the whole project's dollars at its start size (F9 (c)). A capped pool q is **short for S** when `available(q) < ask_S(q)`. A pool S asks nothing of (its grants pay 0 because earlier tiers already met the uncovered cost, F8) is never short, so a programme that would pay nothing never stops a project.

**The award rule** (F9 (c); `awardSet`). For an application with bundle B:

1. S₀ = B.
2. Round n (n = 1, 2, …): price S₍ₙ₋₁₎ and find the pools short for it. If none is short, stop with S = S₍ₙ₋₁₎. Otherwise S₍ₙ₎ is S₍ₙ₋₁₎ less **every programme of every short pool**, all at once; if S₍ₙ₎ is empty, stop with S empty. Then the next round.
3. The application is **awarded with S** when S is not empty and `level(S)` is not below `level(B)` (definitions: `level(S) ≥ level(B) − 1e-9`). Otherwise it waits, and nothing is taken from any pool.

Decisions and reasons, all externally visible:

- **All short pools leave in the same round, and all their programmes with them.** The row says the programmes "whose pools cannot pay their part" are left out; a pool's part is what the whole set asks of it, so it is the pool, not a single programme, that is short. Dropping every short pool at once makes a round a function of the set and the money alone, with no order among pools to choose, so the result does not depend on the order of `finance.pools` or `finance.programmes` at all (F8's tiers are pro rata within a tier, so `fundBundle` has no order among grants either).
- **A pool left out stays out for the rest of that test.** Leaving out a grant raises what remains for the later tiers, and, when its own tier was oversubscribed, the fraction every other grant of its tier pays (F8: pro rata within a tier). So a pool that could pay in round n may be short in round n + 1, from either cause; that is why the row says "repeatedly". No pool is ever added back.
- **It ends.** Each round that does not stop removes at least one pool (a short pool is asked something, so it has a programme in the set), and pools are never added back, so there are at most as many rounds after the first as there are distinct capped pools in B; in the baseline that is 7 (six City pools and the state pool, F12), the uncapped federal pool never being short.
- **Not below, with the tolerance.** `level` uses `isCertain`, so a certain application can be awarded only with a certain set: it never starts as a partly covered project. A partly covered one may be awarded with a set whose coverage is equal within 1e-9 (in practice the same set, or B less programmes that paid nothing) or higher. Coverage above 1 (through loans and guarantees) counts as 1, as the row says, so leaving out a loan that only pushed coverage past 1 does not stop the award.
- **An empty set is never awarded**, even when B's coverage is 0 (a loan at or above the market rate, F8): a project with no programme left has no funding to start on, and its lot keeps its ordinary start.
- **Everything about the award comes from S's price**: the commitments and the ledger (section 4.7), `funding.programmes`, the dollars and certainty (section 4.12), and the stage rule (section 4.9: the project is certain exactly when `level(S) = 1`, which, by the rule above, is exactly when B was certain). Pools outside S are not touched.

Examples (test cases, section 6, with the design's baseline dollars per home: federal 443,108, state 118,390, City 300,027 of 861,525, F12 and design section 3; tier 1 figures; the City's six pools are folded into one here for brevity; the arithmetic is F8's tier rule, exact before `projectDollars`):

- **A bond in the City tier.** Federal and state leave 300,027 for the City tier. An added `goBond` grant of share 0.5 asks 430,762.5, so the tier's grants (730,789.5) exceed what remains and each pays the fraction 300,027 ÷ 730,789.5 ≈ 0.4106 of its share: the City grant 123,176.65 a home and the bond 176,850.35. Coverage stays 1, so the application stays certain; each home now takes less City money, and the City tier's dollars per home stay 300,027.
- **City short, bond share 0.5.** The City programme leaves; the bond, alone in its tier, pays `min(430,762.5, 300,027)` = 300,027 (the tier covers exactly what remains): coverage 1, the project is awarded, certain, and the bond pool commits 300,027 a home.
- **City short, bond share 0.2.** The bond alone pays its full share, 172,305; S covers (443,108 + 118,390 + 172,305) ÷ 861,525 ≈ 0.852, below 1: the project waits, and nothing is taken.
- **Bond short.** The bond leaves; the City grant, alone in the tier, pays its full share again: awarded, certain.

**Monotone in money and in size.** Within one pass the pools only lose money, and a larger project asks more. The award test is monotone in both: if it fails with available money a, it fails with any a′ ≤ a (pool by pool), and if it fails at `units` it fails at any larger size with the same bundle and prototype. Proof sketch, resting on two properties `finance-model` states and tests for `fundBundle` under F8's tier rule (its section 4.5, "The engine's award loop", properties (ii) and (iii)), each applied with any set in the role of "the whole bundle": (ii) leaving an item out never lowers any remaining grant's `granted`; and a loan's `lent`, a guarantee's `guaranteed` and its reserve do not depend on the set (F8: they count in full). So every pool left in a smaller set is asked as much or more. Hence every pool that is short in some round under a is still short, at the end, under a′, and the set left under a′ is a subset of the set left under a. (iii) a subset's `grantEquivalent` is never above the set's, so its coverage, and so its level, is not higher. Termination does not depend on the pricing rule at all: it rests only on pools leaving and never returning. The same argument holds for size, since `projectDollars` is non-decreasing in the number of homes. (Exact in real arithmetic; in floating point a difference of one ulp could move a `Math.round` by a dollar. The serving rule below is defined as a single scan, so no award depends on the monotonicity holding to the dollar; only the equivalence of the two implementations and the per-group threshold of section 4.17 use it, and the property test of section 6 checks them against the single scan.)

**Serving** (F9 (c), (d)): the line is scanned once in key order, and each application is tested by the award rule against the money available at that moment; one that passes is awarded at once with its set S, and its asks reduce the pools before the next is tested. Uncapped pools always pay (F9 (f)). An application that fails is skipped and stays in line; a later, smaller one may be awarded in the same pass, and a later one whose bundle leans on other pools may be awarded too. This is the design's "taking each project its pools can pay". The single scan is the definition; by the monotonicity above, an application skipped in a pass would also fail later in the same pass, so "repeatedly award the first application in key order that passes now" gives the same awards, and the implementation may use either (section 4.17). A pass ends at the end of the line; it is run again only when money returns or arrives (section 4.8).

**When the line is served:** on each day of the loop that has an inflow, a money event, a re-pricing or a new application, after those (section 4.8). A new application that passes is therefore awarded on its application day; a programme that opens on a day with no pool change still reaches the waiting line that day, because its applications are re-made then (F9 (d): the line is served on each day a pool changes, or an application is made or re-made); and the waiting applications, which failed after the last change of money, are not passed over unfairly: nothing they could have used has arrived since.

**Known limit (design, "Out of scope").** An application waits for a pool that is short even when no more money is due to it: a spent pool with no later inflow still counts as short every day, and if the other programmes cannot make up its share, the application waits until the horizon, until its programme ends or its pool is switched off (a re-pricing that empties or shrinks its bundle, section 4.11), or until its lot starts otherwise. The engine does not look ahead at inflows to retire such a programme; the results show each pool's balance, and the user ends the programme to release its applicants. A test pins this behaviour (section 6), so a later change to it is deliberate.

**Leaving the line:** an application leaves the line when it is awarded; when its lot starts by any route (its other kind's award or its ordinary start: rule (e)); when it lapses (section 4.11); and at the horizon (it simply never starts).

### 4.7 Pools and the ledger

**Pool state** (per pool, whole dollars): `received` (Σ `in`), `committed` (committed and not yet spent or released), `spentGrant` (grants paid), `outLoan` (loan principal outstanding), `lostLoan`, `lostGuarantee`.

**Available money** of a capped pool:

`available = leverage × received − committed − spentGrant − outLoan − lostLoan − lostGuarantee`

This is F9 (f) term for term: `spent` is `spentGrant`, `out` is `outLoan`, `lost` is `lostLoan + lostGuarantee`, and `committed` includes the guarantee reserves held. It is also the identity `finance-score` asserts. For a `bank` pool, `leverage` caps commitments plus loans outstanding per dollar received (F5). For a grant from a bank pool this is conservative (a grant removes a whole dollar of headroom, not `leverage` of one). Uncapped pools are never tested; their state is still kept and their events emitted. A pool that is off keeps its state: what is committed stays committed and is spent, repaid, released or lost as scheduled, and what is left stays unspent (F7); it simply is in no bundle, so nothing new is committed from it.

**Events** (F9 (g), (h); the protocol `finance-score` section 4.4 builds on). For one awarded project with units u, the items of its awarded set S priced as in section 4.6, and λ = `terms.lossOnExit`:

| Moment | Grant item (dollars G) | Loan item (principal P) | Guarantee item (reserve H, guaranteed principal Q = `projectDollars(item.guaranteed, u)`) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Award day | `commit` G | `commit` P | `commit` H |
| `entered.construction` | `spend` G (committed → spentGrant) | `spend` P (committed → outLoan) | nothing |
| Exit before construction | `release` G | `release` P | `release` H |
| Completion | nothing | `repay` P (outLoan −P) | `release` H |
| Exit after construction began | `loss` round(λG) (a mark on money already spent: `spentGrant` unchanged) | `loss` round(λP) and `repay` P − round(λP) (outLoan −P, lostLoan + round(λP)) | `loss` X = min(round(λQ), H) (lostGuarantee + X) and `release` H |
| Open at the horizon | stays committed or spent | stays committed or out | stays held |

"Exit after construction began" means the schedule ends in an exit and `entered.construction` is defined (the construction entry is on or before the exit day). A guarantee's loss is `lossOnExit` of the guaranteed principal, at most its reserve, as F9 (h) now says: the reserve is the pool's exposure, so a guarantee pool never pays more than it set aside, and section 4.7's availability never falls below 0 through a loss. With λ = 1 (tier 4, OD-F8, `finance-model` section 4.3) a guarantee loses its whole reserve.

Events of zero dollars are not emitted. Rounding is `Math.round`, as `projectDollars`.

**Inflows.** Each arrival of a capped pool on a day the pool is not off emits `in` with its dollars and `programme: null` (0-dollar arrivals emit nothing) and adds to `received` (section 4.3).

**One event per programme.** The ledger event is emitted per programme of the awarded set, not merged per pool, so a pool that serves a grant programme and a loan programme reports each, and every event other than `in` carries its `programme` id (F10). Only the programmes of S are in the ledger: a programme of B left out at the award has no event, and neither has a programme of S whose dollars at the start size all round to 0 (events of zero dollars are not emitted; section 4.12 says when such a programme is still listed).

**Order within a day** (F10: "called in day order"; within a day this order is fixed so a run's ledger is a pure function of its input):

1. `in` events, by pool in `finance.pools` order, then arrival order.
2. Money events of already-awarded projects dated this day, by award order (the order in which projects were awarded in the run), and within one project: `spend`, `repay`, `loss`, `release`, each by programme in `finance.programmes` order.
3. `commit` events of the day's awards, in award order, by programme in `finance.programmes` order.
4. Steps 2 and 3 repeat while the day's awards create money events on the same day (a project whose stages take 0 days), and a release on the day re-serves the line (section 4.8).

**Ledger identities** (this subsystem owns the ledger, so it states them; `finance-score` cites them and keeps its own numbers I1 to I6 beside these). For each pool, summing its events from `asOf` to any event inclusive, with `C`, `R`, `Sg`, `Sl`, `P` (repaid), `Lg`, `Ll`, `Lh` the sums of `commit`, `release`, `spend` on grants, `spend` on loans, `repay`, `loss` on grants, on loans and on guarantees (the programme's instrument says which):

- E1 (`finance-score` I1). Every cumulative sum never decreases; the pool state's `committed ≥ 0` and `outLoan ≥ 0`.
- E2 (I2). `C = R + Sg + Sl + committed`: every committed dollar is still committed, released or spent; guarantee reserves are committed and released, never spent.
- E3 (I3). `Sl = outLoan + P + Ll`: every lent dollar is outstanding, repaid or lost.
- E4 (I4). For a capped pool, `available = leverage × received − committed − Sg − outLoan − Ll − Lh ≥ 0`, F9 (f)'s formula recomputed from the ledger, equal to the engine's internal value after every event.
- E5 (I6). `Lg ≤ Sg`: a grant's loss is a mark on money already spent, never more than was paid; and `Lh` over one project's guarantee never exceeds its reserve (F9 (h)).
- E6 (I5). An uncapped pool has no `in` event.
- E7 (no counterpart). Per project and programme: Σ `commit` = Σ `release` + Σ `spend` + still committed; for each loan that ended, `spend` = `repay` + `loss`; for each guarantee that ended, `release` = `commit`.

The ledger sink is optional: with `sink.ledger` absent the engine keeps the pool state but builds no event objects, and its decisions are identical (section 4.15).

### 4.8 The day loop

Days with nothing scheduled are skipped. On each day D, in this order:

1. **Inflows** of day D (section 4.7).
2. **Money events** of awarded projects dated D (section 4.7). They change `available`.
3. **Re-pricing and lapse** on a re-pricing day (section 4.11) and **reservation lapses** for lots whose kept reservation day is D.
4. **New applications** of day D join the line: those of parked lots and of lots whose new attempt began after an exit.
5. **Serve the line** (section 4.6). Each award builds its project (section 4.9), emits its commits and schedules its money events; any dated D are processed now (back to step 2 for those only), and if any of them released money the line is served again. This repeats until a pass awards nothing.
6. **Ordinary starts** dated D of parked lots that have not started: the lot leaves every line and its ordinary project runs as today (`simulatedProject`), in parcel-rank order. So when a lot's funded application and its ordinary start fall on one day, the application is served first (rule (c): the funded start starts at once if its pools can pay; otherwise the ordinary start takes the lot that day).
7. For each lot whose project (funded or ordinary) was decided this day and exits on some later day x, its attempt k + 1 is computed from r = x when day x is reached (its events land on days after x, because every latent day is at least `r + 1`).

All sorting within a step uses the line key or the parcel rank, never the order in which lots were met, so the loop is a pure function of the input and the seed.

### 4.9 The award: the funded project

On award day D, lot i, attempt k, kind κ, with the application's current view v, size level f, units and awarded set S (section 4.6). The project is **certain** when `level(S) = 1`, which the award rule allows exactly when the application's bundle B was certain:

- **Stream and id**: the attempt's stream s, so every later draw (pathway, pre-application, stages, revisions) is the draw the ordinary start of that attempt would have used: only one project starts per attempt. `projectId` is `sim:<parcel id>:<k>`, as today (`engine.ts:954`).
- **Pathway**: a Local Program lot in view v takes `streamlined`, as today (`engine.ts:919`): the route is the lot's law. Otherwise a **certain affordable** project draws its pathway from `affordableRoutes` (F12) by `drawOn(s, P.PATHWAY, 0)` over `PATHWAYS` in order, with exactly `newPathway`'s arithmetic (`engine.ts:851-866`) on those shares (F9 (j); ruling R6: the records of 100% affordable projects differ, so they are followed). Every other funded project draws from `pathwayForNew` as today. `affordableRoutes` is read from the run's new-project calibration, as `pathwayForNew` is (`engine.ts:918-919`).
- **First stage**: `preApplication` when `drawOn(s, P.PPA, 0) < ppaShare[pathway]`, else `permitFiled` for `permitOnly` and `filed` otherwise, as today (`engine.ts:920-925`). The records give no separate pre-application share for 100% affordable projects; the existing one applies.
- **Size**: `revisions[0] = { day: D, units, stories }` of view v. `existingUnits` is e.
- **Stages, partly covered**: `runStages` as today, with the simulated revision rule. Its commitments stay at the start size (F9 (i)).
- **Stages, certain**: `job.certain = true`. A stage entered on day e is left on day `e + floor(x × sampleAdvance(t, drawOn(s, P.STAGE_TIME, STAGE_INDEX[stage])).days)`. Here t is the transition `stageStep` reads: the job's pathway and stage in the run's new-project calibration, which is unscaled since the paired-draw fix. x is the job's timer scale for the stage, read as `stageStep` reads it (`job.scale`, which is the run's `newScale` for a simulated project; 1 for a stage it does not name). The outcome is always `advance`, and `P.STAGE`, the kind draw, is not read. This is `scaledTransition` with the kind fixed to advance. So a certain project spends the same days in a stage as an uncertain project with the same stream, pathway, stage and scale that advances, and a timer scales a certain project's stages as any other's ("it takes the time the stages take", R6). Revisions are off (rule NONE). A certain project never exits; it completes, or is open at the horizon.
- **Schedule fields**: `kind: 'affordable'` for κ = affordable (absent for market); `funding` as section 4.12; `regime: v` on a run with regimes, as today.
- **Lot state**: the lot leaves every line; `demolished` is set if the project enters construction; a reservation is voided by the same rule as today (a project begun before the reservation day that does not exit before it, `engine.ts:1039-1040`).

### 4.10 After a funded project ends

A funded project that exits on day x frees the lot: attempt k + 1 begins with r = x on stream `(entity, k + 1)`, exactly as after an ordinary exit (`engine.ts:1042-1043`). Its ordinary start uses INIT and INIT_DAY on the new stream, and its affordable latent uses INIT_AFFORDABLE and INIT_DAY_AFFORDABLE on the new stream; its market latent shares the ordinary draws. A completed or open project ends the lot, as today. A lot that exits and is at risk again is therefore drawn afresh, with the same rules, whether its last project was funded or not.

### 4.11 Re-pricing, lapse and reservations

**Re-pricing days** are the days after `asOf`, up to the horizon, on which some application's bundle or size could change while it waits (F9 (b)): every programme's `from` and `to + 1`, every `off` entry's `from` and `to + 1` (a pool going off or on), and every regime day. On a re-pricing day D every waiting application is re-made under the rules of day D: its view is the view in force on D, its size is the attempt's size draw in that view, its bundle B′ the programmes of its kind offered on D (active, pool not off) whose `minHomes` it meets, priced by `fundBundle`. **Unless it lapses, it keeps its place in line** (its `applied` and `keyDay` do not change), and B′ becomes its bundle.

Reasons. Without re-pricing, an application made under the baseline on the first day would never see a programme the user adds from 2028, and with certain baseline money every eligible lot applies on the first day: the user's money would reach almost no one. With re-pricing that also reset the place in line, every programme date would reshuffle the queue. A waiting project's terms are otherwise not revised: the prototypes and terms do not change during a run, so its price changes only with its bundle or its size.

**Lapse** (F9 (b)). An application lapses on a re-pricing day when any of these holds:

1. its re-made bundle B′ is empty;
2. its lot has no size or a 0 hazard in the view in force;
3. `level(B′)` is below `level(B)`, B being its bundle just before D (as made or last re-made): its offer shrank. Example: a certain application whose state pool is switched off from D goes from level 1 to about 0.863 (the federal and City shares, (443,108 + 300,027) ÷ 861,525; the dollars are tier 1 and the shares tier 2, F12) and lapses.

An application whose level stays (within 1e-9) or rises keeps its place, even if its dollars or its programmes change (an added City bond on a certain baseline lowers what every City grant pays, pro rata, but the level stays 1, so it changes nothing about its place). The comparison is with the bundle just before D, not with the first one: an application whose offer rose on one re-pricing day and falls back on a later one has shrunk on that later day, and lapses.

A lapsed application leaves the line, and that kind's latent start is computed again as a new spell from D: months start on D (r′ = D − 1), with the same draws (`u`, `v`), the rates from D on (at the shifted odds of the bundles from D, so a project that was certain may now start at lower odds), and a fresh `keyDay` (section 4.4). If that gives an application, it is new (its `applied` is its own day). Reusing the draws keeps the run a pure function of the seed with no new purpose (F11 lists only two). A lapse on day D can give a new application on D itself, when the bundle of the month starting on D is certain; it then joins the line behind every application with an earlier `applied`.

**Reservations** (F9 (k)). A lot reserved from day d (`reservedFrom`) keeps today's rule, extended to applications: no application is made on or after d while the reservation is kept, no award happens on or after d, and on day d every waiting application of the lot lapses for good (no new spell). An award before d voids the reservation unless its project exits before d, as an ordinary start does. Each reserved row is reported once to `sink.reservation`, as today.

**Regimes** (F9 (k)): the latents use each month's view (section 4.4); a waiting application is re-made on each regime day; a funded project runs on the view of its award day (which, by re-pricing, is the view its size was drawn in). This is the existing rule "a project keeps the regime of the month it initiated in" (`types.ts:76-83`), with the award as the initiation.

### 4.12 `Schedule.kind`, `Schedule.funding` and `displaced`

Both fields appear only on simulated schedules of a run with `finance`, and only on funded projects; an ordinary start in such a run has neither, so its schedule is shaped exactly as today.

- `kind`: `'affordable'` when the funded start is of kind affordable; absent for a market funded start (F10: absent means market).
- `funding.programmes`: the ids of the programmes that pay the project (F10): those of the awarded set S whose exact per-home `granted`, `lent` or `guaranteed` (from `fundBundle`) is above 0, in `finance.programmes` order (baseline first). A programme of B left out at the award, and a grant of S that pays 0 because earlier tiers already met the uncovered cost (F8), are not listed. The exact per-home amount decides, not the rounded project dollars: under pro rata a grant that pays a few cents a home still takes its part of its tier, and dropping it from the list would let a re-pricing give the other grants of its tier more (below). Such a programme can therefore be listed with 0 project dollars and no ledger event; that needs a share below about one dollar over the project's homes, so it arises only from a typed share, never from the baseline.
- `funding.applied`: the application's `applied` day (the day of its current spell; a lapse starts a new one).
- `funding.certain`: whether the programmes fully fund the project (F10), that is `level(S) = 1` (`isCertain` of S's coverage, F8); exactly the flag that makes the project's stages certain (section 4.9). By the award rule it is true exactly when the application's bundle B was certain.
- `funding.granted`, `lent`, `guaranteed`: Σ over the items of S of `projectDollars(item.granted, u)`, `projectDollars(item.lent, u)` and `projectDollars(item.guaranteed, u)` (the guaranteed private principal, not the reserve), whole dollars, at the start size.
- **Re-pricing `funding.programmes` gives the same dollars.** `finance-score` (F13, tiers weighted by what each programme pays) prices `funding.programmes` again with `fundBundle`. That gives exactly S's per-item amounts for the listed programmes: under F8's tier rule a grant (share above 0) pays exactly 0 only when nothing remains for its tier, and then every grant of its tier and of later tiers pays 0 too, so dropping it changes no other item (R stays 0 for its tier, and earlier tiers do not see it); loans and guarantees do not depend on the set. The listed programmes keep `finance.programmes` order, so the floating-point sums are the same too. The prototype is `prototypeFor(revisions[0].stories, revisions[0].units)`, the start size the engine priced (a simulated schedule's `revisions[0].stories` is never null: it is `fillStories(…)`, `engine.ts:944`). The engine's tests assert this equality (section 6).
- `funding.displaced`: the homes of the ordinary start this funded start pre-empted, exactly: if the attempt's ordinary start `O` exists, is on or before the horizon, is before a kept reservation day, and its size draw in the view of `O`'s month gives a level, then that level's units minus the attempt's existing units e; otherwise 0. `O` is the ordinary day the attempt had when the award was made; it is on or after the award day (an earlier `O` would have started the lot). A market funded start whose ordinary start would have come before the horizon displaces its own counterpart: those homes would have been built without the money.

**`ProjectCard.kind` and `ProjectCard.funding`** (F10). `projectCard` (`card.ts:140-246`) adds two fields to the card it returns, for simulated schedules only:

- `kind`: `s.kind` when it is `'affordable'`; absent otherwise.
- `funding`: absent when `s.funding` is absent (every pipeline card, every ordinary start, and every card of a run without `finance`). Otherwise `{ programmes: [...s.funding.programmes], certain: s.funding.certain }`, copied from the schedule (F10). The card does no pricing.

### 4.16 Determinism and independence from lot order

- Every per-lot quantity is a pure function of (seed, the lot's entity, k, purpose): draws are counter-based (`rng.ts:57-70`).
- The eager phase treats each lot alone; its output for a lot does not depend on any other lot.
- The day loop orders every choice by day, then a fixed step order, then the line key (applied, keyDay, parcel id, kind) or the parcel rank. None of these is the order in which lots were visited, or a row index, so permuting the rows of `parcels` (with the same ids) gives the same schedules and the same ledger.
- Pool decisions compare whole-dollar asks with `available`, which is one product (`leverage × received`, an integer when `leverage` is 1) less integer sums, so no comparison depends on floating-point summation order.
- The award rule (section 4.6) is a function of the bundle, the size, the prototype and the pools' available money: each round removes every short pool at once, so no order among pools or programmes enters it, and `fundBundle` always receives its items in `finance.programmes` order.

### 4.17 Performance (F18)

**Counts on the bench city** (`synthCity()`, `src/lib/sim/scenario/fixtures/synth-city.ts`; measured for this spec with a scratch script on seeds 1 to 7, tier 0 for the fixture): 150,360 lots, 122,681 candidates; lots whose top level yields at least 10 homes 78,129, at least 25 homes 47,471, at least 50 homes 33,505, at least 100 homes 19,005. `simulateNet` today: 9.1 to 10.5 ms after the first run on the machine used (the existing budget is 15 ms). Parcel ids are not in row order, so the parcel rank is a separate memo.

**What the baseline adds** (minHomes 70 for every baseline programme, `finance-data`'s live value; every affordable bundle certain): every candidate that can reach 70 homes in some view (fewer than 33,505) draws its size; those at 70 or more apply on the first day (inferred about 15,000 to 25,000). Per run:

1. The eligible list (candidates whose largest level in any view meets the smallest `minHomes` of any programme of a reached kind) is per input, memoised with the precompute: no per-seed cost for the others, which stay on today's path.
2. For the market kind, a per-input bound on attempt 0's INIT draw, built as `skipAbove` (`input.ts:206-230`) but from the largest log-odds any market bundle offered in each segment (so with `off` applied) can add to the lot (`−Infinity` rate, so no bound, where some bundle is certain): above it neither the ordinary start nor the market funded start falls before the horizon, and the lot is passed over on the one draw, as today. Exact, like `skipAbove`: every draw at or below the bound takes the exact path.
3. Per eligible lot: one SIZE draw and a size-row scan; per applicant one INIT_AFFORDABLE draw, one `log`, and an `exp` and a `log` per segment for `keyDay`.
4. The first day's batch is sorted once by a 64-bit float composite key (`keyDay` capped at 2^28, the parcel rank below 2^19, the kind bit), which is exact below 2^53. Later days append in key order, since every later application has a later `applied`.
5. The line is kept per group of applications that have the same bundle B, kind and price: (kind, B, prototype id for the market kind; section 4.4 says why the affordable price ignores the prototype). Within a group the award rule depends on the application only through its units, and it is monotone in units (section 4.6), so "passes" is "units ≤ H", where H is the group's largest passing size against the pools' money at that moment. H is found by bisection over whole units from 1 to the group's largest size, each step one run of the award rule (at most ⌈log₂ 4,096⌉ = 12 steps for any size the yields reach; 4,096 is a bound on one lot's homes chosen for the bisection, not a model number). Each group keeps a segment tree of units in key order (removed entries hold +∞), so its first passing application is found in O(log n), and serving takes the earliest among the groups' first passes, awards it, recomputes H for every group that shares a pool with it, and repeats until no group has a passing application. The awarded set of that application is the one the award rule gives at its own units. A re-pricing day rebuilds the groups in key order (O(line)), with the lapse test per application a memo lookup and one comparison; there are at most two re-pricing days per programme, two per `off` entry and one per regime. The single scan of section 4.6 is the definition; this is an implementation of it, and the property test of section 6 compares the two on random inputs.
6. Events: inflows about 7 pools × 25 years = 175 days; awards of the order of the City's money over the per-project City share (234 million dollars a year ÷ (300,027 dollars × tens of homes): of the order of 10 to 30 a year, inferred); ordinary starts of parked lots of the order of their share of today's 8,000 starts.
7. **The award rule's cost.** One run of the award rule is at most 1 + (capped pools in B) rounds (8 for the baseline, section 4.6), each a memoised `fundBundle` lookup by set plus one pass over the set's items (8 for the baseline: six City programmes, the state's and the federal one, F12) to sum the asks and compare them with `available`: under about 100 arithmetic operations, with no `exp` or `log`. In the baseline alone nothing is ever left out to any effect: every grant pays exactly its share, because the City tier's shares fill exactly what federal and state money leave (design section 3; F8's tier rule then pays each in full), and leaving out a City grant only leaves the others further from filling it, so when any pool is short the level falls below 1 and the application waits; the rule ends after one or two rounds. Per serve the cost is the number of groups (a few per programme set: one affordable group, and up to five market groups by prototype) × 12 bisection steps × that bound: about 10 × 12 × 100 = 12,000 operations, times about 200 serving days (the inflow days of item 6 plus the award and release days) is about 2.4 million simple operations a run, of the order of 1 to 3 ms in V8 (inferred, not measured).
8. **Off and on days** are re-pricing days like programme days: each costs O(line) (about 15,000 to 25,000 memo lookups) plus, for the applications that lapse, one new spell each (an `exp` and a `log` per remaining segment). The worst case is a pool that every waiting application uses switched off: the whole line lapses at once, about 20,000 new spells, of the order of 1 to 2 ms (inferred), once per `off` entry.

Estimate (inferred from the operation counts above, not measured): 4 to 11 ms over today for the baseline alone, against a measured 9.1 to 10.5 ms today, so within the 30 ms budget; the bench funding set has no `off` entry and adds three programmes, within the 45 ms budget. ### 4.18 `sampleAdvance`

`sampleAdvance(t: Transition, u: number): { days: number }` in `src/lib/timelapse/model.ts`, beside `sampleTime`. It is the paired-draw fix's time sampler with the kind fixed to advance and no time already spent in the stage:

1. `u` must be in [0, 1), else `RangeError('u must be in [0, 1), got <u>')` (the fix's `checkUniform(u, 'u')`). This is checked first, on every transition.
2. If the transition's advance has no mass, the stage never advances: return `days = ceil(MAX_EVENT_MONTH × MONTH_DAYS) − 1`, the last day `sampleTime` can return. It is beyond any horizon, so the project stays open. "No mass" is the condition under which `sampleTime(t, 'advance', u, 0)` throws (`advance has no mass left at 0`): the eventual advance (the `advance` entry of `inf` in the sampler's per-kind columns, `colsOf`) is not above 0. The function tests that value. It does not call `sampleTime` and catch the error.
3. Otherwise `days = sampleTime(t, 'advance', u, 0)`.

This is the advance column renormalised to the advances (F9 (i)). `sampleTime` inverts G(x) = F(x) ÷ F(∞) for the advance's cumulative curve F, which is linear within table months and carries the advance's share of the geometric tail past the table. The result is the day `sampleTransition(t, uKind, u, 0)` returns whenever its kind is `advance`. `sampleAdvance` takes no months-elapsed argument: certain projects are always new, never in flight.

`model-golden.json` does not change: its test asserts the exact set of functions it covers (`model.golden.shared.ts`), and `sampleAdvance` is not added to it. `sampleAdvance` has no golden of its own. The 100 `sampleTransition` cases of `model-golden.json` (28 of them fresh steps) already lock `sampleTime` bit for bit in Node and in the browser, and a test ties `sampleAdvance` to `sampleTime` (section 6).

## 5. Seams

- **Market scenarios and a market control (R7).** The engine treats market steps as segment bounds like any other (section 4.3), for any number of steps. A later package that ships a multi-step market needs no engine change here. This spec adds nothing else.
- **The pipeline and drop-out (R1, R8).** Rule (l) is one branch: pipeline rows never apply. Funded schedules are identified by `funding`, never by `origin`, so a later package that funds pipeline rows adds an application source without changing the line, the pools or the ledger. Exemption from drop-out is the one flag `Job.certain` read in `stageStep`; a later model in which money changes stage chances replaces that branch with a function of coverage, and nothing else moves. No such function is added now.
- **A rent model (R4).** Nothing in this subsystem reads rents or prices; nothing is done now.
