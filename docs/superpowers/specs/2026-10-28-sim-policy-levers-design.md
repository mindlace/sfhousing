# Policy levers for the simulation (`/sim`), phase 1: zoning rules and permitting timers

Status: **draft for partner review** (2026-10-28). This is where we discuss the design: edit it directly.

Source designs: [the SF Housing Simulator design](../design/2026-10-10-1749-sf-housing-sim.md) (seam row `policy-levers`, M3) and [the FZP timelapse design](../design/2026-10-16-1903-fzp-timelapse.md) (which left levers as a seam: T9 `PolicyOp`).

## Why

The tool exists so people can imagine, concretely, how San Francisco's housing crisis could be solved. Someone brings the policy they believe in (their "pet policy"), runs it through the same simulation as every other policy, and sees whether it gets the city where it needs to be.

That only works if two things hold:

- **No policy is special.** A user's zoning goes through the same lot-by-lot initiation hazard, size draws and stage timelines as the Family Zoning Plan (FZP). Only a fraction of what zoning allows gets built, and that fraction is the model's answer, not the user's.
- **Levers look like real policy.** Presets are things SF or California has done or proposed, with sources. Users can start from a preset and push further.

The partner also wants the simple editor (`/`, brush painting with instant feedback) kept as it is. Its quick payoff should draw people into the harder, simulated view.

## Phasing (partner ruling)

1. **This phase: zoning rules and permitting timers.** These reuse what the simulation already has: envelopes and yields for zoning, and per-pathway stage tables for timing.
2. **Next phase: funding.** This covers direct and indirect subsidy for housing starts. It needs much more simulation, roughly a feasibility (residual land value) model. Affordability scoring (rent and housing cost) and the RHNA income-tier breakdown arrive with it, because both depend on knowing which units are affordable and what they cost.
3. **Later: taking or forcing land into use.** This covers eminent domain and use-or-sale requirements for vacant lots. It brings its own hard decisions, such as how displacement is handled. SF has used eminent domain before, in the Fillmore redevelopment, and did harm with it. That history is part of why the lever is real and why it comes with consequences.

## Goals

1. **A `/sim` page.** It starts as a rebadged `/fzp`. `/fzp` keeps working by routing to `/sim`, with query parameters such as `?seed=` preserved. `/` stays the brush editor.
2. **Zoning as layers.**
   - **Stock layers** are published height maps, toggled on or off: the FZP (the real map as it ships today), **SB 79** (generated by us from its rules and `transit.json` stops), and the pre-FZP base zoning. Where several stock layers are on, each lot gets the most permissive allowance among them (my call: "blend additively" read as max; this matches SB 79, which legally raises the floor over local zoning).
   - **One user layer** overrides the stock layers wherever it says something. There may be more user layers later. It is built with rule-like brushes:
     - **Transit:** pick stop types (BART and Caltrain, Muni Metro, rapid bus, frequent bus) and distance bands, each with a height.
     - **Street:** pick a street class (for example major arterials or commercial corridors) or a named street, and set a height for the lots fronting it, optionally one lot deep. The freehand brush can also lock onto a street and follow it.
     - **Area:** pick a neighborhood or a current zoning district type (for example every RH-1 lot).
     - **Freehand:** the existing radius brush.
     - **Density limits:** on or off within the brush's reach. With limits off the zoning is form-based: height alone sets the unit count, as it does on FZP Local Program lots.
     - Each brush can **raise**, **lower** or **set** a height, so the user layer can cap zoning as well as raise it.
   - **Protections** are a single mechanism that wins over every layer, the user's own brushes included. A protected lot keeps its current-law zoning. The user can protect by category (rent-controlled buildings via `rentControlLikely`, historic buildings, buildings newer than N years) or brush protection onto specific lots. There is no separate "freeze" brush.
   - The combined height map, with its density settings, becomes the envelope the simulation runs on. Yields and `hostable` are recomputed with the shared `scenarioYield`, as the scenario-data spec §5 prescribes for envelope levers.
3. **Permitting timers as an editable timeline.**
   - **One timeline per approval route** (discretionary, streamlined, permit-only). Each stage is a segment whose width is its average duration, and a line closes each segment, labelled with the chance of advancing. This is "what your simulation will use": one current set, not a before-and-after view.
   - **The share of new projects on each route** is shown and editable. Real streamlining policy (SB 423) mostly works by moving projects onto the faster route, not by speeding up a route. FZP Local Program lots stay forced onto streamlined unless a preset says otherwise.
   - **Presets** rewrite the timelines and route shares. The starting set is "SF as observed", the SB 423 approval clock (90 days for 150 or fewer units, 180 days above), no discretionary review, the CEQA infill exemption (AB 130 / SB 131, 2025), and permit issuance at a benchmark city's pace (for example San Diego's median of about 134 days, BLA Mar 2026). Each preset cites its sources.
   - **Editing:** the user drags durations. Any edit turns the timeline into "Custom (from <preset>)". **Save** and **revert** work on the current set, and revert returns to the last save or the preset.
   - **Editable durations** are pre-application, filed to entitled, permit filed and permit issued, the stages the city controls. **Construction is shown but locked**: its speed is set mostly by the builder, financing and technology, not permitting policy. Modular construction is a candidate lever for the funding phase. Durations have physical minimums (stage floors are left to the builder, sourced).
   - **The chances of advancing are shown, not editable.** The partner's view is that they mostly move with financing, so they wait for the funding phase. One narrow exception (my call): a preset that legally removes the city's ability to deny a compliant project (ministerial or by-right approval) may set the **disapproved** exit to zero for the projects it covers. Withdrawals, cancellations and expiries stay as fitted. The builder first measures the disapproved share. If it is negligible, drop the exception.
   - My call on how a duration edit is applied: it rescales the stage's fitted cumulative-incidence curve in time, so the spread of real outcomes is kept and the exit shares are unchanged. That means the curve's advance and exit columns are stretched together. The builder decides the exact construction, and `parseCalibration` must accept the result.
4. **Scoring for this phase: total net units against RHNA**, as the timelapse already does (82,069 by 2031-01-31, plus the 15%-buffer reference and the existing references).
   - The comparison twin changes from pre-FZP zoning to **current law**: the FZP map, the SF SB 79 alternative plan (§207.11) and "SF as observed" permitting. `planEffect` then shows what the user's policy adds beyond what is already law.
   - The current pre-FZP twin can stay available as a stock comparison (left to the builder).
5. **Everything goes through the existing seam.** User zoning and timers become `PolicyOp`s passed to `applyPolicies` (`src/lib/sim/scenario/policy.ts`). Under levers the worker posts the transformed lookups (`chanceTable`, `typicalMonths`; see `docs/dev/fzp-mode.md`, lookups seam), and `initScenario` and `startScenario` gain the lever parameter.

## Partner rulings after the builder check (2026-10-28)

- **Timer edits and the in-flight pipeline.** Each preset declares its reach, following the real law:
  - Permit-office speedups, such as permit issuance pace, apply to every project, including ones already sitting in that stage. The remaining time in the stage is rescaled.
  - Approval-route and entitlement changes, such as the SB 423 clock, no discretionary review or the CEQA exemption, apply only to projects that file after the policy start date.
  - Custom edits apply to every project.
  - The disapproval exception follows its preset's reach.
  - This means a `PolicyOp` may need per-project scope (in-flight versus new), not only a rewritten calibration.
- **Protection means "not upzoned" (for now).** A protected lot keeps its **current-law** envelope: FZP + the SF alternative plan (§207.11), fixed, whatever the stock-layer toggles say. Neither the stock layers nor the user layer can raise it, and it stays hostable under that envelope. Resolution order:
  1. Stock layers (most permissive among those on).
  2. The user layer overrides.
  3. Protected lots are reset to the current-law envelope.

  The score shows how much capacity the protections removed relative to the same plan without them. "Not redevelopable", which would protect tenants from demolition, is a later option tied to displacement work.
- **`/sim` policies are publishable.** A policy (stock-layer toggles, user layer, protections, route timelines and shares, comparison twin) is saved locally and can be published as an immutable link, like editor plans (`docs/dev/plan-sharing.md`). That needs a `Plan` schema version (or a sibling document type) carrying these ops, server validation with size limits reviewed (`MAX_PLAN_BYTES`, `OPS_MAX`), and a read-only shared view of `/sim` with a Remix action. Timeline **Save** is a named local snapshot inside the current policy. Publishing captures the current policy as a whole.

## Partner rulings, round 2 (2026-10-28, after plan 1 research)

Planning found SF Ordinance 082-26 (File 260132, approved 2026-05-08): an SB 79 Alternative Plan that keeps the state SB 79 standards from taking effect in SF until the next Housing Element cycle and adds Planning Code §207.11 instead. Rulings:

1. **Current law = FZP + §207.11** (the City's alternative plan, with its permanent and temporary exemptions) + "SF as observed" permitting. **SB 79 as written** is a separate stock layer users can toggle, to see what the state version would have done. The default comparison reads "versus the City's actual rules".
2. **SB 79 density is capped at the statute's du/ac** by tier (Table 1). The State Density Bonus Law on top of it is a later lever. This supersedes the earlier form-based call.
3. **Exemptions:** the SB 79-as-written layer ignores the City's exemptions. The current-law (§207.11) layer applies them, so the exemption parcel tables in Board File 260132 are sourced as a new, non-DataSF source.
4. **Historic protection** covers status codes **A and A\*** of DataSF `3tsw-4idn`. B (mostly "45 years or older") does not count.
5. **"Commercial corridor"** is composed from street class plus neighborhood-commercial (NC/NCT) zoning on the fronting lots.
6. **TOD stop tiers:** if MTC publishes its official SB 79 stop/tier map as open data, use it, with our GTFS-based tiering kept as a cross-check.

7. **Current law changes over time (plan 2).** By default, current law switches on **2032-01-31** to SB 79 as written minus the City's permanent exemptions. That date is one year after the 7th-cycle Housing Element due date, and it is labelled as an assumption. A user toggle, "assume the City keeps an alternative plan", keeps FZP + §207.11 throughout.

## Calls made after the builder check (my calls; partner to confirm)

- **Where yields come from.** The pipeline precomputes yields and `hostable` for every stock layer: FZP, base, **SB 79**, and the **current-law** combination (FZP + the SF alternative plan (§207.11), most permissive). The worker runs `scenarioYield` only for lots the user layer touches, loading their chunks on demand. A citywide rule brush (for example every arterial) can touch many thousands of lots. That recompute runs in the background. The page shows the simulation is out of date until it finishes, and the run uses the new envelopes only once they are complete. If that is too slow, a compact per-lot yield-by-stories table is the fallback, within the V19 budget.
- **Density.**
  - A brush with density limits **on** keeps each lot's current-law cap.
  - Brushes that **raise** height default to limits **off**. Otherwise raising an RH-1 lot's height often adds nothing, which would read as a bug.
  - SB 79 lots are capped at the statute densities (superseded call; see round 2 rulings).
  - **Density and approval route are decoupled.** Turning limits off does not force a lot onto the streamlined route. The forced-streamlined rule stays tied to FZP Local Program lots while the FZP stock layer is on. Route shares in the timeline govern every other lot.
- **Defaults.** `/sim` opens with current law on: FZP + the SF alternative plan (§207.11) + "SF as observed" timers. `planEffect` starts at zero and reads "your policy versus current law". A **compare against** control offers pre-FZP zoning as the alternative twin, so the FZP-effect story `/fzp` tells today stays one click away. `/fzp` → `/sim` links open with that comparison selected and the user layer empty, so they show what `/fzp` shows now.
- **Area brush by zoning district.** "Every RH-1 lot" needs the use district per lot. The scenario file holds only height districts today. Added to the data work (source: DataSF zoning, `acdm-wktn`, already in the lock).

## Non-goals (this phase)

- Funding, subsidy, fees, inclusionary rates, and any feasibility or cost model.
- Rent, price or affordability scores. Income-tier RHNA scoring.
- Eminent domain, use-or-sale and vacancy levers. Displacement modelling. (Protections are the only displacement-adjacent control.)
- Editing chances of advancing, except the narrow disapproval exception above.
- Editing construction duration.
- Changes to `/` beyond what is needed to reuse its brush code.
- Rebuilding the FZP from rules. FZP ships as the real map. Only SB 79 is generated from rules.
- Bulk, setbacks, FAR and lot coverage: the envelope stays `maxStories` plus density on or off.

## Anti-goals (would make this a failure even if it works)

- **A policy that skips the simulation.** No user setting may decide directly how much gets built.
- **Promising what the evidence can't back.** Presets without sources, or durations below physical minimums, make the tool advocacy rather than a simulator. Every number follows the evidence hierarchy: per-record data, then official aggregates, then inferences, then literature, then invented values. It carries its tier and source, and the UI can show both.
- **Making the hard choices invisible.** Protections and their trade-off (fewer sites) must be visible in the score.

## Constraints

- Same stack and conventions as the rest of the repo: Svelte 5 runes, Tailwind 4, colocated Vitest, Playwright e2e, WCAG 2.2 AA (`docs/dev/accessibility.md`), `expect.requireAssertions`.
- The simulation stays client-side in the worker. It is deterministic in `(dataset, levers, seed)`, with common random numbers across the pair.
- New static data (street-frontage matching, SB 79 inputs) goes through the pipeline into an immutable dataset version (`docs/dev/pipeline.md`), with its sources in the lock and attribution. The scenario file size budget (V19, 3.5 MB) applies.
- CSP changes are interface changes (`kit.csp`).

## Known data work

- **Street frontage.** "Lots fronting a street" needs street centerlines with a class, matched to lots. The basemap has street geometry, but there is no lot-to-street match today. Likely source: DataSF street centerlines. This is new pipeline work. (Unverified: the builder confirms the dataset and its class field.)
- **SB 79.** The stop tiers, distance bands, heights and densities come from the statute (ABAG summary: https://abag.ca.gov/sites/default/files/documents/2026-04/SB79-Summary-040826.pdf), plus SF's exemptions and any local alternative plan. The Planning Department estimates 19k–47k likely units, a range our stock layer's simulated output can be sanity-checked against.
- **Historic and new-building categories for protections.** `yearBuilt` exists. Historic status is not in the scenario file today, so the builder finds a source or defers that category.
- **Preset parameters.** The builder researches each preset to the highest available evidence tier. Starting sources: BLA "Post-Entitlement Permitting" (Mar 2026), HCD Policy and Practice Review (2023), SF Planning SB 423 page, AB 130 / SB 131.
