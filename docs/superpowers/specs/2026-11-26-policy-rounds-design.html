Policy rounds: the simulator first, no points

Remove units, drops, the tally and the tree. Keep the simulation page as the forecasting tool at all times, and reveal policies a few per round.

Status Draft for the owner to edit. Open choices are marked as questions.

Intent

The incremental loop (#158) turned the simulator into a game of buying power-ups. The owner's aim for incrementalism was narrower: show the policy choices over time, in a tool that stays an educational simulator where people experiment and see which choices do more. This design keeps the progressive reveal and removes the economy around it.

Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

Goals

  • After the opening there is one screen: the simulation page with the map in 3D, the timeline, Play and Pause, the ruler, the chart, cards and Details (P2, P4, P5).
  • No units, drops, ring, tally, prices or buying anywhere (P3).
  • Policies arrive a few per round. Each arrives fixed, with a switch. A policy becomes editable once the player has won with it on (P8, P9, P10).
  • The target is the player's own best date. Beating it by any means wins the round (P7, P11), once the playhead has seen the goal month (P12).
  • Every policy the player has can be switched or edited at any time, with no need to take a new target first (P6).
  • Games saved since #158 keep working.

Non-goals

  • A second yardstick for money policies. The date cannot tell them apart (see the measurements); Details already shows affordable homes and public money. A later design can add a measure per round.
  • New policies beyond those the tree already had.
  • Any change to the opening (the brush, the room goal, Start).
  • A speed of 1 month per second. It was removed in #63 and P4 asks for the workshop's speeds.
  • Fixing the height anomaly (P15). It is being investigated and gets its own change; see Height.

Anti-goals

  • A screen after the opening that hides the map or the timeline.
  • A forecast the player cannot pause, scrub or replay.
  • A policy the player has won with that they cannot edit, or an edit that silently reverts (the 805 m revert the owner hit).
  • A round the player cannot get past.
  • Any currency, score multiplier or reward that is not the forecast itself.

What exists today

  • Phases. After Start the page moves through starting, run, tally, tree and workshop (src/lib/sim-page/game-phase.ts). Only the workshop mounts the timeline; it is reached through Edit on a bought node.
  • The run view. In starting, run and tally the map is locked flat, north up, fitted to the city (setRunView in SimMap.svelte, runFrame in camera.ts). At that zoom the buildings layer is below its minimum zoom. The design gave the reason as "every drop is then reachable". Nothing of the 3D style was deleted.
  • The run clock. A run is a fixed 20 seconds (RUN_MS, src/lib/fzp/playback.ts): 2 years per second to the first deadline. The workshop's Playhead still has 1 year and 3 years per second, pause, drag and keys.
  • Reveal. A node shows when its ring is at or below the rung and one of its prerequisites is bought (isShown, src/lib/game/tree.ts). Limits come only from bought (limitsOf, src/lib/game/limits.ts); edits are capped at the bought level.
  • Zoning. Eleven zoning nodes all edit one transit rule, game-transit, so their switches move together. The policy document itself allows any number of transit rules; where they overlap each lot takes the tallest.
  • The ladder. Five deadlines: Oct 2066, then Dec 2060, 2055, 2045, 2040 (LADDER_YEARS), set by a rounding rule from one measurement.

Design

Words

Goal
82,069 homes, as today.
Goal month
The month the current forecast first reaches the goal.
Target
The month to beat: the player's best goal month so far. Before the baseline has been seen there is none, and the header shows the legal deadline (defaultGoal(asOf).deadline, Oct 2066).
Round
A set of policies offered together.
Fixed
An offered policy whose only control is its switch.
Editable
A policy the player can change in its editor, to any value the editor allows.
Baseline
The forecast with no offered policy on: current law plus the player's painted plan.

The loop

  1. The baseline comes first. After Start no policy is offered. The player plays or drags to the baseline's goal month. Seeing it sets the first target and offers round 1. Seeing current law before any policy is the comparison everything later is read against. Accepted by the owner (2026-11-26).
    • Nothing depends on the baseline meeting the legal deadline. The owner's rule is that the model follows realism wherever current law then lands, and the game is re-derived from it. The deadline is a mark on the ruler for comparison.
    • If the baseline never reaches the goal in the window, seeing the end of the window offers round 1, and the first forecast that reaches the goal is a win.
  2. A change resets the timeline. When a policy is switched on or off, or edited, playback pauses and the playhead goes to the first month the change takes effect, or to today if that is later (the owner's rule, 2026-11-26). Everything after that point is unseen again. So a player parked past the target cannot win by switching a policy on without watching (P12); they press Play or drag forward from the reset point.
  3. Seen. A month is seen when the playhead has landed on it or passed over it, by Play, drag or keys, since the last change. With the reset above, this is the ruler's "seen" zone.
  4. A win. The forecast is current, its goal month is earlier than the target, and that month has been seen. A win does not move the playhead. Then:
    • the goal month becomes the target (P7);
    • every fixed policy that was on becomes editable (P9);
    • the next round is offered, if one is left.
  5. Any means count. A win needs no new policy on. Editing an older policy for an earlier date wins the round (P11).
  6. Tried. A fixed policy is tried once, with that policy on and the forecast current, the goal month or the target month has been seen, whichever comes first. The tried set is emptied when a round is offered.
  7. No gain. "Fixed policies on offer" means every policy that is still fixed: this round's and any left off in earlier rounds. When all of them have been tried and none of those plays was a win, the round completes anyway: all of them become editable, the target stays, and the next round is offered. Switching every fixed policy on and playing once is enough. The page says the policies added nothing on top of what the player has (P13).
  8. The end. When the last round has completed, the game is finished: the limits are those finished gives today, and "+" offers every kind of policy. The brush stays in the opening. The target keeps tracking the player's best date.

Nothing is ever taken away. A later forecast that is worse changes nothing; the target is the best date reached. Wins and editable policies are not undone by Undo.

The policies and their rounds

Twelve policies, down from 23 nodes. The three "hands" nodes go with the points. The eleven zoning increments become three transit rules, one per class of transit, each its own policy in the document with its own switch (P10). The classes are the owner's: heavy rail (BART and Caltrain), light rail (Muni Metro), and bus (rapid and frequent together). The bus rule holds the app's two bus stop types. Permitting and money policies are the presets the tree already used.

Reach

The transit editor's maximum reach comes down from 3,219 m to a walking distance, and a new rule no longer starts at the maximum (P14). With a walking-distance maximum the four stop types stay different policies; at two miles any one of them covers the city.

What the sources say about walking to transit
SourceFigure
SB 79 (2025), AB 2097 (2022)Half a mile (805 m), straight line from the parcel
Agrawal, Schlossberg and Irvin 2008 (Bay Area and Portland rail)Median walk 0.47 miles (about 760 m)
El-Geneidy et al. 2014 (Montreal)85th percentile: about 524 m to bus, about 1,259 m to commuter rail
  • Maximum by stop type: 1,200 m for regional rail and Muni Metro, the long tail of measured walks to rail; 800 m for rapid and frequent bus, the legal half mile and well above the measured 524 m. The app measures in a straight line, as SB 79 does, so the real walk is longer.
  • Rules that mix classes. In the rounds every transit policy is one class, so its maximum is that class's. A rule can mix rail and bus only outside the rounds: after the end, and in documents already stored. Such a rule takes the shortest maximum among its stop types.
  • Default for a new rule: SB 79's two bands, 402 m and 805 m, which the open editor already uses. A new rule never starts at the maximum.
  • Stored values are kept. A published policy or a saved game with a longer reach keeps its value and keeps running. The editor shows the stored value and clamps only when the player changes it, as it does today. Today a game would silently drop such a policy from its forecast (within in limits.ts); that must not happen.
Decision The owner accepted 1,200 m and pointed out that stop types are separate policies, so the maximum can differ by type (2026-11-26). The bus value of 800 m and the rule for several stop types are my calls. The evidence for 1,200 m is one survey's 85th percentile for rail; the law stops at 805 m.

Height

On the rail rule, 40 stories gives fewer homes by 2066 than 12 (92,259 against 94,074), and 20 stories reaches the goal later than 12 (P15). A counterintuitive result is fine when its cause is real. This one looks like an artefact: a height limit is a maximum, and the model treats it as the height to build.

The cause, read from the code and not yet confirmed lot by lot: the engine builds to the limit instead of choosing the best building at or below it.

  • From 13 stories, a lot of 1,500 m² or more becomes a tower: a 5-story podium and a slim floor plate. On a large lot a 13-story tower holds about half the homes of a 12-story midrise, and a tower only catches up near 40 stories (src/lib/generator/select.ts, massing.ts).
  • The size draw uses a different table once the limit is above 12, one that favours smaller fills (size.byEnvelope in the calibration).
  • The start odds read two features computed from the limit, through fitted bins that do not rise steadily.

The fix is a separate change to the engine: each lot takes the best building at or below its limit, with a height sweep beside scripts/money-monotone.ts as a diagnostic. It changes the dataset version, so it gets its own design. This design does not wait for it: the fixed heights in the transit rules are all below 13 stories. A player who edits a rule above 12 stories will still meet the anomaly until the fix lands.

State and saved games

GameState version 3 holds: the round offered so far, the target, the set of editable policies, the set of fixed policies tried since the last round was offered, the list of wins (goal month, when, and the dataset version), and whether the game is finished. It no longer holds units, bought, the last run's result, side goals, the rung or the run count. limitsOf derives the limits from this state.

A version 2 game is upgraded where it is parsed, in the browser and on the server, and is never deleted:

  • the plan and the policy document are kept as they are;
  • a game with no run played starts at the baseline; any other game has rounds 1 to rung + 1 offered, capped at the last round;
  • the target is the deadline of the last rung met, or the legal deadline;
  • every policy in the document that ran before the upgrade keeps running and is editable: bought presets, the old shared transit rule, and anything the player added (other transit rules, protections, custom timings, edited measures). The old shared transit rule sits beside the new ones as the player's own;
  • a game that had met every deadline is finished.

A new dataset. The target is a month from one dataset's forecast. It is kept when the dataset changes; it is not re-based. If the new forecast beats it, that is a win like any other. If the target is now out of reach, the no-gain rule still moves the rounds on.

The game_save columns phase and rung list a game's progress. Whether rung is renamed by a generated migration or reused for the round is left to the plan; no migration is hand-edited.

The dataset check

The rounds need almost nothing from a dataset. Targets come from the player's own forecast and a round can complete with no gain, so no model result can block the game. This follows the owner's rule (2026-11-26) that levers are judged by realism, and that "more never builds fewer" is a diagnostic and not a gate.

  • The gate. scripts/reach-check.ts still runs before every upload, with one rule: the goal is reached inside the window with every policy on at its fixed values. Without that the game has no target to offer. It no longer requires current law to meet a deadline.
  • The report. The same script prints each policy's effect alone and in its round, and each round's effect all on, replacing tree-balance. A round that gains nothing, or a policy that loses homes, is printed for a person to judge: a real mechanism stays, a model artefact gets fixed.

The pair that does it is the rail rule with the housing bond: no gain on current law, and 4,598 fewer homes by 2066 than the rail rule alone, nearly all affordable. Only in that run, three fixed-amount money pools close in Oct 2031 with money left in them (about $44M of City bond money is never spent), and from 2032 affordable projects start later or never. The closing rule (closeSpent, src/lib/sim/world/funding/closing.ts) calls a pool spent when every waiting application asks for more than it holds. That reads as an artefact, not a real mechanism. It belongs to the funding engine, outside this design; round 1 still gains with it present.
  • Four policies move nothing where they are offered: the SB 423 clock, the revolving fund, the public bank and state-double. State-double gains 2 months once no discretionary review is on. The public bank changed nothing in any case measured.
  • A longer reach can lose homes. Taking the Muni Metro rule's outer band to 1,200 m reaches the goal 12 months earlier but leaves 1,892 fewer homes by 2066.

If a later dataset leaves a round with no gain for a player who edits nothing, the report shows it. Reordering the rounds or changing a fixed value is then the owner's choice.

Constraints

  • The engine, the worker protocol and the map stream are not changed by this design. Seeking is already done in the browser.
  • What is published at /s/<id> is still the runnable document, and existing published policies load and run unchanged.
  • WCAG 2.2 AA: a win and a no-gain result are announced; everything a drag does has a key, as the ruler has today.
  • Page text stays terse; details sit behind a tap.
  • The e2e seam for a seeded game stays, with the new state. Signoff's checks apply (no server stderr, no timing crutches, no retried flakes).
  • Docs change with the code: CLAUDE.md, docs/dev/game.md, game-save.md, sim-page.md, timeline-ui.md, map-interfaces.md, map-perf.md, testing.md.

What done looks like

  • After Start the map is tilted with buildings drawn, and it can be panned and zoomed at every moment of the game.
  • Play, Pause, drag, the keys and the 1× and 3× speeds work from the first second.
  • No unit count, price, drop or tally appears anywhere, including About.
  • The baseline play wins, sets the target to its goal month and offers round 1.
  • Switching on a round-1 policy and seeing the goal month wins, moves the target to that month, and makes that policy editable; setting its reach to any allowed value holds.
  • Editing an older policy for an earlier date wins with no new policy on.
  • Switching a policy on with the playhead at the end of the timeline pauses playback and moves the playhead to today, or to the policy's start if later; no win is given until the goal month is reached again.
  • With every fixed policy tried and no earlier date, the round completes as no gain.
  • A game saved before this change opens with its policies and plan intact.
  • With a baseline that misses the legal deadline, the game still plays: the baseline's goal month becomes the first target.
  • The reach check passes on the current dataset under its one rule and prints the report.
