Policy rounds: the simulator first, no points

Remove units, drops, the tally and the tree. Keep the simulation page as the forecasting tool at all times, and reveal policies a few per round.

Status Draft for the owner to edit. Two lookups are still running (walking-distance sources for the reach maximum, and why a taller height limit can reach the goal later). The places that wait on them are marked as questions.

Intent

The incremental loop (#158) turned the simulator into a game of buying power-ups. The owner's aim for incrementalism was narrower: show the policy choices over time, in a tool that stays an educational simulator where people experiment and see which choices do more. This design keeps the progressive reveal and removes the economy around it.

Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

Goals

  • After the opening there is one screen: the simulation page with the map in 3D, the timeline, Play and Pause, the ruler, the chart, cards and Details (P2, P4, P5).
  • No units, drops, ring, tally, prices or buying anywhere (P3).
  • Policies arrive a few per round. Each arrives fixed, with a switch. A policy becomes editable once the player has won with it on (P8, P9, P10).
  • The target is the player's own best date. Beating it by any means wins the round (P7, P11), once the playhead has seen the goal month (P12).
  • Every policy the player has can be switched or edited at any time, with no need to take a new target first (P6).
  • Games saved since #158 keep working.

Non-goals

  • A second yardstick for money policies. The date cannot tell them apart (see the measurements); Details already shows affordable homes and public money. A later design can add a measure per round.
  • New policies beyond those the tree already had.
  • Any change to the opening (the brush, the room goal, Start).
  • A speed of 1 month per second. It was removed in #63 and P4 asks for the workshop's speeds.
  • Fixing the height anomaly (P15). It is being investigated and gets its own change; see Height.

Anti-goals

  • A screen after the opening that hides the map or the timeline.
  • A forecast the player cannot pause, scrub or replay.
  • A policy the player has won with that they cannot edit, or an edit that silently reverts (the 805 m revert the owner hit).
  • A round the player cannot get past.
  • Any currency, score multiplier or reward that is not the forecast itself.

What exists today

  • Phases. After Start the page moves through starting, run, tally, tree and workshop (src/lib/sim-page/game-phase.ts). Only the workshop mounts the timeline; it is reached through Edit on a bought node.
  • The run view. In starting, run and tally the map is locked flat, north up, fitted to the city (setRunView in SimMap.svelte, runFrame in camera.ts). At that zoom the buildings layer is below its minimum zoom. The design gave the reason as "every drop is then reachable". Nothing of the 3D style was deleted.
  • The run clock. A run is a fixed 20 seconds (RUN_MS, src/lib/fzp/playback.ts): 2 years per second to the first deadline. The workshop's Playhead still has 1 year and 3 years per second, pause, drag and keys.
  • Reveal. A node shows when its ring is at or below the rung and one of its prerequisites is bought (isShown, src/lib/game/tree.ts). Limits come only from bought (limitsOf, src/lib/game/limits.ts); edits are capped at the bought level.
  • Zoning. Eleven zoning nodes all edit one transit rule, game-transit, so their switches move together. The policy document itself allows any number of transit rules; where they overlap each lot takes the tallest.
  • The ladder. Five deadlines: Oct 2066, then Dec 2060, 2055, 2045, 2040 (LADDER_YEARS), set by a rounding rule from one measurement.

Design

Words

Goal
82,069 homes, as today.
Goal month
The month the current forecast first reaches the goal.
Target
The month to beat. The first target is the legal deadline (defaultGoal(asOf).deadline, Oct 2066).
Round
A set of policies offered together.
Fixed
An offered policy whose only control is its switch.
Editable
A policy the player can change in its editor, to any value the editor allows.
Baseline
The forecast with no offered policy on: current law plus the player's painted plan.

The loop

  1. The baseline comes first. After Start no policy is offered. The player plays or drags to the goal month. Reaching the goal on or before the legal deadline is the first win. (Proposal, not yet put to the owner: seeing current law before any policy is the comparison everything later is read against.)
  2. A win. The forecast is current, its goal month is earlier than the target, and the playhead has seen that month under the current policy (the ruler's "seen" zone; an edit makes later months unseen again, as it did before #158). Then:
    • the goal month becomes the target (P7);
    • every fixed policy that was on becomes editable (P9);
    • the next round is offered, if one is left.
  3. Any means count. A win needs no new policy on. Editing an older policy for an earlier date wins the round (P11).
  4. Tried. A fixed policy is tried once the playhead, under a current forecast with that policy on, has seen the goal month or the target month, whichever comes first.
  5. No gain. When every fixed policy on offer has been tried and none of those plays was a win, the round completes anyway: those policies become editable, the target stays, and the next round is offered. The page says the policies added nothing on top of what the player has (P13).
  6. The end. When the last round has completed, the whole tool opens: every limit finished lifts today, plus adding policies of any kind. The target keeps tracking the player's best date.

Nothing is ever taken away. A later forecast that is worse changes nothing; the target is the best date reached. Wins and editable policies are not undone by Undo.

Reach

The transit editor's maximum reach comes down from 3,219 m to a walking distance, and a new rule no longer starts at the maximum (P14). With a walking-distance maximum the four stop types stay different policies; at two miles any one of them covers the city.

Question The maximum and the default, in metres, and whether rail and bus differ. Waiting on the source lookup. A published policy with a longer reach keeps its value and still runs.

Height

On the rail rule, 40 stories gives fewer homes by 2066 than 12 (92,259 against 94,074), and 20 stories reaches the goal later than 12. A height limit is a maximum, so a higher one should never do worse (P15). The cause is being investigated. Its fix is a separate change to the engine, with its own dataset check. Until then the fixed heights in the rounds stay at or below 12 stories.

State and saved games

GameState version 3 holds: the round offered so far, the target, the set of editable policies, the set of fixed policies tried since the last round was offered, the list of wins (goal month and when), and whether the game is finished. It no longer holds units, bought, the last run's result, side goals, the rung or the run count. limitsOf derives the limits from this state.

A version 2 game is upgraded where it is parsed, in the browser and on the server, and is never deleted:

  • the plan and the policy document are kept as they are;
  • a game with no run played starts at the baseline; any other game has rounds 1 to rung + 1 offered, capped at the last round;
  • the target is the deadline of the last rung met, or the legal deadline;
  • a permitting or money policy the game had bought is editable; the old shared transit rule stays in the document as the player's own editable rule, beside the new ones.

The game_save columns phase and rung list a game's progress. Whether rung is renamed by a generated migration or reused for the round is left to the plan; no migration is hand-edited.

The dataset check

scripts/reach-check.ts keeps its place before every upload with new rules: the baseline under current law reaches the goal by the legal deadline, and each round, with all its fixed policies on over the fixed policies of the rounds before, reaches the goal earlier than the round before. So a player who edits nothing can win every round. A report script prints each policy's effect alone and each round's, replacing tree-balance. My call.

Constraints

  • The engine, the worker protocol and the map stream are not changed by this design. Seeking is already done in the browser.
  • What is published at /s/<id> is still the runnable document, and existing published policies load and run unchanged.
  • WCAG 2.2 AA: a win and a no-gain result are announced; everything a drag does has a key, as the ruler has today.
  • Page text stays terse; details sit behind a tap.
  • The e2e seam for a seeded game stays, with the new state. Signoff's checks apply (no server stderr, no timing crutches, no retried flakes).
  • Docs change with the code: CLAUDE.md, docs/dev/game.md, game-save.md, sim-page.md, timeline-ui.md, map-interfaces.md, map-perf.md, testing.md.

What done looks like

  • After Start the map is tilted with buildings drawn, and it can be panned and zoomed at every moment of the game.
  • Play, Pause, drag, the keys and the 1× and 3× speeds work from the first second.
  • No unit count, price, drop or tally appears anywhere, including About.
  • The baseline play wins, sets the target to its goal month and offers round 1.
  • Switching on a round-1 policy and seeing the goal month wins, moves the target to that month, and makes that policy editable; setting its reach to any allowed value holds.
  • Editing an older policy for an earlier date wins with no new policy on.
  • With every fixed policy tried and no earlier date, the round completes as no gain.
  • A game saved before this change opens with its policies and plan intact.
  • The reach check passes on the current dataset under its new rules.
