Funding award rule: more money never builds fewer homes

Award what the pools can pay now, and give a fully funded bundle the ordinary start odds, so that buying any funding preset never lowers net, funded or affordable homes.

Problem

Buying more City money for affordable homes makes the forecast build fewer homes. On today's rule, 13 of the 29 tested pairs (a set S, then S plus one more measure) lose net, funded or affordable homes at some deadline. For example, adding general-revenue to the bond loses 7,234 net homes at Dec 2060, and adding public-bank to bond + revolving fund loses 11,104. All of these drops are reachable on /sim as well as in the game: the game and /sim send identical money documents for every set measured.

The game's money tree depends on this. Its prices were tuned on today's behaviour, and the funded-homes and affordable-homes side goals were dropped because no tuning could meet them.

The mechanism, as found in the code

The trace's lead holds in outline. Two of its causes are corrected below, and two more causes were found.

M1. An award needs the whole bundle's level

  • awardSet (src/lib/sim/scenario/funding.ts:390-404) first drops every programme of every short pool, in rounds (leaveOut, :361-385). It then awards only if what is left is non-empty and its level is not below the whole bundle's (:402). The grouped path in awardPasses (around :487-489) applies the same test, and serve.ts:230 throws if the two paths ever disagree.
  • A bundle is every programme offered for the kind whose minHomes the project's size meets (frame-money.ts:21-22, 102). Baseline and preset programmes therefore sit in one bundle, and the presets only add to it (baselineScale: [], funding-presets.ts:221).
  • The consequence: any added programme raises the whole bundle's level, so it becomes a requirement. When its pool is short, applications wait (and take nothing) even where the baseline's money alone would have funded them at the baseline's level. That money sits idle. The design wrote this down as a choice (financing design OD-F10, line 170, "a pool that is on but short keeps them waiting") and as a known limit (docs/dev/finance-engine.md:244-253).

M2. A certain bundle applies at once

  • Coverage is the share of the uncovered cost that grant-equivalents cover (instruments.ts:236-301). An overflowing tier scales its grants down so that grant coverage never exceeds 1. A level of 1 or more is "certain" (instruments.ts:13-17; the cap is in funding.ts:192-194).
  • Below level 1, the monthly start hazard is logistic(x0 + oddsShift(G)). At level 1 the rate is −∞ (clocks.ts:200), which gives p = 1: every eligible lot applies in its first lot-month, on the first day (clocks.ts:307-310).
  • The baseline shares sum to 0.968895, so with nothing bought no bundle is ever certain.
  • Correction to the trace: the bond alone already makes the bundle certain. The bond is harmless because it is one payment: it is spent, its pool closes, and waiting rows are re-made at 0.969. general-revenue is harmful because its yearly inflows run to 2055-07, so its pool cannot close before 2056. For about 30 years every certain application waits on $36.7M a year (about 230 homes a year at its share), with about 550,000 homes in line. Over the run, federal tax-credit spending falls from $6.32B to $3.10B.

M3. A loan worth little asks for a lot (new)

  • public-bank lends 10% of cost at 6.13%. That loan is worth $2,384 a home as a grant-equivalent (instruments.ts:136-150), but it asks the pool for the full principal, $95,413 a home (instruments.ts:270-271; funding.ts:347-350).
  • It lifts coverage from 0.968895 to 0.971662, which is not certain, so M2 does not apply. But once the bank is short, dropping it leaves a set priced below the whole bundle, so M1 makes the application wait.
  • All affordable starts are then throttled through about $46.9M of lending capacity, roughly 491 homes in progress at a time. That costs 11,802 net homes with the bank alone.
  • The bank pool shows $0 "spent" because spent counts grants only (finance-score.md:131). It lent about $138M over the run.
  • Any loan or guarantee programme with a small pool on the affordable reach would do the same.

M4. A pool closes on the first application too big for it (new)

  • This is the bond's shut. The game moves the bond's arrival to 2026-11-11, so the pool is not shut before it arrives; the probe printed calendar months.
  • askedPast (closing.ts:84-109) closes a pool when any waiting row asks more than the pool holds. A single 3,592-home application asking $564M closed the bond on 2027-01-10 with $220M still in it, and money in a closed pool is never awarded. The same thing closes city-debt with $22.6M left. The code follows its spec (2026-11-22-pool-closing-design.html:206); the condition is what is wrong.
  • It is secondary to M1. Fixing it alone recovers 1,580 of the 7,110 homes lost by bond + general revenue.

M5. "Funded homes" counts relabelled market starts (new; score definition, not the rule)

revolving-fund lends only to market-rate lots. Their funded clock is min(ord, x) (clocks.ts:202), so they start at about their ordinary time, but they now carry funding and count as funded (finance-score.md:156-158). As a result, funded homes rise by about 3,100 while net homes rise by 7 and affordable homes not at all. Any side goal on funded homes is therefore inflated by this measure.

What the rule was modelling, and what it got wrong

OD-F10 says a project's capital stack closes whole: "a project never starts on less than it applied for." That is true of real deals: a tax-credit project does not close while a committed source is missing.

What the model gets wrong is how an application is formed. It applies for every programme on offer, whether or not that programme has money. A real sponsor applies to the sources that are awarding, and restructures when one is oversubscribed. A notice of funding availability that is oversubscribed turns applicants away; it does not freeze the applications that other sources were already funding. Extra City money only enlarges the pot, so in the real world it cannot make fewer deals close.

The rule below keeps "never starts on less than it applied for" by reading the application as formed at award time, from the pools that can pay. It drops the second half of OD-F10, "a pool that is on but short keeps them waiting". That half is the known limit, and the doc already says "a later change to this behaviour is deliberate".

The hazard of 1 at full coverage is the owner's ruling R2 ("any 100% grant shifts the odds to 100%"), which has no records behind it. R6 is different: once awarded, a fully funded project never exits and takes its route from affordableRoutes. R6 rests on records (2 exits in 92 passages) and is kept.

How real programmes avoid this

Checked 2026-11-25 against official sources. Tier 0 is per-record, tier 1 is official, tier 3 is literature. No case was found where added local money lowered production. The model's result is an artifact of four divergences:

  1. City money is the last-in gap filler, not a fixed share. MOHCD sizes its loan to the gap left after debt, tax-credit equity and state awards (Underwriting Guidelines 2022, tier 1). The per-home ceiling is set per solicitation (772 Pacific memo, 2024, tier 0). New City money enlarges one pot and never adds a co-requirement. The model's proportional sharing (R13) is what turns a new pool into one.
  2. The City commits first, and the state and federal sources compete later. A preliminary gap loan supports the state applications, and the final loan comes at closing (160 Freelon, 2023–2025, tier 0). The model applies to every source at once and waits for all of them.
  3. Awards expire, and losers re-apply in rounds. HCD withdraws an award if all permanent financing is not secured within 24 months (Disencumbrance Policy, tier 1). CDLAC rescinds a bond allocation if construction has not started 180, 201 or 222 days after it (2026 regulations §5105(h), tier 1). The model's applications wait with no expiry.
  4. The scarce source is state and federal. Tax-exempt bonds and 4% credits have been competitive since 2020 (BLA audit 2023, tier 1). City-funded projects stall waiting for them (4840 Mission: 104 weeks; the 2026 MHP Gap NOFA). The model's federal pool does have a ceiling, which replaced R18's uncapped pool (#145). But three things keep it from binding:
    • It is set at the best award year, $564.5M a year (1,274 homes). The 2018–2025 per-record mean is 737 homes.
    • Unspent money stays in the pool forever, where real unused credits and bond cap go to other regions.
    • Under R3, a short federal pool is simply dropped from the award.
    So the model overstates what extra City money buys, which is the opposite error to the one this spec fixes.

One real cost of more sources is missing from the model: each added public source adds about 3.9 months and $20,460 a home (Terner Center 2025, tier 3, already in the evidence digest §7). It slows deals; it does not cut the total.

R3 is the minimal fix to this rule. Matching reality more closely would need gap-last City sizing, award expiry, and a federal ceiling that binds. These are model changes for a follow-up spec; gap-last sizing touches src/lib/sim/finance, which is hashed, so it means a new dataset.

Follow-up Money-limited affordable production takes these up. It is implemented after this spec.

Calls made in this draft (check these)

  • Odds at application stay those of the whole bundle. Under R3, an application draws its start from the whole bundle's level and may then be awarded a smaller set. This leftover is accepted: with a continuous hazard, the gap between a level of 0.969 and 1 is a small odds shift, and R3's measured numbers already include it. If the dip trace shows that this is what causes the −212, the exact fix is thinning at award time (the R1 + thinning row), not pricing the application from the pools that can pay.
  • The riders. The measured R2 swap deleted only clocks.ts:200. Through :307 that also turned off the first-day rule, so that rider is already in R3's numbers. The oddsShift clamp was not measured; it only bites where loan coverage exceeds 1. The plan's first step re-runs the sweep with the clamp, before anything is tuned.
  • Homes waiting for money. waitingMoney exists only on the integration branch. On main, the check reads net, funded and affordable homes only. When the game branch merges, the check also fails if homes waiting for money, at any month, exceed current law's peak by more than 10,000. Today's R0 flood is about 550,000; R3 is about 100.
  • Where the dataset check runs. money-monotone needs a real dataset. Main's signoff steps are all local, and reach-measure is not on main. So the dataset run is a required manual step, beside level2-check: before every upload, and for any change on the funding paths listed below (written into docs/dev/pipeline.md and finance-engine.md). Current law moves. R3 changes the run with nothing bought: +3,670 net and +3,921 affordable homes at 2060, and affordable homes at 2035 go from 2,212 to 3,045. R1 alone does this, because baseline pools are short too and the bar was idling them. No calibration fits this throughput:

The monotonicity check

Script: scripts/money-monotone.ts

  • Input: --version <v>, or a built dataset directory, the same way level2-check.ts takes one.
  • Sets: every subset of the offered presets (available in funding-presets.ts; six today, so 64 subsets), each added to current law with addPolicy from month 1, as the game and /sim do. Today's prerequisites are ignored, since /sim allows any set.
  • Edges: every pair (S, S plus one preset): 192.
  • Readings: net (completedNet), funded, and affordable (very low + low + moderate tiers) at Dec 2035, 2040, 2045, 2055 and 2060. Those are the game's deadlines plus 2035.
  • Fails (exit 1) when any reading on any edge drops by more than the tolerance. It prints each offending edge, reading, deadline and size.
  • Tolerance: 5 homes absolute, a named constant. The measured noise floor is 1 home.
  • Cost: 64 views at 0.4–3.8 s each, about 2–3 minutes.

A unit property beside it

A Vitest property test on a small synthetic FinanceIndex. For random bundles, pools and balances: if awardSet awards without an added programme, it still awards with that programme added and its pool at any balance, and the awarded level is not lower. This pins M1 without a dataset. It runs in the server project on every signoff. It is the gate signoff enforces.

Scope and cost

Correction to the premise The award rule and the clock are not hashed into the dataset version. Only src/lib/sim/finance, the pipeline and a few src/lib paths are (pipeline/lib/version.ts:41-50; pipeline/test/version.test.ts:107-118 asserts that other sim code is left out). Ruling tree-catalogue-measure-2 says otherwise; it holds only for src/lib/sim/finance.

No new version also means nothing forces a recheck. So:

  1. Land the fix on main.
  2. Run level2-check by hand on the live dataset. Current law only gains homes, so it should pass.
  3. Run money-monotone, and regenerate the two finance goldens.
  4. Run signoff.

The game is not on main, so no banked game run is affected. If the game ships before this fix, its banked results would carry an unchanged dataset version over a changed engine, and its bars would not be marked stale. That is a reason to land this first.

What the game re-measures when it merges main

  • LADDER_YEARS: npx tsx scripts/reach-measure.ts --version <v> --write. Current law and the full tree both gain homes, so the rung years move earlier.
  • PRICES (especially the money nodes), RUNG_LUMP and SIDE_TUNING: npx tsx scripts/tree-balance.ts --version <v>, iterated by hand until it exits 0, at about 16 s an order. The money-first play should stop sticking at rung 1.
  • level2-check (or reach-check once renamed) on the live dataset.
  • This stacks with the side-goal-effect unit's retune (the side-goal reader bug, ruling conductor-11). Do one retune after both are in, not two.

Can the two side goals come back?

  • affordable-homes: yes. Under R3, money raises affordable homes at every deadline: current law is 15,128 at 2055, bond + GR 17,843, all six 19,032. Its target must be re-measured above the new current law, and tree-balance decides whether some gated play meets it in time.
  • funded-homes: yes, but redefine it first. M5 lets revolving-fund add about 3,000 "funded" homes that are relabelled market starts. Count funded homes on affordable tiers only, or count homes whose start the money moved. Otherwise the goal rewards the cheapest relabelling rather than homes. That is a game-side change to side-goals.ts; the score's fundedHomes stays as it is.

Size

A small engine change (two conditions and the old engine's copy), one closing condition, a new script and a property test, two goldens and four docs. Roughly one implementer day, plus the trace of the public-bank dip. The game retune happens on the integration branch afterwards.

Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

  • Goal: adding any offered preset to any set never lowers net, funded or affordable homes at a game deadline, beyond a 5-home tolerance, and a check fails if it does.
  • Goal: money that a pool holds and a waiting application could use is not left idle because a different pool is short.
  • Non-goal: gap pricing among City sources (option 4), the market's start odds, and yearly lumps (accepted as they are).
  • Anti-goal: a fix that is monotone because it inflates. R1 alone is the example: it gives 87,780 homes for B+GR by starting every lot at once. On the game branch, the check also reads homes waiting for money (see calls).
  • Constraints kept: one future, no random bands; funding lumps stay yearly; evidence is ranked per-record > official aggregate > inference > literature > invented.

  2. Current law moves: decided 2026-11-25, accepted. The new throughput is recorded against the 980-a-year record in finance-data.md, with no refit.
  3. The −212 public-bank dip at 2045 and 2055, on sets holding B+GR+FS. It reverses to +456 by 2060, which points to timing: low-value loans are now awarded early and draw on shared money.
