One world on /sim

Run the user's policy once and the FZP baseline once, and show one future instead of a 48-world average and band.

Why

The timeline runs 48 seeded worlds. Every random draw is keyed by lot or project, so nothing citywide differs between worlds. The per-lot noise averages out, and as a result the "8 in 10 futures" band is a few percent wide and "This city" sits on "Average". The band suggests uncertainty the model doesn't actually measure, and it costs 48× the compute.

The owner's position: the sim shows one possible future. Citywide booms and busts belong in market conditions, an explicit input the user can see and change. They don't belong in random seeds.

Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

  • Goal: /sim runs two worlds on seed 1: the user's policy and the FZP baseline (the comparison twin). Every number on the page comes from those two worlds.
  • Goal: the chart shows one line for homes completed (plus the state target). It has no band, no Average, and no "This city".
  • Goal: "Because of this policy" is the policy world minus the baseline world. Both worlds use the same per-lot dice.
  • Non-goal: market-conditions scenarios (booms and busts). That is separate later work.
  • Non-goal: /lesscars. It keeps its own 101 paired runs and band.
  • Anti-goal: keeping the multi-world machinery "just in case", or showing any range or "chance" built from seeds.

Approaches

  • One world plus the baseline (picked). This is simple and honest. It also makes the page consistent: the map, cards, money, income tiers and "per added home" already read world 1, and only the totals used the mean.
  • Keep 48 worlds and add citywide shocks to each one. This would make the band meaningful. Rejected: the owner wants shocks visible as market conditions, not hidden in seeds.
  • Keep 48 worlds and hide the band. Rejected: it wastes 48× the compute, and the headline would still be an average no one can see.

Design

  1. WORLDS becomes 1 (src/lib/sim/timeline-client.ts). The protocol already accepts worlds >= 1. The plan and the comparison each run one world on seed 1.
  2. The score shape loses its across-world statistics. SeriesChunk and TimelineView drop mean/sd/band/shown in favour of the world's own rows. planEffect is plan minus twin with no sd, and BAND_Z/bandOf are removed. The builder decides whether to collapse the multi-world code path entirely or leave it parameterised at 1; collapsing is preferred where it removes code.
  3. UI: remove the band, the Average line and the "This city" line from ChartRow, ScoreChart/chart.ts, SimResults and score-view.ts. Delete RANGE_NOTE and CITY_NOTE, and reword TWIN_NOTE only as far as it mentions averaging. Keep the copy terse.
  4. Headline, Results totals, targets and RHNA fraction become the world's whole-number values. The e2e debug hook mean() is renamed to fit.
  5. Update the docs ensemble-runner.md, world-score.md, timeline-ui.md, world-core.md, world-funding.md (perf bar), map-stream.md and policy-timeline.md (a note on single-world pairing). Update the tests, e2e and perf budgets. scripts/ensemble-measure.ts defaults to 1 world.
  6. The funding tolerance referee keeps its own 200 seeds; it is a test of the engine, not a page result.
Risk With one pair, the policy effect can jitter. Most lots are cancelled by the shared dice, but competition for the funding pool and the dice-order shifts from resizes and phases still let noise through. On a small policy the effect could come out jumpy or even negative.
Measure, don't pre-fix The plan includes a measurement step: compare the one-pair effect against the 48-pair mean on the fixture policies, before the 48-world path is removed. The result is reported to the owner. If it's jumpy, the fix (removing those noise sources) is separate work, and more worlds are not an option.

Left to the builder

  • How far to collapse the ensemble runner's internals (the restore-snapshot "copies" stay; they serve fast edits, not alternate futures).
  • Regenerating goldens and exact-value e2e expectations, which shift because the headline moves from the mean to world 1.
