Building choice: the developer picks the building a cap allows

Status: approved (2026-11-26). For each lot, list the buildings it could hold at or below its cap, cost and time each by construction type, and start the one a developer would build; check the picks against DBI records.

Intent and why

The simulation never chooses a building. A story cap S turns into five fixed heights (0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.9 and 1 times S, FILL_LEVELS and fillStories in src/lib/data/scenario-codec.ts). Each height gets the one building that the generator's height-and-area cascade produces (selectTypology in src/lib/generator/select.ts). Those yields are baked into gross.bin (scenarioYield in src/lib/data/scenario-yield.ts calls generate(rec, {maxStories: s}, …, 'max')). The size of a start is drawn as a fraction of the cap from buckets keyed on the cap (bucketOf and fillSizeRow in src/lib/sim/scenario/input.ts). The start odds are a logit fitted to the generator's own yields. Stage durations have no height term, and the finance prototypes give a fixed cost per home by invented height bins (prototypeFor, PROTOTYPE_BINNING tier 4, in src/lib/sim/finance/prototypes.ts).

So caps above 12 stories build fewer homes, for reasons that do not exist in the real world:

  • The tower cliff. At 13 stories or more, a lot of 1,500 m² or more becomes a 5-story podium plus one tower plate of at most 900 m² (TOWER_MIN_M2, TOWER_PLATE_MAX_M2, TOWER_PODIUM_STORIES in src/lib/generator/constants.ts; the plate is set in massTower, src/lib/generator/massing.ts). A 12-story midrise covers 75% of the lot. Lot 2455001 (15,648 m²) holds 1,663 homes at 12 stories and 797 at 13. Nothing lets a developer keep the 12-story building.
  • The size bucket. Above an envelope of 12 stories, 17% of starts take the 0.25 fill (3 stories at a cap of 13), against 2.4% below. That includes small lots that physically top out at a 5-story walkup either way.

Full tree, net homes by 2066: 165.1k at 12 stories, 157.4k at 13, 171.1k at 20. With these two artifacts removed in a data-only test, homes rise with the cap. The measurements are in the taller-caps report (scratchpad taller/REPORT.md), reproduced from dataset 2026-11-25-76c90397.

A cap is a permission: a developer may build up to it. The real decision is which building pays best, and it changes with the construction type. Wood (Type V) goes to about 6 stories and Type III to about 8. Above that the frame must be concrete or steel (Type I), which costs more per home and, at the top, takes longer to build. This spec replaces the fill-fraction rule with that decision and checks the result against San Francisco's own permit records.

Goals, non-goals and anti-goals

Goals

  • A lot's start is one of the buildings it could really hold at or below its cap. A higher cap never removes an option.
  • The building is the one a developer would choose, scored with the model's market terms and real cost and build time by construction type.
  • Large lots can hold the forms real large sites use: a full-coverage Type I block, or several towers.
  • The picks match San Francisco records (built height against the zoned limit, construction type by height, build time by height) within stated tolerances.
  • Every parameter names its evidence tier. Invented values are flagged.
  • Start odds, the size model and the game's measures (LADDER_YEARS, the reach measures) are re-derived on the new yields.

Non-goals

  • A land market. Land prices and expectations that respond to the cap are out of scope. The land cost of a lot is the same for every option on it, so it drops out of the choice.
  • The old scenario engine. Only the world engine (src/lib/sim/world/) and the modules it reads are changed. The scenario engine is being removed in another session.
    Call: World engine only. Old scenario-engine readers are not ported. Tests that fail only because they run the old engine (including simulate.golden) are dropped, as the owner authorised. The branch rebases onto main when the removal merges.
  • Density-bonus projects that build over the cap. They keep today's fitted over-limit amount (sizeOverEnvelope). See the decision.
  • Random bands. The world shows one future, and every draw uses the existing seeded per-lot streams.
  • Adjustable costs. The owner wants a later ability to change costs and similar inputs (cost and value per home by type, λ, the carrying cost) to move break-even. That is a follow-up, not this spec, but it must not be designed out: costs, values, durations and λ are data inputs (the calibration and per-lot dataset files), not constants baked into code.
  • New UI. The card and map show the chosen building. Naming its construction type is optional and must follow the terse-copy rule.

Anti-goals

Anti-goal Do not tune any choice, parameter or tolerance so that homes rise with the cap. "A higher cap never builds fewer homes" is not a goal. If the evidence says some caps build fewer homes than a lower one (a costlier Type I building chosen where it pays, a longer build, or a cap that changes nothing), the model shows that. The test is the record checks, not a shape of the cap curve.
  • Do not keep current law or the game's tuning fixed by adjusting this model. They move, and are re-derived afterwards.
  • Do not fit cost or duration to the model's own output. They come from records or sources outside the model.

The option set

An option is one building: a construction type, a form, a story count and the homes the generator masses for it on the lot. The option set of a lot is every option with stories from 1 to the lot's physical maximum (src/lib/generator/physical.ts, tier 0), pruned as below. A cap S and a density cap select the options with stories ≤ S, with homes clipped by the unit cap as capScenarioYield does today.

Pruning

Option o is dropped when another option o′ on the same lot has stories ≤ o's, homes ≥ o's, cost per home ≤ o's, value per home ≥ o's and build time ≤ o's. Such an o′ beats o at every cap and every value index, so the pruning never changes a choice. In practice a taller option of the same type with fewer homes is always dropped, which removes the tower cliff by construction. The builder measures how many options survive per lot (calls left to the builder).

The choice rule

For option o on lot ℓ, with h homes, cost per home c, value per home v and extra carrying cost per home k (all from the parameter table):

RLV(o, λ) = h × (λ × v − c − k)
λ*(ℓ)     = min over ALL the lot's options of (c + k) / v   # break-even of the best option, independent of the cap
λeff(ℓ)   = max(λ(ℓ), λ*(ℓ))
P(o)      ∝ exp(RLV(o, λeff) / (σ × h̄ × c̄))      # h̄, c̄: the lot's mean option homes and cost, so σ is unitless
  • Land drops out. The lot's land cost is the same for every option on it, so it does not change which option scores highest.
  • A start means the project pencils. The start logit, with today's market shift, decides whether a lot starts. Most lots today have no positive residual at the citywide value (the Controller finds every prototype short). A developer who starts anyway knows something the model does not, so the choice treats the lot as at least at break-even (λeff). At break-even the option that pays best per dollar wins. Above it, the margin times homes decides, and bigger buildings win where values carry them. The rule is continuous in λ.
  • λ*, h̄ and c̄ are properties of the lot. They are taken over all of the lot's options, not the cap-filtered set, and baked at dataset build. So a higher cap never moves λeff, and it never changes the odds between options that were already allowed.
    Call: λ* and the averages h̄ and c̄ are taken over all of a lot's options, as a location property independent of the cap, and computed at dataset build.
  • One future. The draw uses the lot's existing size stream (drawOn(stream, P.SIZE, 0) in world.ts), so a run stays deterministic. As σ approaches 0 the rule becomes the argmax. σ is fitted so that the spread of built height ÷ limit matches the records.
  • Revisions (src/lib/sim/world/stage.ts) move to a neighbouring option on the lot instead of rescaling a fill fraction. The builder keeps the existing ratio draw for the unit count when the option's type is unchanged.
Decision (owner): σ is fitted to the records (built stories ÷ baseStoriesAtFiling), so the choice reproduces the under-building the records show. The draw is deterministic per lot: it uses the lot's seeded size stream like the existing per-lot draws, so the world shows one future with no random bands.

Funded and affordable projects

  • Market-rate projects with public money. They choose like any market project. The choice is made at the start, and money arrives later through the funding line. The money then prices the chosen option by its own type and stories, in place of prototypeFor's tier 4 binning: Type V is A (E under 25 homes), Type III is B, and Type I is C, D or the interpolation. The funded latent sizes (sizeAt and the size draw in src/lib/sim/scenario/funded.ts) read the choice row instead of sizeCum.
  • Affordable (100% affordable, tax-credit) projects. They maximise homes, not land residual. The money is awarded per home, and the cost per home is one figure whatever the building (AFFORDABLE.costPerHome 954,127, BLA Exhibit F, tier 1). So their rule is A: the option with the most homes at or below the cap, priced at the affordable cost. The evidence for a different rule is an inference (tier 2) from how they are paid. The check is per-record: the affordable cohort (startCohort = 'affordable', pipeline/lib/affordable-cohort.ts) should build closer to its limit than the private cohort. The builder reports built height ÷ limit by cohort. If the records show no difference, affordable projects use the market rule instead (decided).
    Call: "No difference" means the affordable and market means of built height ÷ limit are within 2 standard errors of each other (the SE of the difference of the two means).
Decision (owner): Affordable projects use rule A (the most homes at or below the cap) if the cohort check shows they build closer to the limit than market projects, beyond 2 standard errors of the difference of the means. Otherwise they use the market rule.

Baked at dataset build, chosen at run time

  • Baked (pipeline, so a new dataset version): per lot, a fixed-width row of K pruned options, each (type, form, stories, homes); λ per lot; the type cost, value and duration tables in the calibration; the refitted initiation, size (σ) and stage tables. gross.bin is replaced by the option rows, or gains a type dimension (gross-table.ts, pipeline/steps/gross.ts). FZP, base, SB 79 and current-law rows (pipeline/steps/scenario.ts, policy.ts) become option rows filtered at those caps. Changing src/lib/generator/ already changes the dataset version (PATHS in pipeline/lib/version.ts).
  • At run time: for a cap from a lever or a game grant, filter the lot's K options by stories and the unit cap, then compute K exponentials. This builds the choice row in place of sizeCum, memoised per view as precomputeOf is today. That is O(K) per lot with no generator call. The per-project columns level, yields and stories (world.ts, columns.ts) hold the option index and the option's homes.
  • Performance budget. precomputeOf on the live dataset stays within 1.5 times its current time, and the purchase-to-ready wait in bench:map stays within its pass bar (docs/dev/map-perf.md). Both are measured on a quiet machine.

Start odds refit

  • potentialRatio and storiesGap (scenario-codec.ts, input.ts) are taken from the option set at the cap. potentialRatio becomes the expected homes of the choice row ÷ existing homes, and storiesGap becomes the chosen expected stories − existing stories. Both are computed with the same code in the panel and at run time.
  • The panel yields that the initiation fit reads (pipeline/steps/scenario.ts, panel; pipeline/lib/fzp-cal/initiation.ts) are recomputed from option rows at each record's base limit. Then initiation is refitted. calibration.market (today.implied, today.private, the slope) is recomputed from the refitted model, as docs/dev/market.md requires.
  • The best option's margin (RLV at λeff) becomes a start feature (decided). It lets costs and values move starts directly. Its overlap with the market slope is measured and reported in the refit.
  • The size model (pipeline/lib/fzp-cal/size.ts, calibration.size) is replaced by σ, fitted on the same histories (built stories ÷ baseStoriesAtFiling). sizeOverEnvelope stays as a reported count.

Acceptance: record checks

The records are fitted on 2015 onward and checked on the same window, at current law, in the world engine's first ten simulated years against the latest ten record years. Tolerances are two standard errors of the record statistic at its own sample size, so they widen where records are thin.

Measurement to report

This is reporting, not a gate. Report net completed homes (completedNet) at 2040 (month 168) and 2066 (month 479) for current law, and for current law with one transit rule (all stop types, 805 m) at 12, 13, 16, 20, 40 and 80 stories. Report the full tree at the same caps too. Put each figure beside the old dataset's from the taller-caps report, with the share of starts by construction type at each cap. A cap that builds fewer homes than a lower one is reported with the reason the records support. It is not fixed.
