# `finance-score`: homes by income tier and public money in the results

Package: `sim-financing` ([design](../design/2026-11-09-1911-sim-financing.md), approved 2026-11-09, amended at the reconciliation of the same day). Research: [financing evidence digest](../research/2026-11-09-financing-evidence.md). This spec owns interface rows F13 and F14 and builds on F1, F4 to F6 and F8 to F10 and F12 as written.

Evidence tiers used throughout: 0 per-record data, 1 official aggregate or law, 2 inference, 3 literature or model, 4 invented. "Verified" means read in the primary document or the code during the writing of this spec; "inferred" means reasoned, not read.

## 1. Scope

The design's Subsystems row:

| Slug | Scope |
| --- | --- |
| `finance-score` | Results: homes by tier per month against the tier targets, the pool series, public cost and cost per home, repayment owed, the legal-limit statements. Extends `TimelineScore` and the scoreboard's `Target`. |

**In:**

- `RHNA_TIER_TARGETS` (the four RHNA 2023–2031 income-tier targets) in `src/lib/scoreboard/`. Step 0 declares `Target.tier?`, `TimelineScore.tiers?`, `TimelineScore.money?` and `LimitStatement` (design "Dependency order", item 0); this spec adds their behaviour, not the declarations.
- `tiersOf`: one completed schedule's homes by income tier, and the per-month tier series built from it, netted the same way `completedNet` is.
- `TimelineScore.tiers`: the series, the baseline by tier and the four `TargetProgressAt` entries. Present on every run.
- `TimelineScore.money`: the per-pool series built from the engine's ledger events, `fundedHomes`, `displacedHomes`, `publicSpent`, `owed` and `limits`. Present only on a run with at least one pool.
- Pure derived helpers that the page reads: public money per added home (`perAddedHome`, F14), the City's share of the money, the repayment owed up to a month, the share of funded homes displaced, and the label of the tier baseline.
- The provenance lists the page's Sources read for tiers and money (`TIER_SOURCES`, `MONEY_SOURCES`), including the engine's `PIPELINE_FUNDED` (F9 (l)) and the statement that a pipeline project's recorded 0 affordable homes may mean "not stated" (F13).
- The assembly of the `Borrowing[]` that `finance-model`'s `limitStatements` measures: the borrowing the policy adds (F14).
- Collecting the ledger in `simulateDetailed` (F10), and computing tiers and money once per shown run in the scenario host.
- The tests that lock all of this, the update of the `TimelineScore` test fixture, and the developer doc.

**Out:**

- Anything the page renders: the Results rows, the Sources entries, labels and formatting of dollars. These belong to `funding-ui`, which reads what this spec posts.
- The engine's funded starts, the queue, the award and the ledger events themselves (`finance-engine`, F9 to F11), and `PIPELINE_FUNDED` itself (the engine exports it; this spec lists it).
- `fundBundle`, `INCLUSIONARY` and `inclusionaryUnits`, `DISPLACEMENT_RANGE`, `debtService` and `limitStatements` with its texts (`finance-model`). This spec decides when those are called and on what.
- `affordableHomes`, `rhnaCompletedNet.byTier` and the baseline programmes' `tiers` (`finance-data`, F12).
- Homes by tier for the twin (design "Out of scope"). The band, the twin and the plan effect are unchanged; the band stays on `completedNet` (F14).

**Hands to:** `funding-ui` gets `TimelineScore.tiers` and `TimelineScore.money` on every `timeline` message, `RHNA_TIER_TARGETS` with their sources, `TIER_SOURCES` and `MONEY_SOURCES`, and the helpers of sections 4.3, 4.6 and 4.7. `finance-levers` gets nothing from this spec, and this spec reads nothing of `FinanceLevers`: what the policy adds is read from the shown run's input against the twin's (section 4.8).

## 3. Global constraints

- **Evidence hierarchy.** Every number the simulation uses carries a `Provenance` with its tier and source, and the interface can show both. A value that cannot be sourced is `Unsourced` with a reason; what depends on it is unavailable, or runs on a value labelled "custom, not sourced" that the user typed. No tier 3 or 4 number is presented as data.
- **Money never decides alone.** A funded project needs a lot the user's zoning makes hostable and free, takes its size from the same draw, and passes through the same stage tables as every other project. Only a fully funded project is exempt from drop-out (R6).
- **Deterministic and paired.** A run is a pure function of (dataset, levers, seed). The plan and the twin share draws: no existing draw purpose changes, and a lot's draws do not depend on the order lots are processed in.
- **No public money means no change.** With `finance` absent, the engine's output is identical, schedule for schedule, to its output before `finance-engine` under the same `market`. A test asserts it. The site's default is not that case: it runs the baseline funding.
- **The engine is in the dataset version.** `src/lib/sim/scenario`, `src/lib/sim/levers`, `src/lib/scoreboard`, `src/lib/timelapse` and `pipeline/` are hashed into the dataset version. The finance model shapes published data too, because the `/lesscars` build step runs the baseline funding: `src/lib/sim/finance/` (the functions and constants of F4 and F8) is added to the hashed paths in `pipeline/lib/version.ts` by `market-baseline`, the first subsystem whose pipeline code imports it. Any change in a hashed path needs a dataset build and upload before signoff ([pipeline.md](../../dev/pipeline.md), "Finishing a branch that changes the dataset"). The page's code stays outside them, in `src/lib/sim-page/`.
- **Goldens change only at the two releases.** The pipeline fixture goldens, the lesscars fixture goldens and `fixture-0` are regenerated by their documented commands for Release 1 (`market-baseline` with `finance-data`) and for Release 2 (`finance-engine`, when the default starts running the baseline funding), each reviewed as an engine or data change. The fixture build emits a fixture market model and fixture baseline funding, labelled tier 4, so the page and its e2e tests have both. `golden-seed1.json` and `model-golden.json` (the mini city and the small calibration, which have no market model and no funding) never change: they are the lock on "no public money means no change".
- **Open data only.** The dataset stays ODbL and PDDL. New sources go in the lock and the attribution list.

## 4. Behaviour

### 4.1 `tiersOf`: one completed schedule's homes by tier

`tiersOf(sc, ctx)` splits the schedule's **final** units, `sc.revisions[sc.revisions.length - 1].units`, into a `TierUnits` whose five fields sum exactly to those units. It is gross: existing homes are netted in the series (section 4.2), never here. The series calls it only for a schedule with `end.kind === 'complete'`; on any other schedule it returns the same split (it is pure), and nothing reads it.

The final revision gives the homes split and the inclusionary size band, because the homes counted are the homes built: a project revised from 40 to 24 homes before construction is a 10–24-home project (verified: revisions end at construction, `types.ts:46`). Only case 1's weights read the start size, because the money was awarded and committed at the start size (F9 (c), (i)); a certain project is never revised (F9 (i)), so for most funded affordable projects the two are the same.

The cases, tested in this order:

1. **A funded affordable project** (`sc.kind === 'affordable'`, F10): the homes are split by the `tiers` of the programmes that pay it, weighted by the grant-equivalent each pays (F13, OD-F10). Exactly:
   1. `ids = sc.funding.programmes` (F10: only the programmes that pay the project, in `finance.programmes` order). If the schedule has no `funding`, or `ids` is empty, every home is `unrecorded` (neither should happen: an affordable start is always awarded with at least one paying programme, F9 (c)). An id missing from `ctx.programmes` throws `Error('tiersOf: unknown programme <id>')`: the ids come from the same input, so this is a defect, never data.
   2. **One paying programme:** its weight is 1, and the shares are its `tiers`. `fundBundle` is not called (it would give the same shares).
   3. **Several:** `p = prototypeFor(r0.stories ?? 0, r0.units)` with `r0 = sc.revisions[0]`, the schedule's start size (F9 (c) and (i): the award and the commitments are at the start size; a simulated schedule always has numeric stories at its start, `engine.ts:943-944`). `b = fundBundle(ids.map((id) => ctx.programmes.get(id).item), 'affordable', p, ctx.terms)`. The weight of programme `k` is `w_k = b.items[k].grantEquivalent ÷ b.grantEquivalent`. This re-prices exactly the engine's award: the awarded set less its non-paying items. Under F8's three tiers (federal, state, the City's) a grant with a positive share pays 0 only when nothing remains when its tier is reached; then every grant of that tier and of the later tiers pays 0 as well. So the non-paying items never change what an earlier tier pays, and within a tier that pays anything every grant pays a positive amount, so none of its items is dropped (inferred from the row text). Within the City's tier the paying grants share what remains in proportion to their shares, so a user's City grant stacked on the baseline lowers what each baseline City programme pays, and the weights follow. For `affordable` the result does not depend on `p`: every quantity F8 uses for that kind (`uncoveredPerHome('affordable')`, `costPerHome('affordable')`, `AFFORDABLE.ownPerHome`) is a constant of F4 (inferred from the row text); `p` is passed because `fundBundle` takes one.
   4. **All weights zero** (`b.grantEquivalent` is 0: possible only when every paying item is a loan or a guarantee that saves the project nothing under F8, such as a loan at or above the construction rate with no lower `requiredReturn`): the paying programmes are weighted equally. This is a tier 4 convention, named as such in the provenance; it agrees with step 2 (one paying programme counts in full whatever it is worth) and still never guesses a tier the programmes do not set.
   5. The shares are `s_t = Σ_k w_k × tiers_k[t]` for each tier `t` of `INCOME_TIERS`, plus `s_unrecorded = Σ_k w_k` over the programmes whose `tiers` is null (F6 requires `tiers` for `reach: 'affordable'`, so this is 0 on any valid input). The shares are memoised in `ctx.weights` by the sorted ids.
   6. The final units are split by these five shares with the rule "Splitting by shares" below, `unrecorded` placed after `aboveModerate` in the tie order.

   With the baseline funding alone every baseline programme carries the same `tiers`, the MOHCD mix (F12; `finance-data` section 4.5 expects very low 0.483439, low 0.433384, moderate 0.076402, above moderate 0.006775 on the live data, tier 0, query of 2026-11-09, inferred from that spec, not re-queried here), so the weights change nothing until a user programme with other `tiers` pays part of a project.

   **Worked example** (hand values, the test of section 6 item 4). A 101-home affordable project is paid by programme `a` (grant-equivalent 600,000 a home, `tiers` very low 0.6, low 0.4) and programme `b` (200,000 a home, `tiers` very low 0.2, low 0.5, moderate 0.3). Weights 0.75 and 0.25. Shares: very low 0.5, low 0.425, moderate 0.075, above moderate 0, unrecorded 0. Exact homes 50.5, 42.925, 7.575, 0, 0; floors 50, 42, 7, 0, 0 (99); the two homes left go to the largest remainders, low (0.925) and moderate (0.575). Result: very low 50, low 43, moderate 8, above moderate 0, unrecorded 0, summing to 101. A tie: 3 homes, two programmes of equal weight with `tiers` all very low and all low: exact 1.5 and 1.5, floors 1 and 1, the one home left goes to very low (the earlier tier): 2 and 1.

2. **Any other simulated project** (`origin: 'simulated'`, kind market, funded or not): `inclusionaryUnits(units)` (F13; `finance-model`), with `units` the final units, gives the on-site homes by tier; the rest are `aboveModerate`. Everything else is `finance-model`'s and is not restated or re-tested here beyond one case per band: the tenure (the default `'rental'`, `ASSUMED_TENURE`, tier 2: the Controller's prototypes are rental apartments), all zeros under 10 homes, the total rounded half up and split by largest remainder (`finance-model` section 4.6), and the rates held at their November 1, 2026 level for the whole run (tier 1, Controller Triennial 2026 pp. 7–8 and Budget Analyst Exhibit J p. 11, verified by `finance-model`; the scheduled rise is not applied). So there is no filing-day argument: the schedule's dates do not enter.
3. **A pipeline project** (`origin: 'pipeline'`, including a phase and an unenriched multi-phase row): `unrecorded = min(a, units)` where `a = ctx.affordable.get(sc.projectId) ?? 0`; the rest `aboveModerate` (F13). So:
   - `affordableHomes` **null** (not recorded) or absent counts as 0: the homes are `aboveModerate`. This is the one place a missing record becomes a number; the alternative, counting all the homes as `unrecorded`, would claim they are income-restricted, which the record does not say either. The developer doc states it.
   - **Zero:** all `aboveModerate`. The Planning field is never blank in the source (53,747 zeros, 945 positives and no blanks on the 2026-10-17 lock; tier 0, `finance-data` concern 4, inferred from that spec), so a recorded 0 may mean "not stated" rather than "none". The count follows the record; `TIER_SOURCES` (section 4.3) says so.
   - **More than the homes** (the record's proposed count can exceed a revised or as-built count): capped at the final units, so a 100% affordable pipeline project is all `unrecorded`.
   - **A revision of size** keeps the recorded count (capped as above), not a proportional one: the record gives a count, not a share (tier 0 kept as recorded).
   - **Phases.** A master's `affordableHomes` is allocated over its phases in proportion to `phases[j].units`, by largest remainder, ties to the lower phase index; each phase's share is then capped at that phase's final units as above. An unenriched multi-phase row (`phases` null), simulated as one schedule with `projectId` the row's id (`engine.ts:642`), takes its `affordableHomes` whole. A master never completes anything (`score.ts:144-147`), so nothing is counted for the master itself.

**Splitting by shares** (case 1; case 2 uses `finance-model`'s own split): for `n` homes and shares `s_f` over the five fields in the order very low, low, moderate, above moderate, unrecorded, each field gets `floor(n × s_f + 1e-9)`; the homes left over go one each to the fields with the largest fractional remainders `n × s_f − floor(…)`, two remainders within 10⁻⁹ of each other being a tie, ties to the earlier field in that order (F13: "ties to the earlier tier"). The result sums exactly to `n`. The `1e-9` guards against `0.29 × 100` style float error, as `formatPercent` does (`scoreboard/format.ts:17-21`); it and the tie tolerance are floating-point tolerances, not model numbers.

### 4.2 The tier series

`tierSeries` returns `completed: TierUnits[]`, one entry per month of `TimelineScore.months`, cumulative, at the end of each month, with exactly the month rules of `shownSeries` (`score.ts:178-225`, `monthIndexer` `score.ts:37-49`):

- At a construction start after `asOf` (`entered.construction > asOf`), `sc.existingUnits` is subtracted from `aboveModerate` in that month: the same event and the same condition as `addNet` (`score.ts:134`).
- At completion, `tiersOf(sc, ctx)` is added in the completion month (`score.ts:135`).
- Phases are pipeline schedules; masters add nothing.

**Why existing homes are netted against above moderate.** The lots' existing homes carry no income-restriction record in the dataset (`parcels.bin` has counts only), and netting them elsewhere would be a guess. Netting against `aboveModerate` keeps the identity below exact and leaves the three lower tiers counting only homes the records or the law put there (tier 2 inference, stated in the developer doc and offered to `funding-ui` for the Sources text). `aboveModerate` can therefore be negative in a month, as `completedNet` can; it is uncapped.

**The identity.** For every month `i` and every run: `veryLow + low + moderate + aboveModerate + unrecorded` of `completed[i]` equals `shown[i].completedNet`. Tests assert it (section 6).

### 4.4 `TimelineScore.money`: the ledger series

Present when the shown run's input has `finance` with at least one pool (F14); absent otherwise (every run of a dataset whose calibration lacks `baselineFunding` or `market`, F9, and any run before `finance-engine`). From Release 2 the default `/sim` run and every `/s/<id>` run carry it, because `buildSimInput` sets the baseline funding (F9). (`/fzp` is no longer a page: it redirects to `/sim`, `src/routes/fzp/+page.ts:1-12`.)

Each pool of `SimInput.finance.pools`, in that order, gets one entry `{ id, label, kind, received, committed, out, spent, repaid, lost, lostCredit }`, each series with one value per month, at the end of the month, in whole constant dollars. A ledger event lands in the month `idx(day)`; an event before `months[0]` lands in month 0 (as `monthOf`, `score.ts:79`); an event after the horizon is dropped (the engine should emit none).

The engine's event protocol this spec builds on (F9 (g), (h); restated so the series are defined; the engine's table is `finance-engine` section 4.7). Every event but `in` carries the `programme` it belongs to, one event per programme of a project (F10); `in` carries `programme: null`. The instrument of an event is the `instrument.kind` of the programme with id `e.programme` in `SimInput.finance.programmes` (the `finance` that `moneyOf` receives).

| Moment | Event |
| --- | --- |
| An inflow arrives | `in` |
| A funded project is awarded: its grant, its loan principal, its guarantee reserve | `commit` |
| It enters construction: the grant is paid, the loan principal goes out | `spend` (the committed dollars move) |
| It exits before construction | `release` of what is committed |
| It completes: a loan comes back; a guarantee reserve is freed | `repay` (loan), `release` (reserve) |
| It exits after construction began | grant: `loss` of `terms.lossOnExit` of the grant (a mark on money already spent); loan: `loss` of that share of the principal and `repay` of the rest; guarantee: `loss` of that share of the guaranteed principal, capped at the reserve, and `release` of the reserve |

The series, with `C, R, S, P, L` the cumulative `commit`, `release`, `spend`, `repay` and `loss` dollars of the pool through the month, and a subscript naming the instrument of the event's programme:

- `received[i]`: cumulative `in`. Always 0 for an uncapped pool, which has no inflows (F5).
- `committed[i]`: a stock, the dollars committed and not yet spent, lent or released: `C − R − S`.
- `spent[i]`: cumulative grants paid: `S_grant`. Grants do not come back.
- `out[i]`: a stock, loan principal outstanding: `S_loan − P − L_loan`.
- `repaid[i]`: cumulative `repay`.
- `lost[i]`: cumulative `loss`, every event of the pool, `L_grant + L_loan + L_guarantee` (F14). A loan's or a guarantee's loss is money that will not come back. A grant's loss is a grant paid to a project that then dropped out during construction: it is also in `spent`, and is shown so the results say how much was spent on homes never finished (OD-F8, tier 4: the whole amount spent is counted lost).

- `lostCredit[i]`: cumulative `loss` on loans and guarantees, `L_loan + L_guarantee`, classed by each event's `programme` (F14): the part of `lost` that enters F9 (f), and the part added to `spent` when public money spent is totalled, so a lost grant is never counted twice. Posted with the other series.

`committed` and `out` are the amounts outstanding at the end of the month; the other four are cumulative (F14).

**Identities**, asserted on every pool, every month, by the property tests. `finance-engine` owns the ledger and states the ledger identities in its section 4.7; the ones below restate them on the monthly series and keep this spec's own numbers. I2 is the engine's conservation of commitments, I3 its conservation of loan principal, I4 its available money by F9 (f), and I5 its rule that a loss never exceeds what was paid or held; I1 and I6 are properties of the series alone.

- I1. `received`, `spent`, `repaid`, `lost` and `lostCredit` never decrease; `committed ≥ 0`; `out ≥ 0`.
- I2. `C = R + S + committed` (every committed dollar is still committed, released or spent).
- I3. `S_loan = out + repaid + L_loan` (every lent dollar is outstanding, back or lost).
- I4. For a pool that is not uncapped, the money it has in hand is never negative: `leverage × received − committed − spent − out − lostCredit ≥ 0`, exactly F9 (f)'s available money (F9 (f)'s `lost` is the losses on loans and guarantees only; a grant's loss is already in `spent`).
- I5. `lost = lostCredit + L_grant`, and `L_grant ≤ S_grant = spent` (a grant loss never exceeds what was paid).
- I6. For an uncapped pool, `received` is 0 in every month.

### 4.5 Funded and displaced homes

- `fundedHomes[i]`: Σ over schedules with `funding` that completed by month `i` (completion month ≤ `i`) of `finalUnits − existingUnits`. Counted at completion, so unlike `completedNet` it does not take the existing homes off at the construction start; it is a count of what funded projects added, not a component of `completedNet`.
- `displacedHomes[i]`: Σ of `funding.displaced` (F10) over the same schedules.
- Pipeline rows are never funded (F9 (l)); the rule keys on `funding` being present, not on `origin`, so a later package that funds the pipeline needs no change here.

Both are counts of net homes, so `fundedHomes[i] − displacedHomes[i]` is the funded homes the run adds beyond what the displaced private starts would have built on the same lots. The displaced start's own completion date is not tracked; its homes are counted in the month the funded project completes (tier 2 simplification, stated in the developer doc). `DISPLACEMENT_RANGE` is not copied into the score; the page imports it from `finance-model`.

### 4.6 Public money spent and per added home

`publicSpent[i]` = Σ over **all** pools (City, state, federal, public bank) of `spent[i] + lostCredit[i]`, the same sum that `moneyOf` posts. It does not use `lost`, which also holds grant losses already in `spent` and would count them twice. It is the public money that is gone: grants paid, plus loans and guarantee calls not recovered. Outstanding loans are not in it (they come back); committed money is not in it (not yet paid). Every pool counts because all of it is public money and the design's goal is "what it does to the public purse"; the City's share is derived by the helper below, so the page can show either without a second series.

**Public money per added home** (derived, not stored, F14):

`perAddedHome(money, i, scope: 'all' | 'city'): number | null` = `spent_scope[i] ÷ (fundedHomes[i] − displacedHomes[i])`. F14's "money spent" is read as `publicSpent` (money that is gone, losses on loans and guarantees included), rounded to whole dollars (half up), where `spent_scope` is `publicSpent[i]` for `'all'` and the same sum over City pools for `'city'`. City pools are the kinds `goBond`, `cop`, `generalRevenue`, `dedicatedTax` and `bank` (F5: `state` and `federal` are not the City's).

- **"Added" is defined within the shown run, not against the twin.** The twin carries the unscaled baseline (F9), so in the default run the twin and the plan spend the same money and add the same homes: a twin difference would be 0 ÷ 0 exactly when the user most wants to know what today's funding buys. The run's own counterfactual per lot (`displaced`, F10) already nets out the private homes the money pre-empted.
- **At the scrubbed month**, so the figure moves with the timeline.
- **Edge cases:** a denominator of 0 or less returns null (the page shows "no homes added yet"; it is never shown as zero or infinite). A numerator of 0 with a positive denominator returns 0. Money spent on projects not yet complete is in the numerator while their homes are not yet in the denominator, so the figure is high early in a run and settles as projects complete; the developer doc says so.
- Expected magnitude, as a sanity check for reviewers, not a test value: at the design's baseline shares a fully funded affordable home costs the City $300,027 and all sources $861,525 (tier 1, Budget Analyst Exhibit F, verified by the orchestrator), before displacement.

`cityOf(money, i): number` returns the City-pool sum of `spent[i] + lostCredit[i]`, exact from the posted series. `perAddedHome(…, 'city')` uses it. `displacedShare(money, i): number | null` returns `displacedHomes[i] ÷ fundedHomes[i]`, null when `fundedHomes[i] ≤ 0`, for the page to set beside `DISPLACEMENT_RANGE`. All the helpers are pure functions of the posted `money` object, so the page can call them on its copy.

**`MONEY_SOURCES: readonly Provenance[]`**, exported from `finance-score.ts` for the page's Sources on money (`funding-ui` renders it under "Money details"), in this order: the engine's `PIPELINE_FUNDED` (F9 (l): pipeline projects are taken as already funded, tier 4; imported from `finance-engine`, not copied); the definition of public money per added home (tier 2: "money spent, grants paid and loans and guarantees lost, over the homes funded projects completed less the private homes they displaced on the same lots, counted when the funded project completes. Homes displaced by funded projects that dropped out or are still being built at the end of the run are not counted."); `DISPLACEMENT_RANGE.provenance` (tier 3, from `finance-model`); and the repayment owed (tier 2, written here: "Level yearly payments at the pool's rate over its term, the method of the Budget Analyst's Exhibit 40 (2026-06-05, p. 106). Nominal dollars: the one figure here not in constant dollars."). The limit statements carry their own `provenance` and are not repeated here.

### 4.7 Repayment owed

`owed` has one entry for each pool with `debt` non-null (the `goBond` and `cop` pools, F5), in pool order, including one whose principal is 0:

- `pool`: the pool's id.
- `principal`: `received` at the last month: the bonds issued by the horizon. Money borrowed is owed whether or not it was spent; a bond's inflow is its issue (F5).
- `repayment`: `debtService(principal, pool.debt).total` (F14; `finance-model` section 4.8), the total of level annual payments, whole dollars as that function rounds them. At 6% over 20 years, $410,442,573 gives 715,685,078 in all, 1.7437 per dollar (tier 2 arithmetic on the Budget Analyst's assumed terms, themselves tier 2, ruling R19; Budget Analyst Exhibit 40, p. 106, verified to the dollar by `finance-model`). Payments that fall after the horizon are included.
- `nominal: true`: the repayment is the one nominal figure (global constraint "Constant dollars").

`owed` covers every debt pool, the baseline's `city-debt` pool (kind `goBond`, `finance-data` section 4.5) included: money borrowed is owed whoever decided to borrow it. The limit statements, by contrast, measure only what the policy adds (section 4.8).

`owedAt(o: owed entry, received: number): Dollars` (this signature is the one `funding-ui` calls, as `owedAt(entry, money.pools[k].received[i])` with `k` the pool of `entry.pool`) returns the repayment at the scrubbed month, `Math.round(o.repayment × received ÷ o.principal)` with `received` the pool's `received[i]`, and 0 when `o.principal` is 0 (then `received[i]` is 0 in every month, because `received` never decreases). `debtService` is linear in the principal before its one rounding, so this differs from `debtService(received[i], debt).total` by at most a dollar (inferred from `finance-model` section 4.8's formula), and the page needs no debt terms.

### 4.8 Legal-limit statements

`limits = limitStatements(addedBorrowing(plan.finance, baseline, asOf, horizon))` (F14; `finance-model` section 4.8). They are computed from the borrowing the policy adds, not from the run's outcomes, and are consequences, never limits (OD-F6): nothing here changes a run. `finance-model` decides which statements appear, their order (`goRate`, `goCap`, `goVote`, `copCap`), their texts and `exceeded`; `goVote.exceeded` is true whenever it is shown (F14).

**`addedBorrowing(plan, baseline, asOf, horizon): Borrowing[]`**, where `plan` is the shown run's `SimInput.finance` and `baseline` the twin's `finance.pools` (the unscaled baseline, F9). For each pool of `plan.pools` of kind `goBond` or `cop`, in `plan.pools` order:

- `arrived(pool) = Σ over pool.inflows of Σ over inflowArrivals(inflow, asOf, horizon) of the arrival's dollars` (F5; `dollars` for a `once` inflow, `dollarsPerYear` for each arrival of a `yearly` one).
- **An added pool** (its `id` is not the id of a pool in `baseline`; by F7 and F12 these are exactly the ids starting `u-`): `principal = arrived(pool)`.
- **A baseline debt pool** (same `id` in `baseline`): `principal = max(0, arrived(pool) − arrived(baselinePool))`, what the scaled pool receives above its unscaled amount. A pool scaled down, or switched off, adds nothing. The difference is taken over the whole run, not arrival by arrival, because a bond authorisation and the Charter cap are totals: a scale of 0.5 until 2029 and 2 after it adds only the net (tier 2 reading of F14, stated in the developer doc).
- An entry is emitted only when `principal > 0`, as `{ pool: pool.id, kind: pool.kind, principal, debt: pool.debt }`. `debt` is non-null for these kinds (F5).

The principal is computed from the inflows, the policy's input, and not from the ledger, so the statements are the same in every seed and do not wait for the run (F14 "not from the run's outcomes"). The inflows already carry the scale (F17), and a pool switched off has its inflows scaled to 0 for that span (F7), so `arrived` needs no `off` lookup. With the default policy `plan.finance` equals the twin's (F17), every baseline difference is 0, and `limits` is `[]`: the default run states no limit, and the baseline's own bond programme, which is already authorised and held level (OD-F3), never reads as a new borrowing.

### 4.9 Which runs carry what, and the cost

| Run | `tiers` | `money` | Computed by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shown run (`/sim`, `/s/<id>`) | yes, every run | when `finance` has a pool | `shownRun` (`scenario-host.ts:1103-1108`), once per run, from `simulateDetailed` |
| Twin | no | no | `simulateNet`, unchanged |
| Band runs (101) | no | no | `simulateNet`, unchanged |
| `/lesscars` build | no | no | `addNet` and `runEngine`, unchanged |

- `simulateDetailed` (`simulate.ts:23-43`) is the one collector of the ledger (F10: the engine adds none). It passes a `ledger` sink that pushes each event in arrival order and returns them as `ledger` (`[]` when `finance` is absent, and no `ledger` sink is passed then, so a run without finance is untouched).
- **Order with `finance-levers`.** This subsystem runs before `finance-levers` (design "Dependency order", item 4): both edit `scenario-host.ts`, and `finance-levers` builds on this spec's changes to `shownRun` and `postTimeline`.
- `shownRun` computes `run.tiers` with `tierSeries` and `run.money` with `moneyOf` (passing `w.loaded.twin.finance?.pools ?? []` as `baseline`), after `shownSeries`, and `postTimeline` (`scenario-host.ts:1183-1215`) passes both to `assembleScore` on every post: revision 0 and every band-only update carry the same values (the same objects; they do not change during a run).
- `TierCtx` is built when the loaded input changes (`scenario-host.ts:921`, a lever revision), not per run. `addedBorrowing` depends only on the plan and twin inputs, so `limits` may be computed once per revision too (internal).
- **The net fast path computes neither, and gains nothing.** `addNet`, `NetAcc` and `simulateNet` are unchanged, so the band, the twin and `/lesscars` cost exactly what they cost now.
- **Cost budget** (tier 4, set here so it is checkable): on the bench city with the bench funding set (F18), `tierSeries` plus `moneyOf` add at most 15 ms to the shown run, measured in `scenario.bench.spec.ts`. Both are linear: tiers O(schedules + 5 × months) plus one `fundBundle` call per distinct set of paying programmes (memoised in `ctx.weights`; at most 24 added plus the baseline's programmes a call, `finance-model` section 4.5), money O(events + 7 × pools × months). The posted money is at most 20 pools (12 added, F7, plus the baseline's 8: six City pools, the state pool and the federal pool, `finance-data` section 4.5) × 7 series × 483 months, about 68,000 numbers per post, which structured clone handles without a measurable stall (inferred).

**`/lesscars`.** Nothing it reads changes: its build step uses `addNet` and `runEngine` (`pipeline/lib/lesscars/runs.ts:47`, `243`), not `TimelineScore`. Because `src/lib/sim/scenario` and `src/lib/scoreboard` are hashed into the dataset version (`pipeline/lib/version.ts:47-49`), this subsystem changes the dataset version. Its branch merges into the Release 2 branch with no upload and no signoff of its own; the dataset is built and uploaded once, at the release, on the release branch (design "How a release is built"; [pipeline.md](../../dev/pipeline.md), "Finishing a branch that changes the dataset"); the `/lesscars` files it produces are byte-identical apart from the version, which a reviewer checks by diffing them.

## 5. Seams

- **Market scenarios and a market control (R7).** Nothing here reads the market. Every money series is per month over the run's own months, so dated market steps change the numbers and nothing else. Nothing to do.
- **The pipeline and drop-out (R1, R8).** Pipeline tiers come only from `affordableHomes`. `fundedHomes`, `displacedHomes` and the money series key on `Schedule.funding` and on ledger events, never on `origin`, so a later package that funds pipeline rows is counted with no change to this module. Until then the engine's `PIPELINE_FUNDED` assumption (pipeline rows taken as already funded, tier 4, F9 (l)) is the first entry of `MONEY_SOURCES`, so the results say that the pipeline's money is not in the pool series; a later package that funds the pipeline removes that entry. Drop-out is not read here.
- **A rent model (R4).** The tiers are counts of homes against RHNA's income bands; no rent, price or affordability figure is computed or stored. A later rent model would add its own fields beside `tiers`.
