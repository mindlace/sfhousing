What the app tells people, and when: review and proposal

An inventory of everything Simulate and Paint convey, ranked by what a person must see, must understand or may discover, with a proposal for where each thing goes.

Why this exists

The first redesign pass tidied spacing and removed duplicate timelines. The owner's verdict was "a modest improvement": it rearranged the screen without asking what the screen is for. This document starts from the content instead.

The owner's ranking rule, which every decision below is tested against:

The user must see the effects of their choices on housing and understand how they can make those choices. They can learn/discover where the data comes from, the assumptions the sim runs on, etc.

Other direction from the owner that this proposal takes as given:

  • Keep the timeline view of policy.
  • Drop Fewer cars from the top navigation for now. The route stays.
  • No undo and redo buttons on desktop.
  • The painted-areas list is an undo stack more than layers.
  • Showing the whole 40 years at once takes away from watching it grow.
  • New projects start with no fanfare, sometimes off the visible map. They may deserve a halo that fades.
  • One data-and-sources place, unless credits must sit on the map.
  • The header number and the Results number should not disagree about what date they are for.
  • "FZP" becomes "current law" or similar plain words.
  • Progress bars should show from further out and keep one size on screen at every zoom.
Status This is a discussion draft. The owner has decided the clock, the reveal rule, the game as the frame, and the four questions first asked (see Decided). The rest is open; see Questions for the owner.

What comes straight after

The owner's next project after this one reworks funding (several sources, no funding that lasts forever, editable amounts) and adds market conditions such as booms and busts. This work does not build either. It must leave room for both:

  • The Public money rows must read well with many sources, each with its own dates.
  • Market conditions become something the person sets on the timeline, not a single fixed row.
  • No money figure is given a permanent main-screen position that the funding rework would have to undo.

The frame: an incremental game

The owner wants the app to work like an incremental game. The aim each time is to get to "enough houses". The person reaches the goal, gets a victory, receives an unlock, and does it all again with more to work with. They start with Paint, where enough is easy to reach. Then policy, which is harder. Then funding.

Later the person will pick an upgrade after each victory. For now they are simply given an unlock or two each time they hit the goal.

"Enough" is the state target today, as a stand-in. Later it must respond to population change, and it will become a ladder of harder goals, such as "10% cheaper housing" or "zero homelessness".

This changes what the screen is for. It is a run at a goal, and everything on it is read against that goal. The design is set out in The loop. Unlocking is not built in this work; the design only has to fit it.

How items are ranked

Each thing the app conveys is put in one of five tiers. A tier says where the item may live and how much attention it may take.

The five tiers and the placement rule each one carries
TierWhat belongs in itPlacement rule
SeeAn effect of the person's choice on housing: homes, projects, where and when. Also the goal and how close they are to it.Always on screen. Moves the moment they act or time plays. Never behind a click.
DoA way to make a choice: add or change a policy, paint, move time.A visible control with a word on it. An empty state says the next step.
ReadThe few words needed to read a See item correctly: its date, the target, what it is compared with.Next to the See item, in a few words. Never its own panel.
FindBackground: breakdowns, money mechanics, assumptions, sources, credits, shortcuts.One step away, in one of two fixed places: Details (the numbers behind an effect) or About this model (data, assumptions, credits, help).
AlertSystem state that needs attention: loading, stale, failed, refused, not saved.Appears only while abnormal, visibly, with the action to take. Normal state is silent or nearly so.

Urgency is the second axis. Live items change as the person acts or plays. On change items matter once, when something happens. On demand items matter only when asked for. A See item is live by definition; a Find item is on demand by definition.

Main findings

1. The page runs on two clocks

One series (homes completed since June 2022, actual plus simulated) appears in eight places. The header reads it at the end of the window, about 2066, and never moves during Play. The Results headline, the chart and the target rows read it at the playhead. The header's meter reads a third date, the January 2031 deadline, and has no visible label. The screen reader text in the header speaks the playhead number while the visible text shows the 2066 number.

The effect: the largest number on the page ignores the main control on the page.

2. The effect of the person's own choice is the quietest thing shown

  • The policy's effect is a small "vs FZP +5,580" in the header, at 2066 only, and two near-identical lines in Results that show the same value one line apart.
  • "FZP" is never expanded on the main screen. Nothing says what the plan is.
  • The comparison silently changes meaning. With no policy it compares the plan with the old zoning; with any policy it compares the policy with the plan.
  • On the map, a project that exists because of the person's policy looks like every other project. The card has an "Allowed by" line, so the link is known; it is not drawn.
  • Starts and completions happen without any signal, at the default zoom and off screen alike.

3. Neither page says how to make a choice

  • A first visit to Simulate shows three closed rows reading "0" with a "+" and no words. The page's only heading is for screen readers.
  • A first visit to Paint has no instruction. The one sentence, "Paint a height on the map to add homes.", is on the hidden Results face. On a phone the tool starts as Move with the panel closed.
  • Publish is disabled with a reason only screen readers get.

4. The future is drawn before it is played

Both charts draw all 40 years about three seconds after the page opens. Play only moves a cursor along a finished picture and swaps solid for dashed. The map is the only thing that grows.

5. Background takes main-screen space, and context is buried

  • On screen by default with no key: transit-capacity shading, zoning height numerals, a credits line, a footer, public-money lines, a second chart with five series and three published estimates.
  • Two levels deep: the only legend for the map's stage colours (Results, then Sources, then Map key).
  • Hidden at the default zoom: the per-project progress bars.
  • Links to the data page appear in four places with three names.

6. Paint and Simulate look like two views of one thing and are not

A painted plan never reaches Simulate. Paint's number is every eligible lot rebuilt to the maximum, today. Simulate's is a forecast over time. They have equal weight in the navigation, share the word "homes added", and no copy on either page contrasts them. The word "Paint" labels five different controls on the Paint page.

7. Some effects and failures are told only to screen readers

  • What a brush stroke did: lots covered, new buildings, lots skipped as ineligible.
  • A stroke refused because the plan is too large. A sighted person sees nothing happen.
  • What undo and redo just did.

Proposal

Six rules

  1. One clock, starting today. Every number on the main screen is for the playhead's date, and the playhead starts at today. The date is shown once, with the headline.
  2. The person's effect is shown three ways. As a number beside the headline, as the gap between two lines on the chart, and as marked projects on the map.
  3. Time is revealed, not pre-drawn. Values are shown up to the playhead. Beyond it they are dimmed where the person has already been under the current rules, and absent everywhere else.
  4. Controls say what they do. Every empty state names the next step. Nothing is disabled without a visible reason.
  5. Two places for background. Details holds the numbers behind an effect. About this model holds data, assumptions, credits and help. Nothing else links out.
  6. Status is silent when normal. One visible message per abnormal condition, with the action.

The Simulate screen

Built differently, 2026-11-20 The key and the Layers toggle sit at the map's bottom right, above the attribution, not at the bottom left as drawn: the bottom left holds zoom, Reset view and Paint's strip, and the right corner is free at every measured width.

Header

  • The headline reads, for example, "47,335 homes by Apr 2040 · +1,817 from your policy · 68% of the goal". All three follow the playhead. At today the first two read zero.
  • The goal's part of the headline names the goal on hover and focus ("State target: 82,069 homes by 2031"). When the goal is reached it says so and when: "Goal reached, 2043".
  • With no policy in force, the middle part is absent.
  • The header holds: the two steps, alerts, the headline, Publish, the account. View, undo, redo and Keyboard shortcuts leave it.
  • Pressing the headline opens Details.

The one chart

  • It replaces both of today's charts and is taller than today's timeline chart.
  • Two lines: with the person's policy, and under current law. The gap is shaded. With no policy there is one line.
  • It starts at today, at zero. The state target is drawn as the homes still needed by its deadline.
  • The lines follow the reveal rule in Revealing time. The state target, its deadline and the policy bars are always drawn in full: they are inputs, not outcomes.
  • It has value labels on its vertical axis and shares the ruler's years.
  • Details can switch what it plots: by source (pipeline, new projects) or the policy's effect alone. The second chart's content survives as views of this one.

The activity panel

At rest the detail column no longer repeats the headline. It shows what has happened up to the playhead:

  • The most recent stage changes first: what was approved, started and completed, where, and how many homes. Those under the person's policy are marked.
  • Totals so far: homes approved, completed and stopped; affordable homes; homes from the person's policy.
  • Cost, only while a Public money policy is in force.
  • A Details button.

It follows the reveal rule: nothing after the playhead appears in it. At today it is empty, so it says the goal and offers a starter policy instead.

Note The list of individual stage changes needs the same project data as the halos. Until the citywide feed exists it can list only projects in the part of the map that is loaded; the totals are available now.

Public money on the timeline

  • Each pool's bar in the Public money rows shows how much is left. It starts full and drains as its money is allocated to projects.
  • The drain is drawn up to the playhead, under the reveal rule, like the chart.
  • A pool that runs dry shows as empty from that month on.
  • Cost per home and money spent stay where they are proposed: in the activity panel while a funding policy is in force, and in Details.
Note The funding rework comes next and will give pools amounts and end dates. The drain is designed for that; with today's pools, an uncapped pool has no level to show.

The loop

Not all of this is built now This work builds the goal, the progress figure, and the reached and missed messages, against today's state target. Levels, locked rows, unlocks, "Next goal" and tuned goals come with the game. They are set out here so that nothing built now has to be undone.

Events

A stage change is the unit of "watching it grow". Three changes get a signal: approved, construction started, completed.

  • On the visible map: a halo at the project that fades over about two seconds of real time. Its colour is the stage's colour from the key. A project that exists because of the person's policy gets the accent colour.
    Built differently, 2026-11-20 The halos have three tones of their own (approved, started building, completed), and the key names each one beside its ring. The stage swatch colours are too pale to reach 3:1 against the map's land.
  • Off the visible map: a short glow on the map edge in the project's direction. The camera does not move.
  • At speed: above a set number of events per second, halos merge into one per neighbourhood, sized by homes.
  • In the tally: the map key's stage counts and the activity panel's yearly counts tick as events land.
  • Reduced motion: with the system setting on, no halos; the counts still update.

Revealing time

The timeline has three zones. They apply to the chart's lines, to the ruler's track and to every number.

The three zones of the timeline
ZoneWhereHow it looks
ShownToday to the playheadSolid lines, full-strength numbers.
SeenThe playhead to the furthest date reached under the current rulesThe same lines, dimmed. The person has been here and nothing has changed since.
UnseenBeyond the furthest date reachedNo lines and no numbers. The ruler's track is hatched.
  • On opening, the playhead is at today and everything ahead is unseen. The window still ends 40 years out.
  • Play, or dragging the playhead forward, moves the furthest date reached. Dragging back leaves it where it was, so the part ahead is dimmed.
  • An edit pulls the furthest date reached back to the first month whose values the edit changes, and never to before the playhead. Months the edit does not touch stay seen.
  • After an edit, the Play button names the next step: "Play from 2028" when the first changed month is in 2028.
  • With the playhead at the end, an edit updates the final numbers in place, because everything up to the playhead is shown.
  • The furthest date reached lasts for the visit. It is not saved, and a shared link opens with everything ahead unseen.
Note "Not yet computed" stops being something the person is told. The forecast fills in about three seconds; if the playhead catches the computed edge it waits, and "Updating" shows with the save state.

Progress bars

  • The bars above projects show from the default zoom, not only at zoom 15 and closer.
  • A bar keeps one size on screen at every zoom, so it shrinks relative to its building as the person zooms in.
  • Zoomed out, bars would collide. Where they do, the larger project's bar wins, and a project caused by the person's policy wins over one that is not.
  • Length is progress through the stages and colour is the chance of finishing, as today. The map key explains both.

Paint

  • The panel holds Tool, Height and Brush size, each labelled, and one starting sentence until the first stroke.
  • The painted-areas list leaves the panel. Its two real uses are covered differently: Erase removes an earlier height where it is not wanted, and hovering a height in the map key outlines where that height applies.
  • A shared plan's read-only view shows one line in place of the list: the heights used and the lots covered.
  • Each stroke reports what it did in a chip. A refused stroke says so.
  • Undo and redo are keyboard-only on desktop and sit in the painting strip on touch.
  • Results stop being a hidden face of the panel. The headline is in the header; the rest is Details.

About this model

One entry, reached from the header menu, the map's (i), and every "how is this worked out" link. It has four parts:

  1. What the numbers mean. Capacity versus forecast, the state target, the June 2022 start, the comparison baseline.
  2. Assumptions. Today's Sources dialog, with the published estimates beside the assumptions they test.
  3. Data and licences. Today's data page, the dataset version, and all credits.
  4. Using it. Keyboard shortcuts.

On the map itself only OpenStreetMap needs a credit. Its guidelines allow the credit to collapse if it stays findable, and name an (i) button as the way. The proposal is MapLibre's collapsed control holding a linked "© OpenStreetMap" and the About link, shown expanded for five seconds on load.

Warning The guidelines do not say outright that an (i) with no visible text is enough. Showing the text for five seconds first is explicitly allowed. The Microsoft, USGS and DataSF terms were not read from their own pages and need confirming when the page is written.

Decided

Decision: one clock The main screen shows one date. No end-of-window figure stays visible. The window still ends 40 years out.
Decision: today is the start The simulation starts, as the person experiences it, at today, even though some figures count from 2022.
Decision: the reveal rule Values show up to the playhead, dimmed where the person has been and nothing has changed, and dashed or absent where they have not been under the current rules. This document picks absent.
Decision: plain words for the baseline "FZP" becomes "current law" on the main screen.
Decision: progress bars Visible from further out, one size on screen.
Decision: Paint is the first stage Paint is the easy first step of one path that goes on to policy and then funding. It is not a separate product.
Decision: steps only for now This work presents Paint and Simulate as steps one and two. Underneath they stay separate: a painted plan does not become the zoning that Simulate runs. Joining them is later work.
Decision: the loop is the frame, and unlocking is not built now The design fits a game of reaching the goal, getting an unlock or two, and going again. This work does not build levels or unlocks.

Decision: going again keeps the policies When the game is built, the next run returns the clock to today and leaves the person's policies in place.

Questions for the owner

Nothing in this work is waiting on an answer. These belong to the game and can wait for it:

  • The goal at each level. One amount with a nearer deadline each level, or a larger amount each level. Either needs tuning against real runs.
  • Where progress is kept. In the browser, with the account when signed in. A saved or shared policy that already uses a kind keeps it usable.

Defects found on the way

  • Keyboard shortcuts breaks the header. Opening it makes the header's single grid row about 330 px tall. Everything centres in that row and drops under the map, leaving only undo and redo visible, with the control that closes it hidden. Read from the code, not reproduced; about 75% confident of the mechanism.
  • Undo and redo look dead on load. They are disabled until the first edit of the session because history is cleared when the document loads.
  • The stale-results sentence never shows on Simulate. The flag is not passed to Results.
  • The painted-area Retry never appears. The failed state is never produced, so a row can stay at "Finding lots…".
  • "A forecast if nothing changes." stays on the headline after the person has changed something.
  • Lot counts in painted-area rows double-count overlaps.
  • The engine always reports that a policy is in force. The worker posts only the plan side's comparison mode, which is "plan" for every document. Before slice 2, a fresh Simulate page therefore said "What your policy added" and "vs FZP +N" over numbers that were current law against the old zoning. Slice 2 made the page decide the wording from the document itself; the engine's report is still wrong at the source.

Ways to build it

  1. Content first, in slices, on shared parts (recommended). Build the small set of shared values and components (spacing, one button, card, chip, key, the About surface), then change one tier at a time: the one clock and headline, the one chart with reveal, events, empty states, Paint. Each slice ships on its own and can be judged on the live app.
  2. Layout only. The first mockup. Fast, and it leaves every finding above in place.
  3. Rebuild both pages at once. One large change against a very large test surface that pins today's layout. High risk of a long-lived branch.
