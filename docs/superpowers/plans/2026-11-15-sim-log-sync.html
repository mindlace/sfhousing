Sim log store, plan 2: registration, the socket client and tabs

Unit 4 of the sim message log, second of four plans: the server's hello carries its dataset, resumes and account deletions are bounded, and a browser library (src/lib/sim-sync/) registers a sim, keeps one socket per sim, sends the store's pending commits one at a time, claims the work of closed tabs and states the save state as data. No page changes: /sim keeps its old store until plan 3.

For agentic workers REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: use superpowers:subagent-driven-development to implement this plan task by task. Steps are checklist items; progress is kept in the workspace ledger, not in the plan.

Overview

Goal
Everything plan 3 needs to put /sim on the log, short of the page: server changes that must merge before browser registration ships (the dataset in hello, a bounded resume, a bounded account deletion), five small additions to plan 1's store, and a SimSync per tab that ties registration, the socket, tabs and the save state together, tested in Node against fakes and, for Web Locks and BroadcastChannel, in real Chromium.

Review Focus

  1. An append answered by silence, and an owed resume. The object sends no frame for a duplicate id (decide.ts:46, sim-object.ts:173-178). The client's one in-flight append must still resolve, from the stored copy on the stream. That holds only because nothing is sent before a resume answer ends with more: false on that connection and no resume is outstanding or owed. Try to make the client send early: after a gap, after a store open (the owed resume), after a reconnect, after a refusal with no id. Pinned by Task 10's A10, A17 and A18, and Task 9's C7 and C10.
  2. A frame or a send for the wrong sim, or after stop. The store's sim changes under a live socket: open of another sim, adopting the current sim, a withdrawn import. Late events of a stopped socket, a registration answer or a retry timer after the sim changed or SimSync stopped, a registration waiting for the browser-wide lock for a sim its tab left, a head() of the new sim on the old socket. Pinned by C17 (Task 8), C18 (Task 9), A13 (Task 10), and Y9, Y10, Y20 and Y22 (Task 13).
  3. The resume's loops and budgets. A socket is marked resumed only when an answer reaches the tip; a page the store cannot apply must not be asked for again (it would spend the budget for ever); a commit the store cannot fold must halt, not crash or loop; readers must not be able to spend the writers' budget. Pinned by Task 2's host tests (the readers' and writers' budgets included) and Task 9's C7, C9, C22, C29, C30.
  4. A connection that died without a close. A frozen tab, sleep or a NAT timeout leaves a socket that never closes; the save state would say "saving" for ever. A ping unanswered for 10 s must close and reconnect. Pinned by Task 8's C24 and C25.
  5. Registration and the save state. No socket may feed another person's commits into a sim this device minted (taken with a tip of 0 stops it); an ok brings write to a socket opened signed out without reconnecting a brand-new one; the save state never says saved while anything is pending or held, never "on this device" while storage has failed; the account-deletion loop ends. Pinned by Y3, Y6, Y8 and Y24 (Task 13), Task 12's property and rows, and Task 3's pass tests and the pane's cap.

Invariants

Each holds after every public call of SimSocket, SimSync and TabPresence returns. The store's I1 to I12 (docs/dev/sim-store.md, Invariants) still hold; this plan adds no way around them. Tests name the ones they pin.

  1. S1, one in flight, after the resume. A socket has at most one append unanswered, and sends none on a connection until a resume answer on it ended with more: false and no resume is outstanding or owed (due but not yet sendable, or waiting out a limit backoff).
  2. S2, only what the store hands out, for its own sim. Every append is store.head() read at the moment of sending, and is sent only when store.ready and store.simId equals the socket's sim.
  3. S3, frames go to the sim they came from. Every receive names the socket's sim id; a socket whose sim is no longer the store's is stopped; events of a stopped or replaced socket are ignored.
  4. S4, the client never confirms or discards on its own. A commit leaves pending only through store.receive (the store's I4) or store.dropPending on an invalid refusal naming it. not-writer, limit, full, newer, gone, a close, taken, sign-in and quota leave every commit pending and stored.
  5. S5, registration only for the device's own work. POST /api/sims is sent only when the store says 'needed' (the sim is stored here, or storage is unavailable or failed for a sim this store minted) and the sim has something to send (pending, or stopped with held commits); never for a look; one request at a time per browser.
  6. S6, a socket only for a sim this browser may read. A socket is open only when the store says 'done' (registered), 'none' (a sim with no record on this device: a look), or 'needed' with a confirmed tip above 0 (the sim is on the server: read it until registered). After taken nothing is sent for that sim; its socket stays open only when the confirmed tip is above 0, and with a tip of 0 it is stopped, so another person's commits never reach a sim this device minted.
  7. S7, dev sends nothing. With live: false no fetch is made and no socket is opened.
  8. S8, claims only from gone tabs. Pending records are claimed only from owners whose tab lock is free, never from this tab; without Web Locks, from every other owner, at open only.
  9. S9, the save state is honest. saved exactly when pendingCount and heldCount are both 0 (a stop with nothing pending or held, as on a look, is saved); never device while local is 'failed'.
  10. S10, every wait is the clock's, and nothing loops. Every timer goes through the injected Clock; reconnect delays follow backoffDelay; a connection with no hello 10 s after opening, or no frame 10 s after a ping, is dropped; nothing reconnects after 4410, an append refused gone, a resume refused gone, another protocol or a commit the store cannot fold; no resume is asked again for a page the store could not apply.

Decisions

  1. Send only after the resume completes (S1). A duplicate append gets no frame (decide.ts:46; sim-object.ts:173-178 answers only a refusal). On a connection whose resume reached the tip, every stored commit has reached the socket before any append it sends is processed (the answer and the live broadcast are in one serialized order, log-host.ts:239-246), so an in-flight id that is a duplicate always arrives on the stream and resolves the append. Confirmed with the prototype (What was run, case 6).
  2. A resume answer ends with more. The last commits frame of every resume answer carries "more": false (caught up) or true (capped: send the next resume); live frames and the other frames of an answer carry none. PROTOCOL stays 1: the field is additive and the e2e helper reads only commits. Reason: today an answer has no end marker (sim-object.md, Resume), so a client cannot tell when it may send (decision 1).
  3. The resume bound. An answer holds at most LIMITS.resumeBytes (8 MiB) of stored bodies (always at least one commit), and each 500-row page is paid as it is read from one of two byte budgets per object, chosen by the socket's right to write when it resumes: writers 24 MiB burst refilled at 4 MiB a second; readers (signed-out visitors and members without write) 12 MiB refilled at 2 MiB a second. A spent budget refuses the first page {"type":"refused","id":null,"why":"limit"} and cuts a later one with more: true. Two budgets because reading needs only a link: with one, anyone could keep it empty and, since a writer sends nothing until its resume reaches the tip (S1), stop every writer of a sim from saving (F1); a reader already cannot spend the append bucket. Each burst is above one full answer plus one commit, so either can always pay for a page; the writers get twice the readers' share because their saving waits on it. What this bounds, plainly: the rate at which resume answers are queued on the object (at most 6 MiB a second over both, 36 MiB at once), not how much is queued (F2). A client that keeps asking without reading its socket grows the send queue at that rate until the runtime drops the connection; today one resume queues a whole sim (up to 64 MiB) in one turn. This client asks for the next page only after the last frame of the previous one, so it never has more than one answer (about 9 MiB) queued. Reason for capping at all: workerd queues send() without limit or bufferedAmount (checked, What was run), and the object has 128 MB with a sim up to 64 MiB.
  4. A socket is marked resumed when an answer reaches the tip (more: false), not at its first page as today (sim-object.ts:153-157). Within the serialized section no commit can be stored between the last page read and the mark, so I7 of the object still holds; a capped answer gets no live frames until it catches up. A socket already resumed stays resumed.
  5. A resume refused gone halts as diverged. The outline said "restarts from 0"; the store cannot: receive skips seqs at or below its tip (local-log.ts, The log), so a resume from 0 would apply nothing. A resume past the tip means the device's log is not this sim's (a takedown closes sockets 4410 first): the socket stops, the commits stay, the save state says why.
  6. One SimSocket class with injected ports, not a pure reducer plus a driver. A pure machine would need the store's head and tip passed in on every event and receive's answer fed back as another event; with the socket constructor, the clock and the store injected, the class is as deterministic in tests and half the surface. Departs from the outline's SocketMachine; the state table below is the contract.
  7. What the device knows decides registration. Plan 1's store keeps registered on the sim record but does not expose it, nor the confirmed tip (the resume needs it), nor which tabs own records (claims need it). Task 4 adds LogStore.tipSeq, LogStore.heldCount, LogStore.registration ('none' | 'wait' | 'needed' | 'done'), LogStore.markRegistered(simId) and SimDb.pendingOwners(simId). A device that recorded the registration never posts again, so a person whose session has expired is not told taken for their own sim (a signed-out repeat of an id in use is 409).
  8. Registration answers. ok records it (markRegistered) and reconnects only a socket that existed before the answer and could not write; a socket opened by the evaluation the record triggers is never reconnected (F11). sign-in: keep, no socket, post again on retryRegistration() or the next load. taken: nothing is sent for the sim; its socket is stopped when the confirmed tip is 0 (never feed another sim's commits into one this device minted) and kept for reading when the tip is above 0 (F15); the sharing unit turns the edits into a remix (R32, R53). quota: keep, report, post again on retryRegistration() or the next load. retry (429, 5xx, network, or no answer within 30 s, when the request is aborted on the injected clock, F10): back off as the socket does; online retries at once. bug (400, 403 without quota, 415, anything else): stop and log the status and code.
  9. Every registration is single-flight through the Web Lock sfh-register (without Web Locks, a promise chain in the tab). The client cannot tell whether it is signed in, and a serialized repeat costs nothing. When a request's turn comes its token is checked again, so a registration queued for a sim its tab has left is never posted (F10, P16); and since a request is aborted after 30 s, a hung one never holds the lock for every tab.
  10. Tabs wait on each other's locks. Each tab holds sfh-tab:<tabId> for its life. At open, owners of the sim's pending records whose lock is free (locks.query()) are claimed through store.open(simId, { claim }); for each owner still alive, and each tab heard on the BroadcastChannel('sfh-sim') ({ type: 'here', tabId, reply }), the tab requests that tab's lock, which is granted only when it is gone, then claims its records with store.claim([owner]). Departs from the outline's pagehide message plus query(): the lock is still held when pagehide fires, so the query would race it; waiting on the lock needs no polling. Tab ids are never reused, so a free lock means gone for good.
  11. Without Web Locks (all current browsers have them: Chrome 69, Safari 15.4, Firefox 96) a tab claims every other owner's records at open and nothing later (the outline's rule): a live tab whose records are taken learns it from its next write (lost) and the claimer sends them, at the cost of rate tokens, never of a commit.
  12. Dev is an option. SimSyncOptions.live: plan 3 passes !dev from $app/environment. With live: false nothing is fetched or opened, tabs and claims still run (they are local), and the save state is device with why dev. Reason: the library must not import $app/*, and import.meta.env.DEV is true under Vitest, which would switch off every test.
  13. The backoff resets on progress, not on hello. A confirmed append, or reaching live with nothing to send, resets it. A sim whose stored rows no longer fold closes every append 1011 (sim-object.md, Close codes): resetting on hello would loop every second.
  14. Network signals and liveness. offline closes the socket and waits; online connects at once (and retries a registration at once); online while already connecting or connected does nothing. visible while waiting connects at once; visible while connected pings at once; visible while the socket cannot write (a not-writer hold, or access said write: false) and something is pending reconnects (SimSync does it), so a sign-in made in another tab gives this socket write. A text ping every 30 s while visible; after each ping a pong or any frame must arrive within 10 s, or the connection is closed and reconnected as a dropped one, so a socket that died without a close (a frozen tab, sleep, a NAT timeout) never leaves an append in flight and "saving" shown for ever (F7). A connection with no hello in 10 s is a failed attempt. The object's hibernation is invisible to the client: the socket stays open and its attachment keeps resumed.
  15. Holds and halts. full and newer hold sending for the life of the SimSocket (across reconnects); not-writer and limit are cleared by a new connection. Halts (gone, diverged, protocol, broken) close the socket for good.
  16. hello.build different from the page's build is reported (buildMismatch) and changes nothing else (outline). No tip-hash check after a resume (plan 1, decision 26): nothing to compare against without another protocol field, and a mismatch would be a server bug the chain already guards (sim-object.md, I2).
  17. SimSync.open does not retry a store open that rejects (p2-store-contract): a failed IndexedDB read is a closed connection or a broken database, which a retry does not mend. It rejects, and plan 3 offers a reload. open of the sim that is already open and ready does nothing: without a database the store's open starts a known sim from an empty log and would drop its unsent edits (F14). openSimDb's null for a version error stays indistinguishable from no storage (p2-db-open): what the page says is plan 3's.
  18. The save state is four kinds and a reason: saved, saving, device (saved on this device only), unsaved (not saved anywhere yet), with why for the details on tap. saved means nothing is pending and nothing is held (heldCount, Task 4): a stopped store with nothing of its own on the device, such as a look at a sim on another dataset, is saved, not "on this device" (F17). Ordering in the save state table. An open draft is not pending and does not change it.
  19. Account deletion is bounded per request. deleteAccount leaves at most 50 sims per call and answers { more }; the route answers 202 { more: true } without signing out until the last pass, which closes the account as today; the pane repeats while it gets 202, at most 100 passes (5,000 sims) before it shows the network error and the person retries. Each pass is about ten D1 queries per sim, well under the per-request limit.
  20. No client end-to-end test in this plan. No page drives SimSync until plan 3 (the outline puts the real socket's proof there, with the page's specs). Playwright here covers the server changes (Tasks 1 and 2). Web Locks and BroadcastChannel are proved in real Chromium (Task 11).
  21. fitsCommit is not re-checked before sending. The store makes a commit pending only after fitsCommit (its I7), so every head() fits; re-checking would add a second refusal path with no case behind it.
  22. A page the store cannot apply is not asked for again. After a frame with more: true, the next resume is sent only when the store's tip moved past the after just answered. A newer build's commit leaves the tip where it was (the store applies nothing from it on and stops 'future'), so resuming from the tip would fetch the same page for ever and spend the budget: the socket stops resuming on that connection, sends nothing, and the save state says why (F5).
  23. A commit the store cannot fold halts the socket. The store's receive rethrows when a stored commit will not fold for any reason but a newer revision (a server bug; local-log.ts keeps the prefix and rethrows). The socket catches it, logs [sim-sync] receive failed with the error's name only, closes with 1000 and halts broken: no crash, no reconnect loop (F6).
  24. A refusal with no id is the resume's. The object names an append's id whenever it can parse one (sim-object.md, Frames); a refusal with id: null answers a resume (gone, the budget's limit) or a frame this client never sends (one over the frame limit). So gone with no id halts diverged, any other reason with no id resumes again after the backoff, and only a refusal naming the in-flight commit is an append's (F8).
  25. SimSync.stop() is final. It sets a stopped flag and advances the token; every handler (registration answers, timers, store and network events, a tab going) returns at once afterwards, so nothing opens or posts after teardown (F12).
  26. A sim that needs registration and is already on the server is read meanwhile. When the store says 'needed' and its confirmed tip is above 0 (a sim another tab of this device registered, or one this device stored before its registration was recorded), a socket opens as for a look; with a tip of 0 and nothing to send there is nothing to read and no socket, so a sim another tab minted and has not yet registered shows nothing from the server until this tab reopens it (F13).

For the owner

What a person will see or can lose once plan 3 puts this on /sim, most important first.

  1. An edit can be stuck on one device as "taken". If a first, signed-out registration reaches the server but its answer is lost (the network drops at that moment), the server made the sim and an account whose session cookie never reached the browser. The retry is signed out and the id is in use, so the server says taken. The person's edits stay on that device, unsent, and the page says so, until the sharing unit can turn them into a remix (R32). Rare, and nothing is deleted. See question 1.
  2. A pseudonymous person whose session has expired keeps editing on the device only. The device knows the sim is registered, so it opens the socket, which is read-only signed out: edits stay on the device and the save state says "saved on this device" (no write). A pseudonymous account has no way back in. Nothing is lost; until unit 5 it is not sent either. See question 2.
  3. Write taken away (removed from a sim, or the last manager left): edits stay on the device, unsent, "saved on this device"; they are sent if write comes back. The remix of R53 waits for unit 5.
  4. Under npm run dev nothing reaches the server; the page says "saved on this device". Use npm run preview to see saving.
  5. A very large sim opens in 8 MiB pages, and when many people open large sims on one object at once the later ones wait for a budget: 4 MiB a second for writers, 2 MiB a second for readers, kept apart so readers can never hold up a writer's saving. A 64 MiB sim takes at least 8 round trips.
  6. Edits a tab leaves behind when it moves to another sim wait on this device until that tab reopens the sim or closes (then another tab, or the next load, claims and sends them). Plan 3 decides whether to warn when leaving a sim with unsent edits, or to keep sending for it.
  7. Old browsers without Web Locks: a second tab takes over the first tab's unsent edits at open; the first tab stops showing them until they come back confirmed. Nothing is lost.
  8. Deleting an account in many sims takes several requests; the button stays busy throughout.
  9. A tab on an older build keeps saving; plan 3 can offer a reload from buildMismatch.

Open questions

  1. Question A lost first answer turns the person's own sim into taken (For the owner, 1). Accept until unit 5's remix re-homes the edits, or make registration recoverable now (for example the browser sends a one-time proof it minted, kept with the sim, and the server answers 200 to the same proof)? Recommendation: accept for now. It needs a lost response at the one moment that matters, loses nothing, and unit 5's remix is the general cure; a proof is a new credential to store and check. A session-first route would also close the case; it is a new way to mint an account, so it is the owner's choice and is not built here. Today no route gives a browser a pseudonymous session before it registers: startSim mints in the same request that creates the sim, after the in-use check (src/lib/server/sims/start-sim.ts:94-110), and the cookie arrives only on that response. It would take a JSON route behind the same origin check (refuseUnlessSameOriginJson) and the same mint rate limit (PSEUDO_MINT_RL), answering 200 when already signed in, 401 sign_in on a known device, and otherwise minting the session; the client would call it first, inside the registration gate, so the session exists before any sim is registered and a lost registration answer is repeated signed in (200).
  2. Question A pseudonymous session that expires leaves the device unable to write its own sims (For the owner, 2). Accept, or lengthen pseudonymous sessions, or have the page offer "keep as a new sim" (a remix) at once? Recommendation: accept in plan 2 and 3; have unit 5's remix offer it. Session lifetime is an auth decision outside this unit.

  • LogStore exposes no confirmed tip, no registered flag, and no owners of other tabs' records (log-store.svelte.ts:221-270); plan 2's contract needs all three. Task 4.
  • The object marks a socket resumed at the first page of an answer (sim-object.ts:153-157); with a capped answer that would send live commits mid catch-up. Decision 4.
  • sim-object.md, Resume: "There is no end marker". Decision 2 adds one.
  • DeleteAccountPane treats any res.ok as done (src/lib/account/DeleteAccountPane.svelte:45): a 202 would end the flow after one pass. Task 3 loops.

  if (i.pendingCount === 0 && i.heldCount === 0) return { kind: 'saved', why: null };

  if (s.halt !== null) return s.halt;
